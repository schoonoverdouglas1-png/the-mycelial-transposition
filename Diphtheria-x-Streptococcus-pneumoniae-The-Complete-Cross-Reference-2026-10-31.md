## Part I — *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*: the entire inventory

### Taxonomy [D]

- **Domain:** Bacteria · **Phylum:** Actinomycetota (Actinobacteria) · **Class:** Actinomycetes · **Order:** Mycobacteriales · **Family:** Corynebacteriaceae · **Genus:** *Corynebacterium* · **Species:** *C. diphtheriae*.
- It is the type species of the genus *Corynebacterium* [D — ScienceDirect overview of *C. diphtheriae*].
- It belongs to the suborder Corynebacterineae, the "mycolata" — bacteria sharing an unusual lipid-rich cell envelope with the mycobacteria, nocardiae, and rhodococci.
- **The diphtheriae species complex [D]:** *C. diphtheriae* sits beside close relatives that also carry or lack the toxin gene — *C. ulcerans* (toxigenic, causes classical respiratory diphtheria, animal source documented), *C. pseudotuberculosis*, *C. rouxii*, *C. silvaticum*, *C. ramonii*, and *C. belfantii* (the former biovar belfanti, now proposed as its own species). The complex matters because diphtheria disease is defined by the toxin, not by the species name alone — a toxigenic cousin is diphtheria in all but name.

### Morphology [D]

- **Gram-positive, pleomorphic rods, often club-shaped** — one end swollen like a club [D].
- **Chinese-letter (cuneiform) arrangement:** the cells divide in a way that leaves them lying at angles to each other like Chinese characters, or in palisades.
- **Babes-Ernst metachromatic granules:** dark-staining granules of stored phosphate inside the rods, visible on special stains — a classic microscopic clue [D].
- **Non-motile, non-spore-forming** [D].
- **Facultatively anaerobic, catalase-positive, oxidase-negative, non-acid-fast, non-capsulate**. That last word carries the whole of Part V: *C. diphtheriae* has **no capsule**.

### The four biovars [D]

Biovars are biochemical variants — same species, different sugar fermentations, colony shapes, and habits. The distinctions below are the classical ones; modern genomics has rearranged some of the furniture (belfanti most of all), and the table says so.

| mitis | Smooth, black, hemolytic colonies | Does not ferment starch | Predominant in southern Central Asia during the same epidemic [D]; the name means "mild," which describes the biovar, not the disease it can cause | PENDING |

- **Potentially toxigenic species screen [D]:** cystinase-positive and pyrazinamidase-negative marks the potentially toxigenic members of the complex — a biochemical flag for "this one could carry the toxin," not proof that it does.

### Toxigenic vs non-toxigenic [D]

- **The split is the disease.** A strain is toxigenic only if it carries the *tox* gene and expresses diphtheria toxin. A strain without it can colonize and even cause local disease, but it cannot cause classical diphtheria — the membrane-plus-toxin syndrome.
- **Non-toxigenic strains are not harmless by definition [D]:** they cause cutaneous disease, invasive disease, and endocarditis in their own right, and they are the reason diphtheria surveillance cultures every suspect isolate and tests it for toxin, rather than trusting the species name.
- **Toxigenicity can be gained and lost [D]:** because the toxin gene rides a phage, a strain can gain the phage (lysogenic conversion) or lose it. Genomic studies show short-timescale gain and loss of the toxin and of biovar-associated genes within sublineages.

### The *tox* gene and corynephage β [D]

- **The weapon is viral in origin.** The *tox* gene (1,683 bp) is carried by the lysogenic bacteriophage corynephage β, which integrates into the bacterial chromosome at attachment sites (attB1/attB2). Without the phage there is no toxin; without the toxin there is no diphtheria.
- **Regulation by iron — the DtxR regulon [D]:** the diphtheria toxin repressor DtxR binds iron and, when iron is plentiful, shuts *tox* transcription down. High iron suppresses toxin; low iron derepresses it. The same regulator controls roughly 15% of the genome, including iron-acquisition genes — toxin production and iron hunger are wired together.
- **The phage-toxin kin [D]:** diphtheria toxin sits in the classic lysogenic-conversion table beside cholera toxin (CTXφ phage), Shiga toxins (lambdoid phages), scarlet-fever erythrogenic toxins (T12 phage), and botulinum neurotoxins (clostridial phages). Five famous diseases, five phage-carried toxins, one mechanism.

### Diphtheria toxin: structure and function [D]

- **An A-B toxin, 535 amino acids, 58–62 kDa, single chain cut into two fragments:**
- **Fragment B (binding/translocation):** binds the host receptor HB-EGF (heparin-binding EGF-like growth factor) on human cells and drives the A fragment across the endosomal membrane.
- **Fragment A (catalytic):** an enzyme, not a poison in the simple sense — one A fragment can kill a cell.
- **The mechanism [D]:** Fragment A ADP-ribosylates elongation factor 2 (EF-2) at a modified histidine residue called diphthamide. EF-2 is the machine that moves the ribosome along messenger RNA during protein synthesis. ADP-ribosylated EF-2 stops working. Protein synthesis stops. The cell dies.
- **Why the heart and nerves [D]:** toxin absorbed into the bloodstream distributes to tissues with high HB-EGF expression and high protein-synthesis demand — myocardium and peripheral nerve above all. That distribution is the whole systemic syndrome: the membrane is local, the toxin is everywhere.
- **Why it is so lethal per molecule [D]:** because Fragment A is catalytic, it is not consumed by its reaction — one molecule inactivates EF-2 molecules again and again. The dose that kills is measured in molecules, not milligrams.

### Other virulence factors [D]

- **Spa pili:** hair-like surface appendages (SpaA/SpaD/SpaH-type pili) that mediate adhesion to human cells — the bacterium's grip on the throat wall. Pili and siderophores were identified as additional virulence factors beyond the toxin, and the pathogenesis is now understood as more complex than toxin alone.
- **Siderophores:** iron-scavenging molecules that steal iron from the host — the other half of the DtxR iron circuit. A bacterium that cannot get iron cannot grow, and cannot make toxin either.
- **Corynomycolic acids (see Cell wall below):** the waxy envelope contributes to survival and to resistance against host defenses.

### Cell wall [D]

- **Peptidoglycan plus arabinogalactan plus corynomycolic acids.** The corynebacterial envelope is a mycolyl-arabinogalactan-peptidoglycan complex: peptidoglycan at the base, a galactan-arabinan polymer above it, and short mycolic acids esterified to the arabinan.
- **Corynomycolic acids are short mycolic acids [D]:** 22–38 carbon atoms (one careful analysis gives 30–36), versus 70–90 carbons in mycobacterial mycolic acids. They are α-alkyl, β-hydroxy fatty acids, made by Claisen-like condensation of two fatty acyl chains. Shorter chains mean a thinner, more permeable waxy layer than tuberculosis builds — one reason diphtheria is a throat disease and tuberculosis is a lung fortress.
- **The mycolate layer is a permeability barrier [D]:** it restricts hydrophilic substances and contributes to antibiotic tolerance; a mycolate-free strain shows higher susceptibility, especially to β-lactams.
- **No capsule.** Stated again because it decides Part V: nothing on this envelope is a serotypeable capsular polysaccharide.

### Culture and diagnosis [D]

- **Loeffler medium:** a serum-enriched slant on which corynebacteria grow fast and show their granules well — the classic first step.
- **Tellurite medium (Tinsdale, Hoyle's):** potassium tellurite inhibits most normal flora and turns *C. diphtheriae* colonies black or gray-black (tellurite reduced to metallic tellurium). Black colonies on tellurite from a throat swab are the laboratory's red flag.
- **Elek test:** the immunodiffusion test for toxigenicity — toxin diffusing from the colony meets antitoxin diffusing from a strip and forms a visible precipitin line. The Elek line is the laboratory's yes-or-no on "this strain makes toxin." Modern laboratories confirm with PCR for the *tox* gene, but Elek remains the classical phenotypic proof.
- **Culture first, toxin test always:** species identification without a toxin test is an incomplete diagnosis, because non-toxigenic strains exist and toxigenic cousins exist.

- **Timing [D]:** myocarditis typically appears in the first or second week; polyneuropathy weeks later — the toxin's damage declares itself on a delay, which is why diphtheria follow-up outlasts the membrane.

### Vaccines [D]

- **All diphtheria vaccines are toxoid vaccines.** The toxin is detoxified with formaldehyde and the immune system is taught to neutralize the toxin, not to kill the bacterium. There is no capsule to teach against, because there is no capsule.
- **DTaP:** diphtheria toxoid + tetanus toxoid + acellular pertussis — childhood primary series and boosters.
- **Tdap:** tetanus + reduced-dose diphtheria toxoid + acellular pertussis — the adolescent/adult booster.
- **Td:** tetanus + reduced-dose diphtheria toxoid — the adult tetanus-diphtheria booster.
- **What toxoid vaccination does and does not do [D]:** it prevents toxin-mediated disease — the membrane's systemic consequences — but it does not necessarily prevent colonization by the bacterium. That is the toxoid bargain, and it is the mirror image of the pneumococcal conjugate bargain (Part II), which prevents the bacterium's invasive disease but leaves the door open to serotype replacement.

### Antitoxin [D]

- **Equine diphtheria antitoxin:** serum from horses immunized with diphtheria toxoid, containing antibodies that bind and neutralize free toxin. It is the only specific antidote, and it only neutralizes toxin not yet bound to cells — which is why it is given as early as possible, on clinical suspicion, without waiting for culture.
- **Antitoxin exists for diphtheria and not for pneumococcus.** That asymmetry is a Part IV split, and it follows directly from the toxin logic: diphtheria has one toxin, made by one gene, carried by one phage. Pneumococcus has no single toxin to neutralize.

### Antibody correlate of protection [D]

- **The standard figure is 0.1 IU/mL.** Antitoxin levels below 0.01 IU/mL mark susceptibility; 0.01–0.1 IU/mL is partial protection; **≥0.1 IU/mL is the accepted protective level** [D — standard serologic correlate, verified for this file].
- **Why this number matters for the whole cross-reference:** diphtheria has a clean, single, universally cited correlate. Pneumococcus does not — its correlate is debated across 0.3–0.5, 1.0, and 1.3 lines depending on assay, population, and endpoint (Part III, item 6). One organism, one toxin, one number; the other organism, one hundred capsules, no single number.

### Epidemiology [D]

- **Vaccine-era decline [D]:** United States cases fell more than 99% from a peak of roughly 206,000 in 1921 after toxoid vaccination became widespread. Diphtheria went from a leading childhood killer to a disease most American clinicians have never seen.
- **The 1990s ex-USSR epidemic [D]:** 1990–1998, more than 157,000 cases and more than 5,000 deaths, peaking above 50,000 cases in 1995. Gravis predominated in Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, the Baltics, and northern Kazakhstan; mitis in southern Central Asia. Causes: waning adult immunity, low childhood coverage, health-system collapse, and population movement. Ribotyping distinguished the epidemic strains — the typing method of that era, before MLST and cgMLST.
- **Persistence where vaccination lags [D]:** diphtheria never went extinct. It persists wherever coverage falls — in undervaccinated communities, in conflict zones, and in populations with waning adult immunity. The epidemic's lesson is that toxoid immunity is a maintained wall, not a permanent one.

- Simply put for Part I: Diphtheria is a club-shaped rod with no capsule and no spores, wearing a waxy coat and arranging itself like spilled matchsticks. Its killer is a poison written by a virus that lives inside it, switched on when iron runs low. The poison stops every cell it reaches from making protein, and the heart and nerves pay first. Medicine fights it two ways: a tamed version of the poison as a vaccine, and horse-made antibodies as an antidote — and the blood level that counts as protected is a single clean number, 0.1.

## Part II — *Streptococcus pneumoniae*: the entire inventory

### Taxonomy [D]

- **Domain:** Bacteria · **Phylum:** Bacillota (Firmicutes) · **Class:** Bacilli · **Order:** Lactobacillales · **Family:** Streptococcaceae · **Genus:** *Streptococcus* · **Species:** *S. pneumoniae* (the pneumococcus).
- **The mitis group [D]:** pneumococcus sits among the oral streptococci — *S. mitis*, *S. oralis*, *S. pseudopneumoniae* — close enough that capsule genes move between them. The 100th serotype's capsule locus carries a large fragment from the oral streptococcus *S. mitis* SK145. The species boundary is porous, and the capsule is the cargo that crosses it.

### Morphology [D]

- **Lancet-shaped (flame-shaped) diplococci:** pairs of oval cocci with their pointed ends facing outward, like two lance heads base to base — Gram-positive, in pairs and short chains.
- **Alpha-hemolytic:** greenish partial hemolysis on blood agar, from hydrogen peroxide production.
- **Optochin-sensitive, bile-soluble, Quellung-positive:** the three classical laboratory identities — killed by optochin, dissolved by bile salts, and swollen visibly by type-specific antiserum under the microscope (the Quellung reaction, Neufeld, 1902).
- **Encapsulated, in most disease strains:** the capsule is the organism's defining structure and its serotype.

### Capsule and cell wall [D]

- **Capsule:** a polysaccharide shell around the cell, antiphagocytic — it hides the bacterium from neutrophils and complement. Capsule chemistry is the serotype (Part V). Unencapsulated ("rough") strains exist and are generally non-pathogenic — Griffith's R strain.
- **Cell wall:** peptidoglycan, wall teichoic acids, and lipoteichoic acids, anchored by **choline** — a distinctive pneumococcal feature. Choline-binding proteins (PspA, PspC/CbpA, LytA) dock onto the choline residues, which is why choline is not a footnote but the wall's organizing principle.
- **No mycolic acids.** Stated plainly against Part I: the pneumococcal wall is peptidoglycan and teichoic acid, not wax.

- **Griffith 1928 [D]:** Frederick Griffith mixed heat-killed encapsulated (S) pneumococci with live unencapsulated (R) pneumococci in mice; the mice died, and live S bacteria were recovered. The "transforming principle" was later shown to be DNA (Avery, MacLeod, McCarty, 1944). Transformation was discovered in this organism, and it is still the organism's engine of change.

- **The one-code-per-organism principle [H→D, his Oct 30 theory, applied here]:** J13, G00.1, and A40.3 are assigned once per organism–disease pair and inherited identically by all serotypes. ICD codes disease + organism, never serotype. A serotype 3 pneumonia and a serotype 19A pneumonia are both J13; a serotype 14 meningitis and a serotype 23F meningitis are both G00.1. The serotype lives in the laboratory and the vaccine record, not in the diagnosis code. Part V applies the same principle to diphtheria's A36 codes.
- **Invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) [D]:** the umbrella for meningitis, bacteremia, and other normally-sterile-site disease. Roughly 25–30% of pneumococcal pneumonia develops bacteremia, and case fatality with bacteremia runs near 10% [D].

### Nasopharyngeal carriage [D]

- **The reservoir is the nose and throat.** Pneumococcus colonizes the nasopharynx, especially in young children, usually without symptoms. Carriage is the rule; disease is the exception — and the exception is what the vaccines target.
- **Carriage precedes disease [D]:** invasive disease almost always follows acquisition of a carried strain. Carriage studies, not disease counts alone, are how serotype replacement is watched.
- **Carriage is serotype-specific in behavior [D]:** some serotypes are carriage specialists (6B, the longest resident; 19F; 23F, the "carriage king"), others are invaders that barely carry (1, rarely carried, epidemic; 12F, eruption-without-carriage, hyperinvasive). The §226 Carriage group holds 6 serotypes; the Outbreak group holds 11.

### Antibiotic resistance [D]

- **Penicillin resistance:** mosaic penicillin-binding protein genes (PBP1a, PBP2b, PBP2x), assembled by transformation and recombination — the same competence machinery that switches capsules builds resistance. Reduced β-lactam affinity, not β-lactamase.
- **Macrolide resistance:** ribosomal methylation (*erm* genes) and efflux (*mef* genes).
- **Resistant serotypes [D]:** the classic multidrug-resistant clones are named for their serotypes — Spain23F-1, Spain6B-2, and the 19A lineage ST320, which evolved from a 19F clone by capsular switch and became the post-PCV7 multidrug-resistant replacement. The §226 Resistant group holds 10 serotypes; 33F is erythromycin-nonsusceptible at 100% in its entry.
- **Resistance travels with the capsule [D]:** because resistance genes and capsule genes move together in clones, vaccinating against a serotype can remove a resistant clone — and replacement can bring a new one in. That is the vaccine–resistance feedback loop in one sentence.

### Vaccines [D]

- **PPSV23 (pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine, 23-valent):** pure capsular polysaccharides, no carrier protein, no conjugate — T-cell-independent immunity, no memory in young children, no effect on carriage. Serotypes: **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6B, 7F, 8, 9N, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 17F, 18C, 19A, 19F, 20, 22F, 23F, 33F**.
- **PCV13 (13-valent conjugate):** polysaccharides conjugated to CRM197 carrier protein. Serotypes: **1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 23F**.
- **PCV15 (15-valent conjugate):** PCV13 + **22F, 33F**.
- **PCV20 (20-valent conjugate):** PCV15 + **8, 10A, 11A, 12F, 15B**.
- **PCV21 (21-valent conjugate):** carrier protein CRM197, a non-toxic diphtheria protein — the cross-reference made flesh (Part III, item 14). As written in the Serology book, its list holds **22 entries and omits serotype 14** — flagged, not fixed (see Flags). Published lists name 15C where the book's per-serotype entries name 15B, and 20A where the book names 20; cross-protection between 20A and 20 is unestablished [GAP]. The book is followed as written in this file's tables.
- **What conjugate means [D]:** attaching the polysaccharide to a protein carrier (CRM197) recruits T-cell help, giving immune memory and carriage reduction in children — the two things PPSV23 cannot do. That is why conjugate vaccines, not the polysaccharide vaccine, drive serotype replacement: they clear carried vaccine serotypes from the nose, and non-vaccine serotypes move into the vacancy.

- Simply put for Part II: The pneumococcus is a pair of pointed oval beads that lives in the nose, wears a sugar coat in one of a hundred patterns, and pops holes in lung cells with a toxin it releases when it dies. It causes ear infections, sinus infections, pneumonia, meningitis, and bloodstream infection — and each of those has exactly one diagnosis code no matter which coat pattern did it. Its vaccines are the coats themselves, stitched to a harmless piece of diphtheria toxin so the immune system remembers them.

That is a small number and a large fact: the two organisms of this file have sat side by side in human disease, in the same clinic, in the same decade that modern vaccinology began.
- **Item 14, read carefully [D]:** CRM197 is a mutant diphtheria toxin with a single amino-acid change that destroys its enzymatic activity while keeping its shape. Every pneumococcal conjugate vaccine in current use is therefore, structurally, a diphtheria-protein vaccine carrying pneumococcal sugars. The two organisms are not merely comparable. At the level of the syringe, they are joined.
- **Item 6, read carefully:** diphtheria's correlate is a single number because diphtheria has a single toxin and a single antibody that neutralizes it. Pneumococcus's correlate is a debate because it has one hundred capsules, and the antibody that protects against one does not protect against another. The correlate problem is the serotype problem wearing a laboratory coat.

- Simply put for Part III: Both are Gram-positive germs that ride on coughs, hide in healthy noses, make poisons, and can be stopped by vaccines. Both can reach the heart and the bloodstream. Both fell when vaccines arrived and came back where vaccines lapsed. Both grow in a dish. And the strangest share of all: the pneumococcus vaccines are built on a piece of diphtheria itself.

## Part V — Serotyping: the deep section

### The asymmetry, first and plainly

**Pneumococcus HAS serotypes. Diphtheria does NOT.**

- Pneumococcus has serotypes because it has a capsule, and the capsule's polysaccharide chemistry differs from strain to strain. A serotype is a capsule chemistry. There are about one hundred of them.
- Diphtheria has no serotypes because it has no capsule. There is no polysaccharide shell whose chemistry could differ, be typed by antiserum, or be targeted by a vaccine. What diphtheria has instead is a biovar, a toxin status, and a genome — and those are typed by biochemistry, by the Elek test and *tox* PCR, and by MLST/cgMLST.

### What "diphtheria serotyping" would even mean

- **If the words were used literally,** "diphtheria serotyping" would mean sorting *C. diphtheriae* strains by the antibodies that recognize their surface. For pneumococcus that works, because the surface carries a thick, variable, immunogenic capsule. For diphtheria it does not work, because the surface carries a thin waxy layer whose components are broadly shared across corynebacteria and mycobacteria, not a variable antigen — and because the disease's antigen that matters is the toxin, which is essentially one antigen, not a hundred.
- **What people mean instead, when they say a diphtheria strain is "typed":** one of three things, and the file keeps them separate —
1. **Biovar** — gravis, mitis, intermedius, belfanti (biochemistry and colony form).
2. **Toxigenicity** — tox-positive or tox-negative (Elek test, *tox* PCR). This is the clinically decisive typing: it answers "can this strain cause diphtheria?"
3. **Genomic type** — MLST sequence type (7 housekeeping genes: *atpA, dnaE, dnaK, fusA, leuA, odhA, rpoB*), or cgMLST type (a 1,305-gene core-genome scheme applied to 602 isolates, defining 151 sublineages). Ribotyping — cutting the genome and reading the ribosomal RNA pattern — was the gold standard before MLST and distinguished the ex-USSR epidemic strains.
- **The naming trap [LOGIC]:** because diphtheria typing uses biovar names and pneumococcus typing uses number-letter codes, it is tempting to treat "gravis" as diphtheria's version of "19A." It is not. Gravis is a biochemical habit; 19A is a capsule chemistry. They are typed by different things, for different reasons, and no table should put them in the same column. This file does not.

### How pneumococcal serotyping works, and how the count reached ~100 [D]

- **Quellung (Neufeld, 1902):** type-specific antiserum makes the capsule swell visibly under the microscope. Almost a century of Quellung work identified 90 capsule types.
- **Monoclonals and genetics (2007 onward):** monoclonal antibodies and capsule-locus (*cps*) sequencing added nine more types starting with 6C in 2007.
- **The 100th type [D]:** serotype 10D, reported by Ganaie and colleagues (mBio, 2020), named for its serologic cross-reaction with 10A; its *cps* locus carries regions syntenic to 6C, to 39, and to the oral streptococcus *S. mitis* SK145. Cross-opsonic antibodies to 10D appear after immunization with 10A polysaccharide in the 23-valent vaccine — a documented crack in the "one serotype, one immunity" wall.
- **Newer names [D]:** a 2025 Clinical Microbiology Reviews update names capsule types discovered from 2007 to 2024 including 6C, 10D, 24C, 33E, and 36B, plus 7D from UK whole-genome sequencing. Serotypes 3 and 37 use a synthase-dependent capsule mechanism, unlike the usual Wzy-dependent pathway.
- **"6E" does not exist [D]:** the name was briefly used for a genetic variant of 6B that produces 6B capsule, and was retired for that reason. Any list containing 6E as a serotype is wrong.
- **The count, stated honestly [GAP, with the tension shown]:** Ganaie 2020 names 10D the 100th type; the 2025 review adds further names (7D, 24C, 33E, 36B) while sources still commonly say "100." The printed list below enumerates exactly 100 named types including those newer names; whether the true total under a single counting convention is 100, or 100-plus with some names still provisional, is not settled in the sources reviewed, and this file does not force it. The list is the working enumeration; the count convention is the open question.

### The full-100 context [LOGIC — arithmetic from the vaccine lists above]

- **Union of all five vaccines:** PPSV23's 23, plus 6A (PCV13-only addition), plus PCV21's additions outside the other four (15A, 15C, 16F, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, 35B) = 32 serotypes covered by at least one licensed vaccine (20A counted as distinct from 20 would make 33; see Flags).
- **Uncovered:** 100 − 32 = **68 serotypes in no licensed vaccine** (67 if 20A is counted separately). The vaccines cover roughly a third of the capsule universe. That is not a failure of the vaccines — it is the shape of the problem: the capsule universe is large, the disease burden concentrates in a minority of serotypes, and the vaccines chase the burden, not the count.
- **The 23-panel context:** the PN23M panel covers the 23 PPSV23 serotypes — the polysaccharide vaccine's list, not the conjugate lists. That is why 6A (conjugate-only) is absent from the panel and why 2 and 20 (PPSV23-only) are present.

### The one-code-per-organism principle, applied to both [H→D]

- **Pneumococcus:** J13 (pneumonia), G00.1 (meningitis), A40.3 (sepsis) are assigned once per organism–disease pair and inherited identically by all 100 serotypes. A serotype 3 pneumonia and a serotype 19A pneumonia are both J13. ICD codes disease + organism, never serotype.
- **Diphtheria:** the A36 codes work the same way. A36.0 is pharyngeal diphtheria whether the strain is gravis or mitis, tox-positive by phage or by any other route — because tox-positivity is the disease, and the biovar is the strain's habit, not the diagnosis. A36 codes organism + site/syndrome, never biovar, never sequence type.
- **The symmetry [LOGIC]:** both organisms prove the same point from opposite directions. Pneumococcus has one hundred serotypes and one code per disease; diphtheria has zero serotypes and one code per site. In both cases the code refuses to carry the typing information — and that refusal is what keeps the typing honest, because the typing lives where it belongs: in the laboratory, in the vaccine record, and in tables like this one.

- Simply put for Part V: The pneumococcus has a hundred different coats, and each coat has a number-letter name. Diphtheria has no coat at all, so it has no coat names — it is sorted instead by its habits, its poison, and its genes. The vaccines cover about a third of the coats, and the diagnosis codes cover none of them: the code says which germ and which disease, and the coat pattern lives somewhere else entirely.
