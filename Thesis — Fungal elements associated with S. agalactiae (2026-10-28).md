## Three-way test

### Way 1 — HOLDS as association [H→D]

*S. agalactiae* (Group B Streptococcus, GBS) and the fungus *Candida albicans* are heavily documented traveling together:

- They often co-colonize the female genital tract, and population studies show a significant association between GBS and *C. albicans* vaginal carriage (Yu et al., Frontiers in Microbiology, 2018; PLOS Pathogens interkingdom review, 2024).
- *C. albicans* increases GBS adherence to vaginal epithelial cells, mediated by the fungal agglutinin-like sequence (Als) adhesins — a physical binding between fungus and bacterium.
- Cohen et al.: *C. albicans* supports GBS fitness in the vaginal tract and ascension to the uterus; hyphae promote GBS aggregation and adhesion, protect GBS from antibiotics, and contact induces arginine biosynthesis in *C. albicans* that drives GBS virulence gene expression (pilus 2b).
- About half of vaginal *C. albicans* isolates in one study showed CAMP-like synergy with *S. agalactiae*.

### Way 2 — BREAKS as composition [D]

*S. agalactiae* itself contains no fungal elements:

- It is a bacterium: peptidoglycan cell wall, no chitin, no ergosterol, no fungal organelles.
- Its Lancefield group B antigen and its capsule are bacterial polysaccharides, not fungal molecules.
- "Heavy fungal elements" as a claim about what the organism is made of does not hold.

### Way 3 — The seam: association, not composition [LOGIC]

The thesis survives in its association form and fails in its composition form. The documented reality is ecological and physical: bacterium and fungus sharing a niche, physically bound, exchanging nutrients — exactly the "stuck to the chain in between" picture in his chain model, but as two organisms traveling as a unit, not one organism made of the other. Note the interaction is bidirectional and complicated: GBS also inhibits *Candida* hyphal development (via EFG1–Hwp1) while benefiting from its presence — not a simple one-way fungal-element story.

## My extra addition (labeled mine)

The sharpest testable form of this thesis is the Cohen finding: *Candida* hyphae physically anchor GBS and shield it from antibiotics. If his chain model predicts bacteria and fungi traveling as a unit, the GBS–*Candida* pair is a documented instance of exactly that unit — measurable by co-carriage rates. One caution on his word "heavy": the association is significant and common, not universal; co-carriage is frequent, not constant.
