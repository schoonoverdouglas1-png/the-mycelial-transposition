## Finding 1 — The settled ruling that explains his count [D from his record]

**Creek Fever = Valley fever — ruled Oct 18, 2026 (§171).** His term for valley fever, settled, never re-ask.

This resolves his Oct 17 count, filed as his words: *"Jamestown Canyon, Creek Fever, and Valley Fever are known to be two different fevers."* Three names spoken, two known fevers — because Creek Fever IS Valley Fever. The two known fevers are Jamestown Canyon virus disease and valley fever; Creek Fever is valley fever's third name, not a third fever.

## Finding 2 — Valley fever's own old names [D]

Valley fever's documented old names: cocci, Valley fever, California fever, desert rheumatism, San Joaquin Valley fever; plus California disease, desert fever, San Joaquin fever, coccidioidal granuloma (historical, for progressive disseminated disease), and Posadas disease. The 1943 control-program source confirms the same set: "valley fever" or "desert rheumatism" and coccidioidal granuloma "as it was then called", with the progressive form also called "secondary coccidioidomycosis," "coccidioidal granuloma," "San Joaquin Valley Fever," "California Disease," or "desert fever".

## How to read the relation columns

- **SAME** = the old name IS that disease.
- **COUSIN [H] / DISTINCT [D]** = his thesis says symptomatically the same; standard medicine holds them distinct. Both labels stay.
- **DISTINCT** = a different disease on both readings.
- **GAP** = the record does not settle the relation.
- **Coccidiitis column** always gives Reading A first, then Reading B.

## Simply put

Valley fever wears eleven old names, and Creek Fever is one of them — settled last month, which is why his "two different fevers" count works: Jamestown Canyon and valley fever are the two, and Creek Fever is valley fever under another name. The canyon family is the cousin, not the same disease — same symptoms in his thesis, different germ in the standard account. Every other fever name in his 53-name list is a different disease entirely, and one — Day fever — is still a gap.
