## The documented legs [D]

- **Jamestown Canyon virus (JCV):** orthobunyavirus of the California serogroup (family Peribunyaviridae); first isolated 1961 from *Culiseta inornata* mosquitoes in Jamestown Canyon, Colorado; mosquito-borne, widespread throughout temperate North America; causes acute febrile illness, meningitis, or meningoencephalitis. CDC 2000–2013: 31 US cases, 13 states, median age 48, two-thirds neuroinvasive, ~half hospitalized, no deaths — likely under-recognized and probably endemic through most of the US and parts of Canada. Genome: three negative-sense single-stranded RNA segments (S, M, L). White-tailed deer the likely primary amplifying host.
- **The literal cousin:** JCV is closely related to **La Crosse virus**, snowshoe hare virus, and California encephalitis virus — all California serogroup, with significant antibody cross-reactivity within the serogroup (cousins that mistake each other in the tube).
- **His record:** arbopanel #1 (Sep 16, 2025, ordered by Shannon Brown, M.D.) carried **California (La Crosse) IgG 1:20, flagged abnormal** (normal <1:10); all else <1:10. His panel #2 (Oct 29, 2025, Rick Klein, D.O.) was fully negative.
- **Coccidioidal meningitis:** real, serious, documented — the fungal leg that reaches the same ground the virus walks.
