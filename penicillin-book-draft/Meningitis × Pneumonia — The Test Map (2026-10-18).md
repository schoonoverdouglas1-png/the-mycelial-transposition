## PART 1 — THE THREE ANCHOR NODES [D]

### Node 1: Fungal meningitis

Documented causes, in rough order of frequency: *Cryptococcus neoformans* / *Cryptococcus gattii* (the leading cause worldwide) [D]; *Candida* spp. [D]; *Aspergillus* spp. [D]; *Histoplasma capsulatum* [D]; *Coccidioides* spp. [D]. Concentrated in immunocompromise but not exclusive to it. The serology that names it: cryptococcal antigen (CrAg) lateral flow assay on serum or CSF — pooled sensitivity/specificity ~98–99% in meta-analysis [D — PMC8522332]. Note the direction: CrAg is an **antigen** test — it finds shed pieces of the organism's polysaccharide capsule, not the patient's antibodies.

### Node 2: Bacterial meningitis

Documented causes by age/setting: *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Neisseria meningitidis* (the two leading adult causes) [D]; *Haemophilus influenzae* [D]; Group B *Streptococcus* (*S. agalactiae*) and *Escherichia coli* K1 (neonates) [D]; *Listeria monocytogenes* (neonates, elderly, immunocompromised) [D]. The classic CSF signature: neutrophilic pleocytosis, low glucose, high protein, often elevated opening pressure [D — standard clinical teaching].

### Node 3: *Streptococcus pneumoniae* — the 23 serotypes

The organism's capsule chemistry defines 23 serotypes in the test panel (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6B, 7F, 8, 9N, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 17F, 18C, 19A, 19F, 20, 22F, 23F, 33F) [D]. Full per-serotype profiles — vaccines, diseases, resistance, biofilms — live in his file *"His Panel × The Serotypes — The 23-Row Comparison (2026-10-17).md"* and are reused here, not re-derived. The meningitis-linked serotypes within the 23: 1, 5, 12F, 14 carry documented meningitis associations [D — from the 23-row file].

**Why these three are one map:** pneumococcus sits in both the meningitis node and the pneumonia world; fungal meningitis shares the meningeal syndrome with bacterial meningitis while sharing organisms (Cryptococcus, Aspergillus) with the fungal pneumonias. The nodes touch at the syndrome AND at the organisms.

## PART 2 — ALL KNOWN PNEUMONIA TYPES, CONNECTED TO THE NODES [D]

### Bacterial pneumonias

| Pneumonia | Connection to the anchor nodes [D] |
|---|---|
| **Pneumococcal** (*S. pneumoniae*) | IS the bridge — same organism as Node 3; bacteremia from the lung seeds the meninges (Node 2) [D — standard pathogenesis] |
| ***Klebsiella pneumoniae*** | Gram-negative necrotizing pneumonia; shares the bacteremia→sepsis pathway with pneumococcus; no meningitis role of note [D] |
| ***Pseudomonas aeruginosa*** | Nosocomial/structural-lung pneumonia; the biofilm organism — documented in polymicrobial biofilms; no meningitis role [D] |
| ***Mycoplasma pneumoniae*** | "Atypical" — no cell wall; diagnosed by PCR/serology rather than culture; extrapulmonary syndromes documented but meningitis rare [D] |
| ***Chlamydophila pneumoniae*** | Atypical; obligate intracellular; chronic-infection hypotheses exist but meningitis link not established [GAP on CNS role] |
| ***Legionella pneumophila*** | Diagnosed by urinary antigen — but the test detects **serogroup 1 only**, other serogroups/species missed [D — standard limitation]; no meningitis role |

### Viral pneumonias

| Pneumonia | Connection to the anchor nodes [D] |
|---|---|
| **Influenza** | THE documented bridge virus — influenza infection precedes and enables pneumococcal superinfection via three mechanisms: PspA adhering to dead/dying lung cells [D — Cell Reports], type I interferon sabotaging IL-17/neutrophil clearance [D — JCI 2009], pneumococcal NanA/NanB supporting viral replication [D]. The 1918 pandemic's mortality was substantially bacterial superinfection [D] |
| **SARS-CoV-2** | Viral pneumonia; secondary bacterial/fungal superinfections documented (including aspergillosis — "COVID-associated pulmonary aspergillosis") [D] |
| **RSV** | Viral pneumonia, predominantly pediatric/elderly; bacterial superinfection documented though less mechanistically mapped than flu [D] |

### Fungal pneumonias

| Pneumonia | Connection to the anchor nodes [D] |
|---|---|
| ***Pneumocystis jirovecii*** (PJP) | β-D-glucan positive; diagnosed by PCR/microscopy on BAL; shares the immunocompromised host with cryptococcal meningitis (Node 1) [D] |
| ***Aspergillus*** (invasive pulmonary) | Galactomannan (serum/BAL) is its biomarker; documented *A. fumigatus*↔pneumococcus antagonism at species level [D — from his files]; angioinvasive like disseminated crypto [D] |
| ***Cryptococcus*** (pulmonary) | Same organism as Node 1's leading meningitis cause — lung is the portal, meninges the destination [D]; CrAg positive, β-D-glucan negative (the discriminator) |
| **Endemic dimorphic** (*Histoplasma*, *Coccidioides*, *Blastomyces*) | Granulomatous like TB; Histoplasma galactomannan cross-reacts with Aspergillus assay [D]; Blastomyces yeast phase evades β-D-glucan detection [D — Mayo Labs] |

## PART 3 — THE HANDFUL OF TESTS: THE TEST MAP [D]

The organizing principle: a small set of tests whose **combined pattern** separates the entities. Each test below is documented; what it detects and which nodes it separates are stated. Nothing here recommends ordering anything.

### The map, test by test

**1. CSF profile — opening pressure, cell count with differential, glucose, protein [D]**
What it detects: the inflammatory pattern, not the organism. Separates: **bacterial** (neutrophils, low glucose, high protein) vs. **viral** (lymphocytes, normal glucose) vs. **fungal/TB** (lymphocytes, low glucose, high protein; markedly elevated opening pressure points toward cryptococcal) [D — standard clinical teaching]. This is the orienting test — it aims the rest of the map.

**2. Multiplex meningitis/encephalitis PCR panel — e.g., BioFire FilmArray ME [D]**
What it detects: 14 pathogens in CSF in ~1 hour — 6 bacteria (*E. coli* K1, *H. influenzae*, *L. monocytogenes*, *N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*, *S. agalactiae*), 7 viruses (CMV, enterovirus, HSV-1/2, HHV-6, parechovirus, VZV), 1 yeast (*Cryptococcus neoformans/gattii*) [D — Leber et al., J Clin Microbiol 2016; doi:10.1128/jcm.00730-16]. Separates: all of Node 1's crypto, all of Node 2's bacteria, and the viral encephalitides — in one run. Limitation built in: it only finds its 14 targets; *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *Histoplasma* meningitis are outside it [D].

**3. Cryptococcal antigen (CrAg), serum and CSF [D]**
What it detects: shed cryptococcal capsular polysaccharide — ~98–99% sensitivity/specificity [D — PMC8522332]. Separates: **Node 1's leading cause** from everything else on the map. Note: antigen test (finds the organism's pieces), opposite direction from antibody panels.

**4. (1→3)-β-D-glucan (Fungitell), serum [D]**
What it detects: a cell-wall polysaccharide of most invasive fungi — positive in *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *Pneumocystis*, *Fusarium* [D — Mayo Clinic Labs, mayocliniclabs.com/test-catalog/Overview/604094]. Separates by its **negatives**: it does NOT detect *Cryptococcus*, Mucorales, or (reliably) *Blastomyces* [D — same source]. The discriminator logic: **BG-positive + CrAg-negative → non-crypto fungus; BG-negative + CrAg-positive → cryptococcal.** That pair is the fungal fork of the map.

**5. Galactomannan (Platelia Aspergillus EIA), serum or BAL [D]**
What it detects: *Aspergillus* cell-wall galactomannan [D — PMC5753135]. Separates: invasive aspergillosis from other fungal pneumonias. Honest cross-reactivity [D]: the assay cross-reacts with *Histoplasma*, *Penicillium*, *Fusarium* — shared sugar structures again.

**6. Pneumococcal urinary antigen (BinaxNOW) [D]**
What it detects: C-polysaccharide cell-wall antigen common to **all** pneumococcal strains — in urine, and validated for CSF [D — PMC6955439]. Separates: pneumococcal disease from other bacterial pneumonias/meningitis. Two limits that matter: it does **not** identify the serotype (C-polysaccharide ≠ capsule) [D], and it stays positive for weeks after disease [D]. Also usable on CSF in suspected pneumococcal meningitis [D].

**7. Legionella urinary antigen [D]**
What it detects: *L. pneumophila* **serogroup 1 only** [D — standard limitation]. Separates: Legionella pneumonia from the rest — but a negative never excludes non-serogroup-1 disease. The map marks what each test cannot see.

**8. Blood cultures [D]**
What they detect: bacteremia and candidemia — the bloodstream highway both meningitis nodes travel [D]. Separates: bacteremic from non-bacteremic disease; the positive culture names the organism outright. Slow (days), but definitive when positive.

**9. Mycoplasma PCR / serology [D]**
What it detects: the wall-less atypical — invisible to Gram stain and routine culture. Separates: *Mycoplasma* pneumonia from the typical bacterial pneumonias.

**10. Respiratory viral multiplex panel [D]**
What it detects: influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV, and other respiratory viruses from a swab. Separates: the viral pneumonia layer — and per Part 4, a positive here is the **forward marker** toward likely bacterial superinfection.

### How the handful works together [LOGIC — the map's reasoning, steps shown]

Step 1: CSF profile orients — bacterial vs. viral vs. fungal/TB pattern.
Step 2: ME panel names 14 entities in an hour, covering both meningitis nodes plus viral encephalitides.
Step 3: CrAg ± β-D-glucan splits the fungal fork (crypto vs. non-crypto).
Step 4: Urinary antigens + blood cultures + Mycoplasma testing cover the pneumonia side.
Step 5: The viral panel reads the viral layer — which doubles as the forward marker (Part 4).
Ten tests, three syndromes, one map. The map shows what each test contributes; it never says which to order — that is the clinician's decision on the patient in front of them.

## PART 4 — VIRUSES AS FORWARD/BACKTRACK MARKERS

### Documented linkages [D]

- **Influenza → pneumococcus (forward):** three mechanisms — PspA binding dead/dying lung cells [D — Cell Reports], type-I-interferon-mediated sabotage of IL-17/neutrophil clearance [D — JCI 2009], pneumococcal NanA/NanB neuraminidases supporting viral replication (the backtrack: the bacterium feeds the virus too) [D]. In co-colonized mice under viral conditions, pneumococcus disperses while staph dispersal is inhibited — almost exclusively pneumococcal pneumonia follows [D — mBio 2017, from his files].
- **Viral pneumonia → bacterial superinfection (forward, general):** documented across influenza, SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-associated pulmonary aspergillosis), and RSV — damaged epithelium plus ciliary dysfunction opens the door [D].
- **CMV/EBV contexts:** documented in immunocompromised hosts as markers of the host state in which fungal disease emerges [D — general transplant/ID literature]; the specific predictive direction is less mapped than flu→pneumo [GAP on precise mechanisms].

### His marker thesis [H] — filed with the honest line

His thesis: viral findings can be used to **point forward** to likely bacterial/fungal elements and to **backtrack** from a bacterial/fungal finding to the viral context that enabled it. Honest line: the forward/backtrack method is **documented for specific pairs** (influenza↔pneumococcus above) and the **general method is his to evidence** [H]. The file keeps the documented pairs and his generalization in separate paragraphs, labeled.

### His combination approach [H] — filed as his belief

His belief, filed verbatim in spirit: antibody testing (pneumo-style, or anything parallel) **along with** the arbopanel is the combination approach — the bacterial/fungal serology and the viral serology read together, each covering what the other cannot see. Filed as his [H]; the honest note is structural: antibody panels and antigen panels look in opposite directions (his blood's response vs. the organism's pieces), so "along with" is doing real work in his design [H→D — the CrAg/antibody inversion documented in this file].

### Namology: "La Crosse in Latin equals Lyme disease" [H] — credited to Dr. Obaid as his source

His namology claim, filed as his: disease names connect to older names — "La Crosse in Latin equals Lyme disease." Honest line [D]: **"La Crosse" is French** — *la crosse*, the hooked stick or crosier — **not Latin**; **Lyme** is named for **Lyme, Connecticut**, where the disease cluster was recognized. There is no documented etymological equivalence between the two names; the equation is his namology, his to evidence.

Possible foothold [H→D]: both are vector-borne diseases prominent in the upper Midwest — La Crosse virus (mosquito, named for La Crosse, Wisconsin) and Lyme disease (tick, *Borrelia*) share geography and the vector-borne showing without sharing a name root. The geographic overlap is documented; the name equation is not. Filed side by side, labeled.

## ASSOCIATION WEB — how the nodes hang together

- **Pneumococcus** is the keystone: the only organism sitting in the meningitis node, the pneumonia list, the 23-serotype panel, and the urinary-antigen test — all four [D].
- **Cryptococcus** is the fungal keystone: the only organism sitting in the meningitis node, the pneumonia list, the CrAg test, and the ME panel [D].
- **The polysaccharide thread** runs under the whole map: pneumococcal capsules, cryptococcal capsule (CrAg target), β-D-glucan, galactomannan — four different sugar structures doing diagnostic work, with documented cross-reactivities where the sugars resemble each other [D].
- **The viral thread** runs alongside: flu enables pneumo (forward), pneumo enzymes feed flu (backtrack) — the only fully documented two-way marker pair on the map [D]; his generalization to a method is [H].
