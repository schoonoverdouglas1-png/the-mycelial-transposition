## How to read this tree

*Simply put:* this tree repeats itself on purpose. Each of the 23 serotypes appears under EVERY disease group it belongs to — written out fully each time, never "see above." The repetition is how you see what you're looking at. A serotype that shows up in seven branches is a different kind of thing than one that shows up in one. If the tree refused to repeat, that fact would be hidden. The redundancy is the point.

The expert load: this manuscript's standing redundancy rule (§227) — a serotype may hang on multiple branches, and each appearance is complete. No cross-reference, no "as noted above," no inheritance. The overlap between branches is itself diagnostic information [D the overlaps; the tree's form follows his commission]. Part A gives the trunk (the panel itself) and the ten first branches. Parts B, C, and D carry the serotype nodes — every serotype, every association, under every branch it hangs on.

**Tier tags** — every claim wears one: [D] = documented in the literature · [H] = his — Douglas's thesis, account, or coinage, filed verbatim · [H→D] = his with a documented foothold · [GAP] = unknown or untested, his to fill. The 51% bar applies: documented support at the majority level counts.

**Dual register** — every node in this tree opens with "Simply put" (6th–8th-grade telling) and then carries the expert load. Simple words, never simplified ideas.

**His two readings carried through the whole tree (filed as his):** all 23 serotypes are *Streptococcus pneumoniae* — one coccus group, 23 capsule chemistries [D the identity; H — his reading that "the group is what makes sense"].

## The Trunk — the 23-serotype IgG panel

*Simply put:* the trunk of the tree is a blood test. It measures the antibodies in your blood against 23 kinds of pneumonia-bacterium capsule. It reads immune memory — what the body remembers — not live germs.

The expert load: the *Streptococcus pneumoniae* IgG antibody panel, 23 serotypes — Mayo Clinic test code **PN23M** / Quest Laboratories test code **16963** [D]. It measures serum IgG antibodies against the capsular polysaccharides of serotypes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6B, 7F, 8, 9N, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 17F, 18C, 19A, 19F, 20, 22F, 23F, 33F [D]. More than 100 pneumococcal serotypes are known; the panel's 23 are the PPSV23 1983 formulation's 23 [D]. The test was built to test antibody response to the vaccine [D the test's design; his account of its purpose is filed as his]. A serotype's capsule is a polysaccharide; the serotype number is a capsule-chemistry classification [D]. The panel reads immune memory — antibodies in blood — never live organisms [D].

**Lab cutoffs** — Mayo: ≥1.0 mcg/mL per serotype [D]. Quest FAQ (FAQS.181, effective 10/19/2017): ≥1.3 µg/mL suggested as more relevant after pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccines and for immunocompromised patients — a higher bar than Mayo's on the page, same test, different lab [D]. Quest also offers smaller panels — 16374 (13 serotypes) and 19564 (14 serotypes), same method [D].

**His research-use thesis [H], filed as his:** he wants the test used as a RESEARCH test through Rochester/Mayo Labs — coupling serotype antibodies to the viruses each serotype connects to. The tree that follows is built for exactly that use: every branch carries virus couplings (documented where the record has them, his where it doesn't, [GAP] where nobody's looked yet).

**The trunk is one coccus group, 23 capsule chemistries [D]; the group is what makes sense [H — his reading].** Everything in the branches is *S. pneumoniae* wearing a different capsule. The branches below are ten first cuts through the 23 — ten disease-shaped ways of asking which capsule is in the room.

### Branch 1 — Meningitis

*Simply put:* the meninges are the membranes around the brain and spinal cord. "Meningitis" means those membranes are inflamed. This branch holds the serotypes that are documented reaching them.

The branch means, diagnostically: meningitis enrichment is not evenly spread across the 23 — it clusters here [D]. The group's signature contrast: 15B rides the carriage niche and still reaches the meninges [D]; 12F cannot ride the niche at all and reaches them by eruption [D]; 17F can reach them but almost never does [D]. Same endpoint, three different roads — that is what the grouping is for.

Serotypes on this branch: **1, 12F, 15B, 14, 4, 3, 7F, 18C, 17F, 23F** [D]. The anchors: 1 — northern Ghana meningitis epidemics, mirroring the meningococcal belt's seasonality and clonal dominance [D]; 12F — adult meningitis its featured complication, 33% meningitis among outbreak cases [D]; 15B — "much higher in meningitis compared to overall IPD" (Belgian reference center), pediatric meningitis documented [D]; 4 — adult meningitis via the bloodstream, 48% mortality documented [D]; 17F — one documented neonatal meningitis case, vertical transmission probable [D].

**Virus couplings:** at the group level, no documented virus–meningitis-group association is on the record — **the group's virus column is [GAP]** — nobody's asked it yet. His theses carried to this branch [H]: viral reactions happen with serotype GROUPS, not just individual serotypes; each antibody may have a virus attached to it. The work under this branch is his to build.

### Branch 2 — Pneumonia

*Simply put:* pneumonia is "the lung condition" — the lungs are sick. This branch holds the serotypes whose signature or amplified showing is pneumonia.

The branch means, diagnostically: the pneumonia group splits into two documented mechanics — the post-viral amplifiers (4, 19F, 3, whose pneumonia follows influenza) and the invasive-first pneumonias (8, 22F, whose pneumonia arrives without the virus's help) [D the patterns].

Serotypes on this branch: **3, 4, 19A, 19F, 8, 14, 12F, 1, 22F** [D]. The anchors: 3 — adult pneumonia, most prevalent strain in an 18-country adult review, plus childhood empyema [D]; 4 — the post-viral pneumonia marker, influenza amplification 58%→100% [D]; 19F — post-influenza pneumonia 91%→100% [D]; 22F — adult invasive disease with pneumonia in the replacement cohort [D].

**Virus couplings:** this is the tree's virus-heaviest branch on the documented record [D] — **influenza**. Post-viral amplification is documented head-to-head for 4 and 19F [D]; serotype 1's version of the flu interaction is not worked out [GAP]. His theses [H] — viral reactions happen with serotype groups, not just individual serotypes; each antibody may have a virus attached — land here first: the pneumonia group is where the virus walks in before the bacterium. Note for the record: the foundational influenza–pneumococcus coinfection kinetics model (the Smith within-host model) was built on serotype 2's D39 strain [D] — the mechanistic map of how the virus opens the door was drawn from a serotype modern patients almost never meet [D the model, H the bias reading]. ## Branch 1 — The Meningitis Group

*Serotypes documented with meningitis enrichment or meningitis in their disease record. Meningitis —* meninges *+* -itis*: inflammation of the membranes wrapping the brain and spinal cord [D].*

**The branch-level virus coupling (documented):** his encephalitis-in-all-viruses study counted 62 sickness-causing viruses: **85.5% have documented encephalitis** (53 of 62) and **90.3% have documented CNS involvement of some kind** (56 of 62) [D — the 2026-10-17 census]. No virus family in the study is clean of the brain [D]; the arboviral families (flavi, toga, bunya) are 20-for-20 on encephalitis [D]. His reading [H]: encephalitis is part of ALL sickness-causing viruses — the brain always reacts with inflammation as part of the disease's markers; the step from 90.3% to 100% is his to evidence, row by row [H]. For the meningitis branch, this is the context the group sits in: the membranes of the brain are where the viral and the bacterial records meet — bacterial meningitis is a documented differential in every viral-encephalitis workup [D], and cryptococcal (*Cryptococcus*, an encapsulated yeast) meningitis is a documented mimic that the workup must exclude [D]. Empiric meningitis medication (IDSA 2004): vancomycin + ceftriaxone (or cefotaxime); add rifampin in dual penicillin/cephalosporin resistance; 10–14 days [D — guideline context, never a recommendation].

#### Serotype 1 — on the Meningitis branch
**Group placement:** northern Ghana meningitis epidemics, mirroring the meningococcal belt's seasonality and clonal dominance [D].
**Simply put:** Serotype 1 is the epidemic serotype — the one that causes meningitis the way meningococcus does, in great waves that move through whole populations instead of settling into one nose [D]. It almost never lives quietly in the throat, so when it arrives, it arrives as an outbreak [D]. On the meningitis branch because it has caused meningitis *epidemics*, not just meningitis cases [D].
**The cocci:** *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, serotype 1: Gram-positive diplococcus, capsule type 1 — the epidemic capsule [D]. Its behavior reads like a different genus's playbook: the northern Ghana outbreaks caused meningitis epidemics with the seasonality, broad age range, and clonal dominance characteristic of *Neisseria meningitidis* (meningococcus), a Gram-negative diplococcus [D]. Two diplococci, one belt, mirrored epidemics — the cousin-ing is ecological, not taxonomic [D the epidemics, H the cousin-ing reading]. Capsule thickens under stress alongside 3, 19A, and 23F (CRH study) [D].
**Concurrent infections:** With serotype 5, caused >30% of Gambian invasive disease across all ages — the traveling pair, moving through populations rather than living in individuals [D]. Rarely found in carriage studies [D]; the direct serotype-1 co-pathogen record is thin [GAP].
**Disease names:** Epidemic meningitis — inflammation of the meninges (brain/spinal cord membranes) [D]; empyema (pus within the pleural cavity) [D]; invasive pneumococcal disease; pneumonia [D]. Named in the systematic outbreak review's serotype set (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7F, 8, 12F, 14, 20, 23F) [D].
**Extrapolated indications:** **High invasive potential with rare carriage** — shows up in disease far more than in the nose [D]; outbreak-setting marker: crowded settings, epidemic seasonality [D]. Wins by eruption, not persistence.
**Vaccine coverage:** PPSV23, PCV15, PCV20 — but NOT PCV21, the adult-focused 2024 conjugate [D]: the field's own arithmetic about which serotypes threaten adults now.
**Resistance:** Not a classic resistant serotype; historically penicillin-susceptible [D] — carries no resistance armor, surviving by invasiveness.
**Biofilm:** Capsule thickens under stress (CRH study, with 3/19A/23F) [D]; architecture group unmeasured [GAP] — consistent with a serotype that doesn't live in the biofilm niche.
**Virus couplings:** Serotype 1's flu interaction is not worked out head-to-head — the influenza amplification was measured for 4 and 19F, not 1 [GAP]. Post-influenza secondary bacterial pneumonia applies at the species level: *S. pneumoniae* follows influenza damage to the airways [D — IDSA/ATS CAP context]. His thesis: viral reactions at group level [H] — on the meningitis branch, 1 sits beside 4 (the documented post-viral amplifier); whether 1's epidemics ride viral seasons is [GAP]. Each antibody may carry its virus [H] — serotype 1's is unmapped [GAP]; his 46 empty fungal/yeast cells include this row [GAP].

#### Serotype 12F — on the Meningitis branch
**Group placement:** adult meningitis is its featured complication; 33% meningitis among outbreak cases [D].
**Simply put:** Serotype 12F is the one that erupts — it can't settle in anyone's nose, so it doesn't, and instead it sweeps through crowded places in waves and leaves [D]. A third of its outbreak cases reached the brain's membranes [D]. On the meningitis branch because meningitis is its featured complication, not a side note [D].
**The cocci:** *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, serotype 12F: Gram-positive diplococcus, alpha-hemolytic, encapsulated [D]. Marks the outbreak clones ST218 and ST989 (clonal complex CC218) [D]. Bacterium-cousins: *S. pyogenes* (M/emm-typed), *S. agalactiae* (neonatal meningitis), staphylococci (*S. aureus* — no serotypes at all; typed by spa/MLST/SCC*mec*), enterococci [D]. Word-cousin via his cousin-ing [H]: *Coccidioides* — the Valley-fever fungus (his "Creek Fever" [H]) — shares the *cocc-* root; shared syllables are his lens, not phylogeny [H→D seam].
**Concurrent infections:** **Hyperinvasive**: high disease potential, failure to colonize — carriage surveys repeatedly fail to find it even where it dominates disease (France, Israel) [D]. Concurrence is **crowding, not viral season**: daycare centers, military groups, a jail, a homeless shelter [D]; rural Alaska waves 2003–2008 — single dominant clones introduced episodically, spread 1–3 years, then vanished entirely: "highly virulent, but immunogenic" [D]. Adult carriage existed during outbreaks, then disappeared with them [D].
**Disease names:** Adult **meningitis** — inflammation of the meninges [D]; invasive pneumococcal disease (5.9% of adult post-PCV13 IPD); bacteremia/septicemia — bacteria in the blood [D]; pneumonia (44% of 9 outbreak cases, with 33% meningitis) [D]. A Japanese cluster report genome-sequenced ST4846 from a meningitis case [D]. Outbreak serotype with meningitis as the featured complication [D].
**Extrapolated indications:** **Outbreak-prone, meningitis-leaning, low-carriage disease** in under-vaccinated adult populations [D]. Never picked up by any conjugate before PCV20 — the PCV13 era left it uncovered, and its post-PCV7/PCV13 emergence was framed as clonal expansion [D].
**Vaccine coverage:** PPSV23, PCV20, PCV21; NOT in PCV15 [D].
**Resistance:** Classically drug-susceptible isolates [D], but emerging resistant clones: 5.3 kb mega cassette (msrD/mefE → macrolide resistance), then step-wise parC/gyrA mutations (fluoroquinolone resistance) under drug selection; some clones carry Tn5252 transposons (erm(B)/tet(M)) [D]. Watchlist, not proven at population scale — the soft edge of the resistant group [D].
**Biofilm:** Unmeasured at serotype resolution [GAP] — consistent with the eruption strategy.
**Virus couplings:** ICI-class, overrepresented in post-viral pneumonia (Song) [D] — the documented species-level viral door. Post-influenza concurrence applies at the species level (influenza NA strips sialic acid → pneumococcal adherence surges; oseltamivir improved survival ~75% in coinfected mice, McCullers 2004) [D]. But 12F's particular concurrence is crowding, not viral season [D]. His group-level thesis [H] puts 12F's viral reaction on the meningitis branch beside 1, 4, 14 — unmapped per serotype [GAP]. His 46 empty fungal/yeast cells include this row [GAP].
