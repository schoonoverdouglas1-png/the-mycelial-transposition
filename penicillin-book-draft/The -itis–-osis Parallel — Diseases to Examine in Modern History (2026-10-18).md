# The -itis–-osis Parallel — Diseases to Examine in Modern History

**Filed:** 2026-10-18 · For *The Mycelial Transposition* (manuscript reference only — never medical advice, no treatment recommendations, no personal-result interpretation)

## The postulate under test [H]

Douglas's postulate: ALL of medicine should be viewed through the osteomyelosis/osteomyelitis parallel — every *-itis* disease has a *-osis* (fungal-element) counterpart worth examining. This list tests that postulate case by case across modern history's significant *-itis* entities.

## The morphology, honestly [D]

*-itis* = inflammation — not "bacterial." Viral encephalitis, autoimmune hepatitis, and rheumatoid arthritis are all *-itis* without bacteria. *-osis* / *-iasis* = condition/process — the characteristic suffix of fungal disease names (candidiasis, aspergillosis, coccidioidomycosis, cryptococcosis). The parallel is morphological, not etiological: the claim being tested is whether each inflammatory entity has a documented fungal counterpart, not that *-itis* means bacterial.

## Tier tags

[D] documented in the literature · [H] his, filed as his · [H→D] his with a documented foothold · [GAP] no documented fungal counterpart — his postulate predicts one; the gap is the finding.

## The list

### Meningitis → cryptococcal / Candida meningitis [D]

Fungal meningitis is documented — *Cryptococcus neoformans*/*gattii* leading, plus *Candida* spp., *Aspergillus*, *Histoplasma*, *Coccidioides*. Diagnosed by cryptococcal antigen (CrAg) testing of serum/CSF. His element split puts meningitis on the fungal side; the disease is real.

### Osteomyelitis → fungal osteomyelitis [D]; *osteomyelosis* [H]

Documented and reviewed: a 2023 systematic review (678 studies, 1,072 patients) found *Aspergillus* 26.5%, *Candida* 20.7%, *Mucor* 16.8% as causes of fungal osteomyelitis, vertebrae most involved (MDPI Microorganisms 2023). His coinage *osteomyelosis* names the fungal-element category; the parallel is well-formed, the term is his.

### Pneumonia (-ia, not -itis — handled honestly) → pulmonary mycoses [D]

The suffix breaks here and the file says so: pneumonia is *-ia*, not *-itis*. The fungal counterparts are nonetheless the richest in the list — invasive aspergillosis, *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia, pulmonary cryptococcosis, and the endemic mycoses (histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis, blastomycosis). The parallel holds on substance if not on suffix.

### Arthritis → fungal arthritis [D]

Documented, reviewed: principally *Candida* (albicans leading), plus *Aspergillus*, *Histoplasma*, *Blastomyces*, *Coccidioides*, *Cryptococcus*, *Sporothrix* (JAAOS review; PMC9945246). Insidious course, often diagnosed years late — the "missed because unsuspected" pattern his book tracks.

### Endocarditis → fungal endocarditis [D]

Documented and quantified: *Candida* spp. ~50–60% of fungal endocarditis, *Aspergillus* spp. ~25%, with *Histoplasma*, *Trichosporon*, *Fusarium* also reported; mortality 54–72% across reviews (Exploration Cardiology 2025; IJID multicenter cohort). Prosthetic valves, catheters, and immunosuppression are the documented risk settings.

### Sinusitis → fungal rhinosinusitis [D]

A full documented spectrum: non-invasive (fungus ball, allergic fungal rhinosinusitis — *Aspergillus*, dematiaceous fungi) and invasive (acute fulminant mucormycosis — *Rhizopus*/*Mucor* — chronic and granulomatous invasive aspergillosis). Classification by invasiveness, not by organism alone.

### Dermatitis → dermatomycoses [D]

The most familiar parallel in the list: tinea corporis/capitis/pedis (*Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*) and cutaneous candidiasis. Fungal skin disease is textbook.

### Encephalitis → fungal (meningo)encephalitis [D]

Documented: cryptococcal meningoencephalitis is the classic form; *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *Coccidioides*, and *Histoplasma* CNS disease reported. Rarer than bacterial/viral encephalitis; concentrated in immunocompromise.

### Hepatitis → fungal hepatitis: thin [GAP-leaning]

No established "fungal hepatitis" entity. Disseminated histoplasmosis and candidiasis can involve the liver as part of systemic disease, but a primary fungal hepatitis is not a documented category. His postulate predicts one; the gap stands.

### Nephritis → Candida pyelonephritis / renal candidiasis [D]

Documented: ascending *Candida* urinary infection involving the kidney (pyelonephritis), and hematogenous renal candidiasis in disseminated disease. The renal fungal counterpart exists, concentrated in obstruction, instrumentation, and immunocompromise.

### Peritonitis → fungal peritonitis [D]

Documented and quantified in peritoneal dialysis: *Candida* spp. cause 70–86% of fungal peritonitis episodes (albicans, parapsilosis, tropicalis, glabrata); prior antibiotics are the key risk factor; mortality 14–44% across series (AJKD; Frontiers in Pediatrics 2021). A complete documented parallel.

### Cellulitis → cryptococcal / phaeohyphomycotic cellulitis: thin [D, case-report level]

Documented but rare: cryptococcal cellulitis and other mold cellulitides reported in immunocompromised hosts. Exists at case-report level — a thin [D], honestly marked.

### Bronchitis → Aspergillus tracheobronchitis [D]

Documented: *Aspergillus* tracheobronchitis (including ulcerative/pseudomembranous forms) in immunocompromised and post-influenza patients. A real fungal bronchitis entity.

### Pharyngitis → oropharyngeal candidiasis (thrush) [D]

Documented and common: *Candida* pharyngitis/thrush, especially with inhaled corticosteroids, antibiotics, dentures, and immunocompromise. Complete parallel.

### Tonsillitis → fungal tonsillitis: thin [GAP-leaning]

Essentially case-report level (*Candida* tonsillitis reported rarely). No established category — marked as a thin-to-gap finding.

### Otitis media → fungal otitis media: thin [GAP-leaning]

Otomycosis (*Aspergillus*, *Candida*) is outer-ear disease and well documented; middle-ear fungal infection is rare and reported mainly in immunocompromise or chronic perforation. The outer/inner distinction matters — marked honestly.

### Conjunctivitis → fungal keratitis [D]

Documented: *Fusarium* and *Aspergillus* keratitis (contact-lens-associated outbreaks established the entity); fungal conjunctivitis per se is rarer but reported. The ocular fungal counterpart is real.

### Urethritis → fungal urethritis [GAP]

No established category; rare *Candida* urethritis case reports exist but do not constitute a documented entity. His postulate predicts one; the gap stands.

### Vaginitis → vulvovaginal candidiasis [D]

The textbook parallel: *Candida albicans* vulvovaginitis is among the most common fungal diseases known. Complete.

### Gastritis → Candida gastritis: thin [GAP-leaning]

Rare case reports (*Candida* gastritis in immunocompromise); no established category. Thin-to-gap.

### Colitis → Histoplasma / CMV-adjacent fungal colitis: thin [D, narrow]

*Histoplasma* colitis documented in immunocompromised hosts (endemic areas); otherwise essentially a gap. Narrow documented foothold, honestly marked.

### Pancreatitis → fungal pancreatitis [GAP]

No documented entity. His postulate predicts one; the gap stands.

### Cholecystitis → fungal cholecystitis [GAP]

Rare *Candida* case reports in the literature do not constitute a documented entity. Marked as gap.

### Appendicitis → fungal appendicitis [GAP]

No documented entity. His postulate predicts one; the gap stands.

### Diverticulitis → fungal diverticulitis [GAP]

No documented entity. His postulate predicts one; the gap stands.

## Synthesis: what the pattern shows

**Where the parallel holds documented [D] — 13 of 25:** meningitis, osteomyelitis, pneumonia (substance, not suffix), arthritis, endocarditis, sinusitis, dermatitis, encephalitis, nephritis, peritonitis, bronchitis, pharyngitis, vaginitis, conjunctivitis/keratitis. The fungal counterparts cluster where medicine already looks for fungi: immunocompromise, devices and catheters, the sinuses, the skin, and the peritoneal dialysis population. The parallel is strongest exactly where fungal disease is an established discipline.

**Thin but real [D, case-report level] — 4 of 25:** cellulitis, tonsillitis, otitis media (middle ear), gastritis, colitis (narrow). These exist in the literature but barely — the honest reading is that they are under-recognized, under-reported, or genuinely rare. His book's "missed because unsuspected" pattern fits here best.

**Gaps his postulate predicts — 7 of 25:** hepatitis, urethritis, pancreatitis, cholecystitis, appendicitis, diverticulitis (and tonsillitis/gastritis at the thin edge). The gaps cluster in the abdomen's hollow organs — precisely the territory where bacterial *-itis* is commonest and fungal investigation rarest. Whether that is absence of disease or absence of looking is the open question his postulate poses — and it is a genuine research question, not a conclusion.

**The morphology held:** *-osis*/*-iasis* really is the fungal suffix across the documented rows (candidiasis, aspergillosis, cryptococcosis, mucormycosis). His *osteomyelosis* coinage follows the same rule. The universal postulate — *all* medicine through this lens — remains his [H]; this list is the case-by-case test, and it returns: mostly documented, partly thin, partly open. That is a result, not a failure.
