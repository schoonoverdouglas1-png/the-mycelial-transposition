## ABSTRACT [H/LOGIC]

His doctrine: the easiest way to diagnose is to spend the fewest tests for the most information. Diagnosis begins with symptoms; the tests are ranked by discriminating power — tests that separate multiple nodes at once go first. Names of tests are thought about and ignored both. The bacterial, yeast, and fungal elements are the organizing frame. This paper states the doctrine, shows the method, parallels it to the laboratory testing he has had done, and closes with the minimal set as his manuscript's diagnostic doctrine.

---

## 1. THE PRINCIPLE — MAXIMUM INFORMATION PER TEST [H]

Three of his doctrines converge here:

1. **The handful of tests.** From the test-map workup: a small set of tests whose *combined pattern* separates the entities — fungal meningitis, bacterial meningitis, *S. pneumoniae*, all connected pneumonias [H]. The question is never "which test finds X" but "which few tests, together, separate everything."
2. **No more panel.** Test names should be thought about and ignored both — the name tells you what the namer thought the test was for; it must not constrain what the result is allowed to mean [H]. A result can point at origins the name never contemplated. Filed: *Thesis Note — No More Panel (2026-10-18).*
3. **Symptoms first.** "Symptoms are the beginning and basis of diagnosis" [H]. The clinical history has always been diagnosis's starting point [D — standard clinical method]; his doctrine states it as law. Every test is ordered against a symptom picture, never in place of one.

The principle, stated whole: **rank every candidate test by information yield per test — the number of diagnostic branches it opens — and spend the smallest set that opens the most branches.** [H]

## 2. THE METHOD — TESTS RANKED BY DISCRIMINATING POWER

### 2.1 What "discriminating power" means [LOGIC]

A test's value is not what it detects but **how many possibilities it eliminates at once**. Steps:

1. List the entities on the table (the three anchor nodes + connected pneumonias — from the test map, Part 2).
2. For each candidate test, count the branches: which entities does a positive rule in, which does a negative rule out.
3. Order the tests so that each successive test splits the largest remaining ambiguity.

### 2.2 The ordered sequence — from the test map, by yield

**Test 1 — the orienting test: symptoms + CSF profile [D/H].** Symptoms first (his doctrine). Where meningitis is in question, the CSF profile — cell count with differential, glucose, protein, opening pressure — splits three ways at once: neutrophilic/low glucose/high protein (bacterial) vs. lymphocytic/normal glucose (viral) vs. lymphocytic/low glucose/high protein with high opening pressure (fungal/TB, pointing toward cryptococcal) [D — standard clinical teaching]. One test, three branches — the highest yield on the map. This is why it aims everything that follows.

**Test 2 — the multiplex meningitis/encephalitis panel [D].** Fourteen targets in ~1 hour from CSF: 6 bacteria, 7 viruses, 1 yeast (*Cryptococcus neoformans/gattii*) [D — Leber et al., J Clin Microbiol 2016; doi:10.1128/jcm.00730-16]. It names entities across *both* meningitis nodes plus the viral encephalitides in one run. Built-in honesty: it only finds its 14 targets; *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *Histoplasma* meningitis are outside it [D].

**Test 3 — the fungal fork: CrAg ± (1→3)-β-D-glucan [D].** Cryptococcal antigen finds shed capsular polysaccharide (~98–99% sensitivity/specificity) [D — PMC8522332]. β-D-glucan is positive in *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *Pneumocystis*, *Fusarium* — and **negative** in *Cryptococcus*, Mucorales, and (reliably) *Blastomyces* [D — Mayo Clinic Labs, mayocliniclabs.com/test-catalog/Overview/604094]. The discriminator: **BG-positive + CrAg-negative → non-crypto fungus; BG-negative + CrAg-positive → cryptococcal.** Two tests, one fork, the fungal element sorted.

**Test 4 — the bloodstream and the lung: blood cultures + urinary antigens [D].** Blood cultures travel the bloodstream highway both meningitis nodes use — bacteremia and candidemia named outright, slow but definitive [D]. Pneumococcal urinary antigen detects C-polysaccharide common to all pneumococcal strains (validated on CSF too) [D — PMC6955439] — but does not identify serotype and stays positive for weeks [D]. Legionella urinary antigen detects serogroup 1 only [D — standard limitation] — the map marks what each test cannot see.

**Test 5 — the atypical and the viral layer: Mycoplasma PCR/serology + respiratory viral multiplex [D].** Mycoplasma is invisible to Gram stain and routine culture — the test exists because the organism evades the others [D]. The viral panel reads influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV — and a positive here is the **forward marker** (Section 4).

### 2.3 The yield ledger [LOGIC]

| Order | Test | Branches opened |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Symptoms + CSF profile | Bacterial vs. viral vs. fungal/TB — three branches |
| 2 | Multiplex ME panel | Names up to 14 entities across both meningitis nodes + viral encephalitides |
| 3 | CrAg + β-D-glucan | The fungal fork: crypto vs. non-crypto |
| 4 | Blood cultures + urinary antigens | Bacteremic vs. not; pneumococcal vs. other bacterial pneumonia |
| 5 | Mycoplasma + viral multiplex | Atypical pneumonia; the viral layer (doubles as forward marker) |

The logic: each step splits the largest remaining ambiguity, and no test is spent until the previous one has aimed it. Least testing, most information. [H]

### 3.2 What each test contributes, informationally

**The pneumo panel [D/H]:** it measures serotype-specific IgG — *how much antibody the blood holds per capsule* [D — Mayo PN23M]. Informationally, it is one test that reports **23 analytes** — the highest per-test analyte count in his history. What it cannot see: function (ELISA measures binding, not killing — the elderly gap documented in PMC6169307), colonization, carriage, biofilms, or the biome [D — from his serology paper]. The blood test and the biome are different instruments pointed at different things [D].

**The arbopanel [D/H]:** it measures IgG + IgM against four arboviruses — the viral-serology layer [D]. Informationally, it looks where the pneumo panel cannot: at the viruses, and at encephalitis-capable agents [D — California (La Crosse) is an encephalitis virus]. Its honest limit: antibody serology reports the immune system's memory, not the organism's presence [D — standard serology].

### 3.3 What the combination contributes — his combination thesis [H]

His thesis, filed: antibody testing (pneumo-style or parallel) **along with** the arbopanel is the combination approach — the bacterial/fungal serology and the viral serology read together, each covering what the other cannot see [H]. The structural honest note [H→D]: antigen tests (CrAg) find the organism's pieces; antibody tests find the blood's response — opposite looking directions. "Along with" is doing real work in his design: two instruments, opposite directions, one reading.

### 3.4 The interpretive strides — his framework, tier-tagged

- **His top/bottom pattern [H]:** "when the top test was active the bottom ones may or may not show; when you have any of the bottom ones activated, the top one always shows also" — filed verbatim, his logical structure, no medical inference drawn here.
- **Name-ignored readings [H]:** per his "no more panel" doctrine, each test is read for what it measures, not what it is called [H].
- **His antibody-persistence thesis [H] vs. the standard framework [D]:** his thesis — antibody showing anywhere means still infected [H]. The standard framework, stated generally and not applied to any person: IgM marks recent/acute, IgG marks past exposure and immunity and persists, IgA is mucosal [D]. The two positions stand side by side, unreconciled here; what his results mean for him is Kelsey's call.

## 4. VIRUSES AS FORWARD/BACKTRACK MARKERS IN MINIMAL TESTING

The minimal-testing strategy uses the viral finding to aim the next single most-informative test [H/LOGIC]:

- **Forward:** a positive influenza on the respiratory viral panel points toward pneumococcal superinfection — three documented mechanisms (PspA binding dead/dying lung cells; type-I interferon sabotaging IL-17/neutrophil clearance; pneumococcal neuraminidases supporting viral replication) [D — Cell Reports; JCI 2009; from his files]. The next most-informative test after a positive flu: pneumococcal detection (urinary antigen / blood cultures) [LOGIC — one step, the documented pair]. The 1918 pandemic's mortality was substantially bacterial superinfection [D] — the forward marker has history.
- **Backtrack:** a bacterial or fungal finding points back to the viral context that enabled it — damaged epithelium plus ciliary dysfunction opens the door [D — general viral→bacterial superinfection literature]. The next most-informative test after an unexpected bacterial/fungal result: the viral panel, to find the enabler [LOGIC].
- **The documented two-way pair:** flu enables pneumo (forward); pneumococcal enzymes feed viral replication (backtrack) — the only fully documented two-way marker pair on the map [D]. His generalization of the method beyond documented pairs is his thesis, his to evidence [H].

The viral marker is what makes the sequence *minimal*: instead of running everything, the virus tells you which single test to run next. [H]

## 5. CLOSING — THE MINIMAL SET AS DIAGNOSTIC DOCTRINE [H]

His diagnostic doctrine, stated whole:

1. **Symptoms first** — the beginning and basis of diagnosis [H].
2. **Least testing, most information** — rank tests by discriminating power; spend the smallest set that opens the most branches [H].
3. **Names ignored** — thought about and ignored both; the name on the order form is not the thing in the tube [H].
4. **Elements as the organizing frame** — bacterial, yeast, fungal; each needing its own reading [H].
5. **Viruses as markers** — forward to what they enable, backtrack to what enabled [H].
6. **Clinician and patient together** — testing decisions belong to both; consent is required for the decision to be consensual [H — his doctrine].

The strides are filed: his top/bottom pattern, his combination approach, his antibody-persistence thesis, his "no more panel" doctrine — each labeled his, each standing beside the documented record, unreconciled where they disagree. The manuscript holds both; the clinician holds the patient.
