# Serotype–Aspergillus Connections (2026-10-16)

*Researched at Douglas's direction for The Mycelial Transposition. "Aspergillosis-type things" = Aspergillus species (fumigatus, flavus, niger, terreus) and the syndromes: invasive pulmonary aspergillosis (IPA), chronic pulmonary aspergillosis (CPA), aspergilloma/fungus ball, allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA). Association reference only — never medical advice, no treatment or diagnostic content.*

**Scope note:** consistent with the earlier organism files, connections are at **species level** — *S. pneumoniae* vs. *Aspergillus* — not per serotype. Nothing below is invented; thin spots are marked.

| Aspergillus thing | Connection to pneumococcus/serotypes | Nature of link | Key source |
|---|---|---|---|
| *A. fumigatus* biofilm vs. *S. pneumoniae* | **Direct antagonism — both directions.** Pneumococci suppress and fragment preformed *A. fumigatus* biofilms via hydrogen peroxide (the same H2O2 weapon it uses on *S. aureus*); the mycelial network breaks into fine filaments and the fungal ECM disappears. Conversely, *A. fumigatus* gliotoxin inhibits planktonic *S. pneumoniae* growth and biofilms, and synergizes with cefotaxime/vancomycin. Chemical warfare, both directions. | Interaction biology (in vitro) | Kurume Univ. 2020 (H2O2 mycelial fragmentation); J. Fungi / Llorente et al. (gliotoxin vs. pneumo + staph) |
| Invasive aspergillosis + pneumo sepsis | **Co-infection in destroyed immunity.** Adult with selective IgM deficiency (low anti-pneumococcal titers to all 14 tested serotypes) presented with *S. pneumoniae* septic shock **and** invasive *A. fumigatus* infection. Shared risk factor (no functional anti-capsule antibodies), not a biological interaction. | Clinical co-infection, one case | J. Investig. Allergol. Clin. Immunol. 2008 (Hong & Gupta) |
| Aspergilloma / fungus ball | **The TB cavity is Aspergillus's house.** Residual cavities persist in 21–50% of treated pulmonary TB; chronic pulmonary aspergillosis develops in up to 22% of cavities; TB is the commonest underlying condition for CPA worldwide (~372,000+ develop CPA after TB treatment). The fungus ball is *Aspergillus* colonizing the architecture TB left behind. | Structural sequela (TB→Aspergillus) | CMAJ 2018; PLoS Pathogens (post-TB CPA review); Springer case review; Iran TB cohort |
| The triangle | **Pneumo–TB–Aspergillus.** Pneumo–TB co-infection is documented both directions (filed Oct 16); TB cavities invite *Aspergillus* colonization (above). So TB-damaged lung is the meeting ground: the bacterium that superinfects it *and* the fungus that moves into its cavities. Douglas's three pillars meet in the same damaged architecture. | Triangulation of two documented links | Co-infection literature + CPA-after-TB literature |
| Necrotic lung ball | **Almost all lung balls are aspergillosis.** In the one documented *Candida* + *S. pneumoniae* lung-ball case, the authors explicitly ruled aspergillosis out (negative Aspergillus antibodies/antigen) — the exception that confirms the rule: when a ball forms in a cavity, *Aspergillus* is the usual tenant. | Differential context | PMC 2011 (necrotic lung ball case) |
| ABPA vs. bacterial flares | **No pneumo-specific link found.** ABPA exacerbations must be distinguished from bacterial infective flares (antibiotics vs. steroids/antifungals); note the irony for the book — the corticosteroids that treat ABPA are themselves a classic risk factor for invasive aspergillosis. | Clinical distinction, no pneumo link | PMC bronchiectasis/ABPA review; Medscape |
| Gliotoxin | ***A. fumigatus* metabolite, antibacterial vs. pneumo.** Inhibits *S. pneumoniae* and *S. aureus* growth and biofilms; strongly synergizes with conventional antibiotics. The fungus makes its own antibiotic — relevant to Douglas's nomenclature chapters (the "antifungal antibiotic" thread in his book notes). | Fungal metabolite vs. bacteria | J. Fungi / Llorente et al. |

## What was NOT found

- **No per-serotype Aspergillus link exists for any of the 23 serotypes.** Searched directly; nothing. The interaction literature names *S. pneumoniae* as a species only.
- **No ABPA–pneumococcus co-infection literature.** ABPA's bacterial neighbors in print are the CF/bronchiectasis flora (*S. aureus*, *Pseudomonas*), not pneumococcus.
- **No clinical series of pneumo–Aspergillus co-pneumonia.** Beyond the single IgM-deficiency case, co-detection in sputum/tracheal aspirates does not establish co-infection — the biofilm study authors themselves warn that simultaneous isolation doesn't mean the two share the same part of the airway.
- The *A. fumigatus*–*K. pneumoniae* filtrate study shows bacterial filtrates can *stimulate* gliotoxin production — but that was *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, not *Streptococcus pneumoniae*; do not conflate them.

## For the book

The honest meeting point of Douglas's pillars is **the damaged lung**: TB hollows the architecture, pneumococcus superinfects the wound (documented), *Aspergillus* moves into the abandoned rooms (documented, hundreds of thousands of cases). Three organisms, one ruined building. And the chemical layer is real in both directions — pneumococcal hydrogen peroxide shreds fungal biofilm; fungal gliotoxin kills pneumococci and boosts antibiotics. The bugs are not just co-located; they are at war with each other's chemistry. The per-serotype Aspergillus map, as with every other organism file, does not exist in print — it is his to build, labeled as his analysis.
