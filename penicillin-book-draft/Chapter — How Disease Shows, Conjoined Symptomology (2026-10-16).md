# Chapter — How Disease Shows: Conjoined Symptomology (2026-10-16)

*Dedicated at Douglas's direction for The Mycelial Transposition. His chapter, his method: assess the conjoined symptomology between Latin-named diseases — that is where he draws his extra connections. Manuscript material, never medical advice.*

## His premise (filed as his)

Disease must be studied **the way it shows** — the symptomology, the felt and observable manifestation. And differently-named diseases may show the same way: **conjoined symptomology between Latin name diseases needs to be assessed, and that's where I draw my extra connections.** The names divide; the showing joins. The chapter's work is to lay the showings side by side and find where they meet.

## The method

1. Take two or more Latin-named diseases.
2. Write out how each one shows — symptoms, course, fluids, tissues.
3. Mark the conjoined ground: the symptoms they share.
4. Draw the connection — his to draw, labeled as his.

## Worked examples (from this thread's material)

**One name, many members — meningitis.** "Meningitis" shows one way (fever, neck stiffness, altered consciousness — the classic showing), but the name contains serotypes 3, 4, 14, 18C and more. Same showing, multiple numbered members. The name is the group; the showing is the join.

**The triangle — TB, aspergillosis, pneumonia.** Three Latin names; one pulmonary showing: cough, fever, night sweats, weight loss, cavities on imaging. His thesis: what manifests as tuberculosis *is* aspergillosis + pneumonia combined. The conjoined symptomology is the evidence he assesses; the chain-of-custody on each diagnosis is how he evidences it.

**The reverse case — lupus.** *Lupus vulgaris* (skin TB) and *lupus* (systemic autoimmune) share a name and show nothing alike. Same name, different showing — the mirror image that proves the rule: names and showings must be assessed independently, in both directions.

**Encephalitis — the byproduct showing.** Brain inflammation showing downstream of sickness, not as the primary event (his teacher's framing). The showing is the marker; the sickness is the story.

## The honest line the chapter must carry

Conjoined symptomology is real — and it is exactly why differential diagnosis exists. Shared showing does not prove shared cause; it proves shared *ground*, and the ground is where his connections are drawn. Every connection in the scaffold above is his assessment to make and his to evidence — the chapter shows the work, case by case, culture by culture, custody chain by custody chain.
