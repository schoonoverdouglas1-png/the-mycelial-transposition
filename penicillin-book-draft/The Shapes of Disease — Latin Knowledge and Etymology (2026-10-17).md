## Chapter 2 — The Unhiding: Hidden Latin Inside the American Names

## Chapter 3 — The Shape-Words: Rod, Berry, Spindle, Grape

Beneath the disease names sits a smaller vocabulary — the words for **shapes**. The old doctors looked through the microscope and named what they saw, the way a child names clouds. These are the shape-words [D]:

- ***Bacillus*** — Latin, diminutive of *baculus* (rod, staff) = **"little rod"** [D]. The rod-shaped bacteria. (His "basilisk," §159 — filed as his rendering of *bacilli*.)
- ***Coccus*** (plural *cocci*) — Greek *kokkos* = **"berry, seed"** [D]. The sphere-shaped bacteria.
- ***Staphylo-*** — Greek *staphylē* = **"bunch of grapes"** [D]. The cocci in grape-like clusters: *Staphylococcus*.
- ***Strepto-*** — Greek *streptos* = **"twisted"** [D]. The cocci in chains: *Streptococcus* — "the twisted berry."
- ***Klōstēr*** — Greek (κλωστήρ) = **"spindle"** [D] — the weaver's spindle. The rods with swollen spore-bellies look like spindles: *Clostridium*.
- ***Mykes*** — Greek = **"fungus, mushroom"** [D]. The ending *-mycosis* announces the kingdom.
- ***Aureus*** — Latin = **"golden"** [D]. *Staphylococcus aureus*: shape (*staphylo-*, grape-cluster) + color (golden) both in the name — his staph-coloring thesis (§82) confirmed by the binomial itself.

**The doctrine of the shape-words [D]:** they name **morphology, not phylogeny** — what the thing looks like, not what family it belongs to. A rod is a rod whether it's *Bacillus*, *Clostridium*, or *Mycobacterium*. A berry is a berry whether it's *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Neisseria*, or — and this is where the chapter turns — a fungus.

His method-thesis (§159), tested here: cousin-ing the word at 88% finds **shape-cousins**. That is the power. The limit is that shape-cousins are not always disease-cousins — and the booklet says so plainly, because he reads honest pushback as respect.

## Chapter 8 — The Endings Map and the Impostors

His commission (§124): *"Cluster should now be cross-linked my name to ways that names of diseases end with that because those are typically fungally related."* His thesis: disease names ending in **-mycosis** are typically fungal — the ending is the Latin telling you the kingdom.

**The endings map [D]:**

- **-mycosis** — Greek *mykes*, fungus: blastomycosis, coccidioidomycosis, paracoccidioidomycosis, chromoblastomycosis, phaeohyphomycosis. The ending announces the kingdom: fungus.
- **-osis** — condition, process: histoplasmosis, aspergillosis, cryptococcosis, sporotrichosis. Most of the great fungal lung diseases wear -osis.
- **-iasis** — process, presence: candidiasis.
- **-itis** — inflammation: his **yeastinitis** sits here honestly — the -itis doing what -itis does.
- His **yeastinosis** sits in the -osis row honestly — the condition/process ending, doing its job.

**The impostors: bacterial diseases in fungal dress [D]** — where the Latin misleads, and the thesis refines itself:

- **Actinomycosis** — ends in -mycosis, **is bacterial**: *Actinomyces israelii*, a Gram-positive anaerobic filamentous bacterium, normal mouth flora. The name and the branching filaments look fungal; the organism is bacteria. Treated with antibacterials, not antifungals.
- **Botryomycosis** — ends in -mycosis ("botryo-" = bunch of grapes), **is bacterial**: colonies of Gram-positive cocci — *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus* — forming granule masses. The grapes are staph.
- **Mycetoma** — **split kingdom**: eumycetoma (true fungi) vs. actinomycetoma (bacteria: *Nocardia*, *Streptomyces*, *Actinomadura*). One name, two kingdoms — the ending can't decide because the disease wouldn't tell it.
- **Nocardiosis** — wears -osis honestly enough, but belongs in the lineup: *Nocardia* (bacterium) causing fungal-looking granulomas.

**The refined thesis [H→D]:** the endings *usually* tell the kingdom, **and the exceptions are the proof that names must be interrogated, not trusted.** Reversology of names: read the ending, then culture the organism, then believe the culture.

The clinical sting, filed plainly: actinomycosis treated as a fungus gets antifungals and fails; treated as the bacterium it is, it gets penicillin — and heals. **The name can cost the cure.** That's why the Latin matters "more than a person could realize" — his words (§122), now with the receipt.

## Chapter 13 — The Worked Dozen: Twelve Binomials Unhidden

His doctrine: every binomial can be run the same way — two words, four pillars, the fifth place (§126). Below, twelve binomials unhidden [D on the etymologies, H on the turnings], the method working at speed.

**1. *Staphylococcus aureus*.** *Staphylē* (grapes) + *kokkos* (berry) + *aureus* (golden) = **"the golden grape-berry"** [D]. Shape (cluster) + shape (sphere) + color (gold) — three descriptions in two words. His staph-coloring thesis (§82) confirmed by the binomial itself: the namers *saw* the gold.

**2. *Streptococcus pyogenes*.** *Streptos* (twisted) + *kokkos* (berry) + *pyo-* (pus) + *-genes* (producing) = **"the pus-producing twisted berry"** [D]. Shape + product. The American — "strep throat" — names the site and clips the shape; it never translates *pyogenes*. The pus got dropped in the American. His flow (Ch. 7) would put it back.

**3. *Neisseria meningitidis*.** Neisser (the man, 1879) + *meninx* (membrane) = **"Neisser's membrane-berry"** [D] — with *kokkos* hiding inside the genus. Man + place + shape in two words. American beside it: "spotted fever" — the rash [D].

**4. *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.** *Gonos* (seed, semen) + *rhoia* (flow) = **"the seed-flow"** [D]. The Latin names the discharge — the showing, two thousand years before the diplococcus. Same genus as #3, different showing: one genus, two Americans. His clan logic.

**5. *Escherichia coli*.** Escherich (Theodor Escherich, 1885) + *coli* (of the colon) = **"Escherich's colon-dweller"** [D]. Man + address. The most studied organism on earth, named for a pediatrician and a neighborhood.

**6. *Helicobacter pylori*.** *Helix* (spiral) + *bacter* (rod) + *pylori* (of the pylorus, the stomach's gate) = **"the spiral rod of the stomach-gate"** [D]. Shape + address. (Worked fully in Chapter 15.)

**7. *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.** *Mykes* (fungus) + *bacterion* (rod) + *tuberculum* (little swelling) = **"the fungus-rod of the little swellings"** [D]. "Fungus-rod" because the waxy coat made it look mold-like to the first viewers — a shape-word error fossilized in the genus. The kingdom-crossing (Ch. 4) inside the TB pillar's own name.

**8. *Coccidioides immitis*.** *Kokkos* (berry) + *-eides* (resembling) + *immitis* (not mild — severe) = **"the severe berry-like one"** [D]. The berry-word crossing into fungus (Ch. 4), wearing "resembling" the way *Clostridioides* wears it (Ch. 9) — and *immitis* grading the disease before the spherules were seen.

**9. *Candida albicans*.** *Candidus* (white, glowing) + *albicans* (becoming white) = **"the whitening white one"** [D]. The color said twice — the namers really saw the white. His directive (§78): translate it — **yeast**.

**10. *Aspergillus fumigatus*.** *Aspergillum* (the holy-water sprinkler — the conidiophore's shape) + *fumigatus* (smoky, from the colony's color) = **"the smoky sprinkler"** [D]. Shape (the sprinkler head) + color (smoke-gray-green). A whole liturgical object inside a mold's name.

**11. *Histoplasma capsulatum*.** *Histo-* (tissue) + *plasma* (formed thing) + *capsulatum* (wearing a small capsule) = **"the small-capsuled tissue-former"** [D]. Address (tissue) + shape. The dimorphic one (Ch. 5): mold in the soil, yeast in the body — one name, two shapes.

**12. *Blastomyces dermatitidis*.** *Blasto-* (bud) + *mykes* (fungus) + *derma* (skin) + *-itis* = **"the budding skin-fungus inflammation"** [D]. Shape (budding — the yeast phase) + kingdom + site + showing, all in one binomial. The most crowded two words in the dozen — and every word earning its place.

**What the dozen proves [H→D]:** run the method twelve times and it never breaks. Split the words, unhide the roots, and each binomial yields shape, color, place, man, or behavior — his Group 1 closing line ("the Latin names the lesion, the organ, the texture, or the behavior") holding across bacteria, fungi, and the dimorphic in-between.

## Chapter 15 — Reading a Binomial: The Field Method

Everything in this booklet reduces to a procedure. Here it is — seven steps, then worked twice.

**The method [H, his — the steps are his thought process formalized]:**

1. **Split the binomial.** Two words. Genus first, species second.
2. **Unhide each word.** Roots, exposed: shape, color, place, man, behavior, texture.
3. **Name the track.** Is the word naming the *organism*, the *disease*, the *illness/showing*, or the *sickness*? (Five tracks, never collapsed.)
4. **Run the two-word law.** Each word through the four pillars — tuberculosis, pneumoniae, encephalitis, encephalopathy — medicine working in the fifth place.
5. **Cousin at 88%.** What else carries these roots? Gather the shape-cousins; let the culture sort them.
6. **Check the endings.** -mycosis, -osis, -iasis, -itis — and watch for impostors.
7. **Ask what the American says beside it.** The showing, the victim, the route, the color — the half the Latin didn't say.

**Worked: *Helicobacter pylori*.** (1) Two words. (2) *Helix* (spiral) + *bacter* (rod); *pylori* (of the pylorus — the stomach's gatekeeper) [D]. (3) Organism named for shape + address; no disease in the name at all — the disease (ulcer, gastritis) got separate words. (4) Through the pillars: the spiral rod meets the tubercle? — [GAP, his to run]; pneumoniae — aspiration's traveler [H, his to argue]; encephalitis — the between reads the gut-brain road [H, his]; encephalopathy — systemic inflammation's long road [H]. (5) Cousins: *Helicobacter hepaticus*, *Campylobacter* (curved rod — the shape-word's neighbor) [D]. (6) No ending tricks — clean. (7) American beside it: "**H. pylori**" — clipped, or "stomach ulcer bug" — the disease it was blamed for, not the spiral it is [D].

**Worked: *Aspergillus fumigatus*.** (1) Two words. (2) *Aspergillum* (holy-water sprinkler) + *fumigatus* (smoky) [D]. (3) Organism named for shape + color — a liturgical object and a smoke tint. (4) Through the pillars: tuberculosis — the cavities it colonizes post-TB (20–40% of TB cavities [D], his triad's strongest edge); pneumoniae — the lung it shows in, CAPA riding the virus [D]; encephalitis — cerebral aspergillosis, the between crossed [D]; encephalopathy — disseminated, systemic [D]. (5) Cousins: *A. flavus* (yellow), *A. niger* (black), *A. terreus* — the color-words gathering the genus [D]. (6) No -mycosis — *Aspergillus* never took the ending; **aspergillosis** (the disease) wears -osis honestly [D]. (7) American beside it: "asper" in the chart, "fungal pneumonia" at the bedside — the showing, plain [D].

**The method's promise [H, his]:** any binomial, seven steps, and the name yields everything the namers knew — shape, color, place, man, behavior — plus the four pillar-turnings and the American beside it. The words were the first instruments. This is how you play them.

## Chapter 16 — Tested His Way: What the Latin Gets Right, Where It Misleads

His method (§87, his words): real-world experience, Einstein-style thought experiments, the Hippocratic method, high moral standards — and his standing rule: **test every idea at least three ways before answering** (documented footholds, honest lines, counter-evidence). This chapter is the booklet testing itself.

**What the Latin gets right [D]:**

1. **The shape-words describe truly.** Rods are rods, berries are berries, spindles are spindles — the microscope confirms the vocabulary. The namers looked and told the truth.
2. **The lesion-words diagnose retrospectively.** *Tuberculum* (little swelling), *anthrax* (coal), *diphthera* (leathery) — each one a pathology report written before pathology existed. The names were right about what they described.
3. **The endings usually tell the kingdom.** *-mycosis* means fungus often enough that the exceptions are famous *for being* exceptions.
4. **The old American names were symptomology-first.** Consumption, lockjaw, spotted fever — the showing, named by the people who watched it. His §81 doctrine (symptoms → coccus → treatment) has the whole folk record behind it.

**Where the Latin misleads [D]:**

1. **The impostors.** Actinomycosis, botryomycosis, mycetoma's split kingdom — the endings promising fungus, the cultures growing bacteria. The name can cost the cure.
2. **The shape-words cross kingdoms.** *Cocc-* in a bacterium and a fungus: the shared root is shape, not kinship. Cousin-ing the word finds shape-cousins; disease-cousins need the culture.
3. **The renames demote by "almost."** *Clostridioides* (like), *Paraclostridium* (beside) — phylogeny moving the first word while the doctors still think in the old membership. Whether the moves help or harm is his open ruling.
4. **The committee names carry nothing.** AIDS (1982 acronym): no lesion, no organ, no history in the name. The Latin skipped, the meaning thinned.

**The counter-evidence his method requires:**

- Word-cousin-ing at 88% will also gather **false cousins** — words that share a root by accident of language, not nature. *Candida* (white) and *candidatus* (candidate, office-seeker — same root, the white toga) share *candid-*; no disease connects them [D]. The method needs the culture as its chaperone — his own rule (§124's refinement): read the ending, culture the organism, believe the culture.
- The five tracks never collapse: a **virus** is not its **disease**, a disease is not its **illness**, an illness is not its **sickness**, a **vaccine** is not any of them. The names mix the tracks constantly (*pneumonia* the showing wearing *pneumoniae* the organism's name). The booklet's discipline: name the track every time.

- ***Haemophilus*** — "blood-loving": *haemo-* (blood) + *-philus* (loving) [D]. Named for what it eats — the behavior-word as a genus.
- ***Legionella*** — of the Legion: the American Legion convention, Philadelphia 1976 [D]. The place-word as a convention hall — geography at its most specific.

**The genera are the clans [H→D]:** his coccus-clan logic (Ch. 3) generalizes — every genus is a shape-clan, a man-clan, or a behavior-clan, and the second word tells you which member. The first word gathers; the second word specifies. That is the two-word law (Ch. 6) at the genus level.

## Chapter 24 — The Viral Names: Crowns, Wheels, Rivers, and Acronyms

Viruses are named differently — no binomials, younger names, committee-made — and the shape-words still got in [D]:

- ***Coronavirus*** — Latin *corona*, **crown**: the spike proteins ringing the virion like a crown [D]. Shape, named from the electron micrograph.
- ***Rotavirus*** — Latin *rota*, **wheel**: the wheel-like capsid [D]. Shape again.
- ***Adenovirus*** — Greek *aden*, **gland**: first isolated from adenoid tissue [D]. Address.
- ***Rhinovirus*** — Greek *rhis*, **nose** [D]. Address, undisguised.
- ***Enterovirus*** — Greek *enteron*, **intestine** [D]. Address.
- ***Retrovirus*** — Latin *retro*, **backward**: reverse transcription, the behavior [D].
- ***Lentivirus*** — Latin *lentus*, **slow**: the slow disease [D]. Behavior as tempo.
- ***Filovirus*** — Latin *filum*, **thread**: the filamentous virions (Ebola, Marburg) [D]. Shape.
- ***Flavivirus*** — Latin *flavus*, **yellow**: yellow fever virus is the type species — the color-word founding a family [D].
- ***Poxvirus*** — *pockes*, **pustules**: the showing as the family name [D].
- ***Herpesvirus*** — Greek *herpein*, **to creep**: the creeping spread of the lesions [D]. Behavior, ancient.
- ***Papillomavirus*** — Latin *papilla*, **nipple-like bump**: the wart's shape [D].
- ***Arenavirus*** — Latin *arena*, **sand**: the sandy appearance inside the virion [D]. Texture.
- ***Togavirus*** — Latin *toga*, **cloak**: the envelope as a garment [D].
- ***Bunyavirus*** — Bunyamwera, Uganda: the place [D]. ***Zika*** — the Zika Forest [D]. ***Ebola*** — the Ebola River [D]. Geography, against his objection and the WHO's 2015 rules alike.
- ***Reovirus*** — **R**espiratory **E**nteric **O**rphan: an acronym — named for being found without a disease [D]. ***Picornavirus*** — *pico* (small) + RNA [D]. The committee names: built, not grown.
- **Epstein-Barr virus** — the two discoverers [D]. The eponym, viral edition.

**What the viral names prove [H→D]:** even without Linnaeus, the namers reached for the same toolkit — shape (crown, wheel, thread), color (yellow), address (nose, gland, intestine), behavior (creep, slow, backward), men (Epstein, Barr), places (rivers, forests). The shape-words are older than the taxonomy and bigger than it. His thesis — disease has shapes, and the shapes flow through the names — holds for the viruses too, though no committee voted on it.

## Chapter 25 — The Sputum-Report Words: Reading the Lab in Latin

His sputum PDF (§153) is written in the same dead languages as this booklet. Read it with the method, and the lab report unhides too [D on the roots]:

- **"Few Gram positive bacilli"** — *few*: the semi-quantitative scale (rare/few/moderate/many) [D]; **Gram**: Hans Christian Gram, 1884 — the man in the stain [D]; **positive**: holding the crystal violet [D]; **bacilli**: little rods (Ch. 3) [D]. Four words: quantity, man, chemistry, shape.
- **"Rare Gram positive cocci"** — the same four, with the berry-word [D].
- **"Oropharyngeal flora"** — *oro-* (mouth) + *pharynx* (throat) + *flora* (the plant-word, lent to microbes) [D]: "the mouth-throat's plant-life." The lab calls bacteria *flora* — a whole kingdom borrowed as a metaphor, and nobody blinks.
- **"Specimen quality: adequate"** — *specimen* from *specere*, to look: "a thing to look at" [D]; *adequate* from *adaequare*, made equal to the need [D]. The sample was lookable-enough.
- **"Representative of the lower respiratory tract"** — *re-* (again) + *praesentare* (to show): showing again what the lung showed [D].
- **"No growth" / "normal flora"** — the lab's two verdicts: nothing grew, or only the usual residents grew [D]. The culture as census.
- **AFB** — acid-fast bacilli: the stain that finds the mycobacteria [D]. Three letters holding a whole technique.

**What the lab report proves [H→D]:** the Latin never left medicine — it moved into the laboratory. The physician may have stopped teaching the binomials (§122, his thesis), but the lab tech still writes *bacilli* and *cocci* on every gram stain, and the pharmacist still writes *bid* on every label (Ch. 26). His "way more important than a person could realize" has two standing witnesses, not one.

## Chapter 26 — The Prescription Latin: The Compounding Side

His account (§122): he studied Latin and Roman medicine on **both** sides — the physician side and the medical compounding side. The compounding side is this chapter: the Latin that never died, written daily on every prescription pad [D]:

- ***Rx*** — *recipe*: "take thou" — the imperative, commanding the pharmacist [D].
- ***Sig*** — *signa*: "write, label" — the directions for the patient [D].
- ***bid*** — *bis in die*: "twice in a day" [D]. ***tid*** — *ter in die*: three times [D]. ***qid*** — *quater in die*: four times [D].
- ***po*** — *per os*: "by mouth" [D].
- ***prn*** — *pro re nata*: "for the thing born" — as needed, as the need arises [D]. The most philosophical abbreviation in medicine: the need gives birth to the dose.
- ***stat*** — *statim*: "immediately" [D].
- ***ung*** — *unguentum*: ointment [D]. ***gtt*** — *guttae*: drops [D]. ***ss*** — *semis*: half [D].
- ***ac / pc*** — *ante cibum / post cibum*: before food / after food [D]. ***hs*** — *hora somni*: "at the hour of sleep" — bedtime [D]. The day itself, in Latin: meals and sleep as dosing landmarks.

**The doctrine of the compounding side [H→D]:** this Latin is not decorative — it is *operational*. *Bid* doesn't describe; it instructs. The physician's Latin named the disease; the compounder's Latin moves the drug. Two Latin functions, one language — and the second one never needed defending, because it never stopped working. His thesis stands on the stronger leg here: the Latin that *does* things outlived the Latin that *names* things. The booklet's final argument for the hallway (Ch. 7): the living Latin is the proof the dead Latin can live again — in English, holding and flowing the meaning.

## Chapter 27 — The Numbers in the Names

Some names are numbers. The Latin ran out, and the counting took over [D]:

- **The 23** — PPSV23's twenty-three serotypes: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6B, 7F, 8, 9N, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 17F, 18C, 19A, 19F, 20, 22F, 23F, 33F [D]. Not a name but a roster — his panel, his 88% bar's home field. Over a hundred serotypes exist; the 23 are the 1983 formulation's choice [D]. The number is history, not nature.
- **Types 1–3** — poliovirus types 1, 2, 3 [D]. The number as the whole identity: no shape-word, no color-word, just the count.
- **Type b** — *Haemophilus influenzae* **type b**: the capsule type that made the disease, lettered [D]. The letter did what the Latin couldn't — marked the dangerous one.
- **O157:H7** — *E. coli* O157:H7: O (the lipopolysaccharide antigen) 157, H (the flagellar antigen) 7 [D]. Two numbers, two molecules — the name is a lab result.
- **BCG's strains** — Danish 1331, Tokyo 172-1, Pasteur 1173P2: the vaccine's family tree in numbers [D]. The strain number as provenance.
- **COVID's alphabet** — Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, Omicron: the Greek alphabet drafted as variant names [D]. The dead language, conscripted again — shape-words retired, letter-words enlisted.

**What the numbers mean [H→D]:** the numbers are what the namers use when the words give out — too many serotypes for adjectives, too many variants for metaphors. His 88% bar was built for exactly this territory: where the names are numbers, only the *relationships* carry meaning. The numbers don't describe; they *distinguish*. And distinguishing — telling this one from that one — is where his cousin-ing does its real work. The words unhide the shapes; the numbers await the cousin-ing.

## Builder's Extra (labeled mine)

*His standing rule: every deliverable gets one extra addition of my judgment — the piece needed for completeness — clearly labeled as mine.*

The booklet proves the Latin *describes*. It doesn't yet prove the Latin *predicts* — and prediction is where his 88% cousin-ing either becomes an instrument or remains a lens. The missing experiment, stated as a commission for his clinical-research setting (§156): take fifty binomials, cousin their shape-words at 88%, and score how often the shape-cousins share a showing, a treatment, or a test — against a control set of random pairings. If the shape-words beat chance, the method is an instrument and the booklet is its manual. If they don't, the method stays a lens — still his, still useful for unhiding, but honest about what it is. Either result belongs in the next edition. The words did their part; now the counting does its.
