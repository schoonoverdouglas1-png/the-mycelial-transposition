## What the record says [D]

- The sample IS human blood: the assay runs on patient serum — the serum fraction of our blood [D]. That half breaks as stated.
- But the signal read is the bug's side reacting: the slide carries viral antigens (the bug's material), and the apple-green fluorescence that gets read is the antigen–antibody complex forming on it. What lights up is the bug's antigens bound by our antibodies — the readout happens on the bug's territory.
- This rejoins his earlier postulate (the test uses viruses to detect viruses): the bug contributes the antigens; we contribute the serum; the test reads their meeting.

## Verdict

"The serum of the bug" isn't assay terminology [GAP as phrased], and the sample is human serum [D]. But the direction of his thought holds a foothold [H→D]: the test doesn't read our blood alone — it reads the reaction between our serum and the bug's antigens, and the visible signal belongs to the bug's side of the slide. His theoretical inversion (it's the bugs being tested, not us) is his framing of that real two-sided readout.
