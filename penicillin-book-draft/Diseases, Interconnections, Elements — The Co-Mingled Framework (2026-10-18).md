## LIST 1 — THE DISEASES

### 1a. Tuberculosis, all forms [D], plus his coinage [H]

Documented forms: pulmonary TB (the common form) [D]; miliary/disseminated TB [D]; tuberculous meningitis — the classic TB↔meninges link, via hematogenous spread and Rich foci [D]; spinal TB (Pott disease) [D]; lupus vulgaris — tuberculosis of the skin [D]; renal TB [D]; scrofula — tuberculous lymphadenitis of the neck [D]. His coinage: **"tuberculosis pneumoniae"** [H] — his TB-plus-old-lung-showings pair, the fungal diseases TB had to be told apart from. His construction **"tuberculosis vulgaris"** [H] — no historical record of the term; expanded in his four-track paper.

### 1b. All pneumonias, by kind [D]

**Bacterial:** pneumococcal (*S. pneumoniae*, 23 serotypes) [D]; **Klebsiella pneumoniae** — Gram-negative necrotizing pneumonia, his name-example [D]; *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* — nosocomial/structural-lung, the biofilm organism [D]; *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* — "atypical," no cell wall [D]; *Chlamydophila pneumoniae* — atypical, obligate intracellular [D]; *Legionella pneumophila* — urinary antigen detects serogroup 1 only [D].

**Viral:** influenza [D]; SARS-CoV-2 (with COVID-associated pulmonary aspergillosis as the documented superinfection) [D]; RSV [D].

**Fungal:** *Pneumocystis jirovecii* (PJP) [D]; *Aspergillus* (invasive pulmonary) [D]; pulmonary *Cryptococcus* [D]; endemic dimorphics — *Histoplasma*, *Coccidioides*, *Blastomyces* [D].

### 1c. All meningitides, by kind [D]

**Bacterial:** *S. pneumoniae* and *N. meningitidis* (leading adult causes) [D]; *H. influenzae* [D]; Group B *Streptococcus* and *E. coli* K1 (neonates) [D]; *Listeria monocytogenes* [D].

**Fungal:** *Cryptococcus neoformans/gattii* (leading worldwide cause) [D]; *Candida* spp. [D]; *Aspergillus* spp. [D]; *Histoplasma capsulatum* [D]; *Coccidioides* spp. [D].

**Viral:** enterovirus, HSV-1/2, VZV, parechovirus [D]; the arboviral encephalitides — La Crosse, St. Louis encephalitis, EEE, WEE (his arbopanel's four) [D].

**Tuberculous meningitis** [D] — filed in 1a and here both; the disease refuses the categories, which is the point.

### 1d. Encephalitis — and the bridge word [D]

Encephalitis (brain parenchyma) and meningitis (coverings) are documented as distinct, but the record's own compound word — **meningoencephalitis** — refuses to keep them fully separate [D]. His thesis: they are "literally cousins," direct connections from the same sicknesses [H] — the documented floor is that two different organisms (a mycobacterium, a pneumococcus) both reach the same coverings of the same brain [D].

### 1e. The element entities — bacterial, yeast, fungal

- **Bacterial element:** *bacteriosis* — a real dictionary word, "any bacterial disease" [D]; bacteremia — bacteria in the blood [D]. His element split: bacteremia and pneumonia are both the bacterial element [H].
- **Yeast entities:** candidiasis (mucosal, esophageal, bloodstream) [D]; cryptococcosis — a yeast that causes meningitis [D]; PJP — taxonomically a fungus, kept in his yeast-adjacent thinking per his three-group tracks [H for the grouping]. Yeast stays its own column even though documented taxonomy counts it as a fungus — his grouping stands as his [H].
- **Fungal element:** *mycosis* — any fungal disease [D]. His element split: toxemia and meningitis are both the fungal element [H].

## LIST 2 — THE INTERCONNECTIONS, AS THEIR OWN LIST

### Kind (a) — NAME-commonalities: the words that connect

1. **"Pneumonia" as the common place** [H — his example, filed as his]: *Klebsiella pneumonia*, pneumococcal pneumonia, *Pseudomonas* pneumonia — different organisms, one shared word marking the shared showing. The name is the meeting point.
2. **The -itis/-osis morphology** [D]: *-itis* = inflammation, *-osis*/*-iasis* = condition — genuinely the fungal suffix across the documented rows (candidiasis, aspergillosis, coccidioidomycosis). His universal postulate — every -itis has an -osis counterpart — tested 25 diseases: 14 documented parallels, 4 thin, 7 gaps [H, tested].
3. **The *vulgaris* tag** [D]: Latin for "common" — the same labeling habit across acne vulgaris, psoriasis vulgaris, pemphigus vulgaris, and **lupus vulgaris = tuberculosis of the skin** [D]. Old medicine's shared shelf.
4. **The SLE/SLE collision** [D]: his arbopanel lists "SLE" as St. Louis encephalitis; "SLE" is also systemic lupus erythematosus. One acronym, two diseases — a genuine namology catch.
5. **auris vs. aureus** [D]: *auris* = Latin *ear*; *aureus* = Latin *golden* — two different words wearing one sound. His additive-naming thesis: renaming adds an alias [H].
6. **"Arbovirus" as a functional glob** [D]: not a taxonomy but a transmission habit (arthropod-borne) — La Crosse (Orthobunyavirus), EEE/WEE (Alphavirus), SLE (Flavivirus) share nothing taxonomically with each other or with pneumococcus; the documented meeting point is the syndrome only.
7. **Meningoencephalitis** [D]: the compound word as the record's own admission that the categories touch.

### Kind (b) — ORGANISM overlaps: the bodies that connect

1. **Pneumococcus: pneumonia ↔ meningitis** [D]: bacteremia from the lung seeds the meninges — the same organism, two syndromes, one bloodstream between them.
2. **Cryptococcus: lung → meninges** [D]: the lung is the portal, the meninges the destination — pulmonary cryptococcosis and cryptococcal meningitis are one organism's two stations.
3. **TB: lung → meninges** [D]: primary pulmonary infection → bacillemia → Rich foci → rupture into the subarachnoid space. Same destination as pneumo, different road.
4. **Aspergillus colonizing healed TB cavities** [D]: the aspergilloma ("fungus ball") grows in the scar TB left — the bacterial disease builds the house the fungus moves into.
5. **Histoplasma mimicking TB** [D]: clinically and radiographically confusable in endemic regions — a documented diagnostic confounder.
6. **Influenza → pneumococcal superinfection** [D]: three documented mechanisms (PspA adhesion to dead lung cells; type-I interferon sabotaging neutrophil clearance; pneumococcal NanA/NanB supporting viral replication). The virus opens the door; the bacterium walks through.
7. **Candida: mucosa → blood → heart** [D]: candidemia seeding endocarditis — the yeast traveling the bacterial element's own road (the bloodstream).
8. **COVID-associated pulmonary aspergillosis** [D]: the documented viral→fungal bridge of the SARS-CoV-2 era.

### Kind (c) — TEST overlaps: the instruments that connect

From his test-map workup, reused not re-derived:

1. **CSF profile** [D]: one test whose pattern separates bacterial (neutrophils, low glucose) from viral (lymphocytes, normal glucose) from fungal/TB (lymphocytes, low glucose, high opening pressure) — a single instrument touching all three meningitis kinds plus TB.
2. **BioFire ME panel** [D]: 14 targets in one run — 6 bacteria, 7 viruses, 1 yeast (*Cryptococcus neoformans/gattii*). One test spanning two elements and the viral markers at once. Limitation built in: *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *Histoplasma* meningitis are outside it [D].
3. **CrAg** [D]: serum or CSF, lung or meninges — one shed capsular polysaccharide, two syndromes. Antigen direction (finds the organism's pieces), opposite from antibody panels.
4. **β-D-glucan** [D]: positive across PJP, *Candida*, *Aspergillus* — negative in *Cryptococcus* and Mucorales. One test whose negative is as informative as its positive: the BG−/CrAg+ fork vs. the BG+/CrAg− fork.
5. **Pneumococcal urinary antigen** [D]: detects all serotypes (C-polysaccharide), not serotype-specific; stays positive for weeks — the name says the organism, the positive can be an echo.
6. **His "no more panel" doctrine** [H]: test names should be thought about AND ignored both — thought about (the name carries the namer's history), ignored (the name must not constrain what the result can mean). Documented floor: IgG = memory not infection; antigen persists weeks; PCR can't separate colonization from infection [D].

### Kind (d) — SYNDROME overlaps: the showings that connect

1. **Meningoencephalitis** [D]: the compound syndrome — both meninges and parenchyma inflamed at once.
2. **His lupus-symptomology thesis** [H]: Lyme disease and California (La Crosse) encephalitis symptomology = systemic lupus symptomology — filed as his, his to evidence.
3. **The worldwide note** [H]: disease exists worldwide and the bacteria exists worldwide — the organism is not confined to its name-place; filed as his standing note against geographic naming.

### F3. His yeast thesis [H]

**Yeasts are dealt with by dealing with the other two — with antibiotics and antifungals.** Filed as his, verbatim in substance.

Honest lines, kept beside it: in the documented literature, yeast infections (candidiasis, cryptococcosis) are treated with antifungals [D] — antibiotics do not treat yeast in the record. His framework's claim is that the yeast element's ground is covered by the bacterial-element and fungal-element sets acting together. The three-group tracks (fungi, yeast, bacteria kept separate) are his grouping [H]. The thesis is his to evidence; the seam is marked.

His statements, filed: **antibacterials exist as well** ("back" in dictation — read as backup/reserve); **it is not impossible that those might have to be utilized**; **most of those are salts.**

Verification, done lightly [H→D]: the salts claim holds.
