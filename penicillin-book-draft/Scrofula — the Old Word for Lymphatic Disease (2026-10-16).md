# Scrofula ("Skrulla") — the Old Word for Lymphatic Disease (2026-10-16)

*Compiled at Douglas's direction. Naming reference for the book — never medical advice.*

## The word

**Scrofula** — medieval Latin *scrōfula*, diminutive of *scrōfa*, "brood sow." Two stories for why: swine were supposed to be subject to the complaint, or the line of swollen neck glands was thought to resemble the belly of a nursing sow. The 1911 Britannica gives it as "Lat. for 'little sow'." It was also called **struma** — and "scrofulous," "strumous," and "tuberculous" were once nearly interchangeable words.

## What it is

Tuberculosis of the **neck lymph nodes** — tuberculous cervical lymphadenitis. About 95% of adult mycobacterial neck infections are *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. (In children the pattern flips: ~92% are nontuberculous mycobacteria.) For centuries it was believed to be a **different disease** from lung consumption — the neck lumps and the wasting lung were given separate names until Koch found the bacillus in scrofulous nodes in 1882.

## The king's evil

Because medieval England and France believed a sovereign's touch could cure it, scrofula was called the **king's evil**. Edward the Confessor was the first English king credited; St. Louis (Louis IX) in France. From 1633 the Anglican Book of Common Prayer carried a ceremony for it, and the monarch gave each touched sufferer a gold "angel" coin — a touchpiece on a silk ribbon. Queen Anne touched the infant Samuel Johnson in 1712. England dropped the rite in the early 1700s; France's kings continued until Louis XV stopped it (briefly revived 1825).

## The associated diseases — lymphatic and otherwise

| Name | Association |
|---|---|
| Tabes mesenterica | TB of the **mesenteric (abdominal) lymph nodes** — scrofula's inland cousin; the old *tabes* (wasting) again |
| Lymphadenitis (general) | any inflamed lymph node — the sign without the TB name |
| Bubonic plague buboes | swollen lymph nodes from a **different** disease — same swelling, different cause; old medicine read the lump, not the microbe |
| Lymphoma / Hodgkin's disease | cancer of the same territory — different category entirely, same neck lumps |
| NTM cervical adenitis | nontuberculous mycobacteria doing scrofula's job in children |

## Namology note for the book

The lymph node is a crossroads: TB, plague, cancer, and ordinary infection all announce themselves in the same glands. Old medicine named the **swelling** (sow-like lumps), the **site** (neck), and even the **cure** (the king's touch) — everything except the cause. The cause got its name in 1882. Scrofula is the purest case of his doctrine's warning: the names described the theater faithfully and the play wrongly, for five hundred years.
