## The naming-dates check — Theorem [D]

His claim was that all three were named before WWII. The record confirms it, with room to spare:

- **Aspergillus** — genus named by Pier Antonio Micheli in **1729** (after the aspergillum, the holy-water sprinkler). First human pulmonary case described by Schönlein in **1842**; Virchow's foundational paper in **1856** (the year he also coined "mycosis"). *Sources: diseasemaps.org history; Wainwright 2008 via ResearchGate; Popular Science Monthly 1882 (Virchow essay record).*
- ***Streptococcus pneumoniae*** — isolated independently by Pasteur and Sternberg in **1881**; named "pneumococcus" by Fraenkel in **1886**; *Diplococcus pneumoniae* 1920; *Streptococcus pneumoniae* 1974. *Sources: PMC11332154 (historical review); Wikipedia pneumococcus history; Robert Austrian historical lecture (NLM).*
- ***Mycobacterium tuberculosis*** — Koch announced the tubercle bacillus on **March 24, 1882**, before the Physiological Society of Berlin. *Source: victorianweb.org bacteriology chronology; Koch's "Die Aetiologie der Tuberculose," 1882.*

**Theorem [D]:** all three triad members were named and described before WWII — the oldest (Aspergillus) more than two centuries before it, the youngest (the tubercle bacillus) fifty-seven years before it. His "named before World War Two" is not approximate; it is comfortably true.

## Pairwise tested connections

### Edge 1 — TB → Aspergillus: the cavity is the house [D]

The strongest edge in the tree. Tested, quantified, and replicated across six decades:

- Residual cavities persist in **20–40%** of lungs after treated pulmonary TB. Cavitation lets inhaled *Aspergillus* spores colonize saprophytically; a fungal ball (aspergilloma) — hyphae, tissue debris, inflammatory cells, mucin — may form in the cavity. *Source: PLoS Pathogens review (journals.PLOS.org, "Post-tuberculosis chronic pulmonary aspergillosis: An emerging public health concern").*
- The landmark 1960s British cohort: **544 patients** with persistent cavities ≥2.5 cm and TB-negative sputum for ≥1 year — at one year, **14%** had aspergillomas and 25% had Aspergillus precipitins; at three years, **22%** had aspergillomas and 34% had precipitins. *Source: same PLoS Pathogens review, citing the mid-to-late-1960s multi-clinic study.*
- Modern studies: CPA prevalence **6.3–13.7%** among TB patients; treated TB is the underlying condition in **17–93%** of all CPA cases. A 2025 systematic review (22 studies, 2,884 patients): CPA in **9%** during TB treatment, **13%** post-treatment — rising to **48%** in patients with persistent post-treatment symptoms. *Sources: PLoS Pathogens review; JoVE systematic review (2025).*
- Global burden: Denning 2011 modeling — **852,000–1,372,000** develop CPA within five years of a TB diagnosis; ~**1.2 million** prevalent cases attributable to previously treated TB. India revision: 5-year prevalence **1,575,716**. StatPearls: ~**10%** of preexisting tuberculous cavities develop aspergillomas. *Sources: Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology 2025; StatPearls aspergilloma.*
- **Plain language:** TB hollows the lung; *Aspergillus* moves into the hollow. This is structural, not speculative — the fungus colonizes the architecture TB left behind.

### Edge 2 — *S. pneumoniae* ↔ *M. tuberculosis*: co-infection, both directions [D]

Tested in trials, cohorts, and case literature:

- **The vaccine-probe trial:** in a phase III PCV9 trial in South Africa (39,836 children), hospitalization for culture-confirmed pulmonary TB was **43.4% less likely** among pneumococcal vaccinees than placebo recipients — suggesting that much childhood PTB presenting as acute pneumonia carries superimposed pneumococcal infection. *"Children admitted with pneumonia in these settings should be investigated for underlying PTB."* *Source: PubMed 21155174 (Madhi et al.).*
- **Prevalence in TB clinics:** 21.4% *S. pneumoniae* carriage/infection among 422 presumptive adult TB cases in Ethiopia (Dilla University, 2021); co-infection confirmed among HIV patients on HAART in Nigeria (2.7% dual-positive). *Sources: Bohrium/Dilla study; ClinMed Journals (IJVA).*
- **Both directions hypothesized:** the first reported non-HIV case of CAP with pneumo–TB co-infection (PMC6748207) lays out both arrows — pneumococcal pneumonia may provide "fertile ground" for latent TB to reactivate; *or* subclinical TB with impaired lung architecture may invite aggressive pneumococcal infection. The authors recommend testing CAP patients for TB even when another diagnosis is found, given TB-endemic exposure or upper-lobe lesions. *Source: PMC6748207.*
- A 2025 case (PMC12163197, on file): TB–pneumo–staph triple presentation in an immunocompetent child.
- **Plain language:** these two bacteria share the lung, the patient population, and the clinical presentation — and each can open the door for the other. Which door opened first is case-by-case; that the doors connect is settled.

### Edge 3 — *S. pneumoniae* ↔ *Aspergillus*: chemical warfare, both directions [D — in vitro]

The most mechanistic edge — tested in the lab, both arrows:

- **Pneumococcus attacks the fungus:** *S. pneumoniae* suppresses and fragments preformed *A. fumigatus* biofilms via **hydrogen peroxide** (the same H2O2 weapon it uses against *S. aureus*) — the mycelial network breaks into fine filaments and the fungal extracellular matrix disappears. *Source: Kurume University 2020 (on file in "Serotype–Aspergillus Connections").*
- **The fungus attacks back:** *A. fumigatus* **gliotoxin** inhibits planktonic *S. pneumoniae* growth and biofilms — and synergizes with cefotaxime and vancomycin. The fungus makes its own antibiotic. *Source: Llorente et al., Journal of Fungi (on file).*
- **Clinical co-occurrence:** one documented case — selective IgM deficiency (low anti-pneumococcal titers to all 14 tested serotypes), presenting with pneumococcal septic shock **and** invasive *A. fumigatus* infection. Shared immune gap, not a proven interaction. *Source: Hong & Gupta 2008, J. Investig. Allergol. Clin. Immunol. (on file).*
- **Plain language:** these two are not just co-located; they are chemically at war. The bacterium shreds fungal biofilm with peroxide; the fungus kills the bacterium with its own toxin — a toxin that *boosts* conventional antibiotics.
- **Honest boundary:** the warfare is demonstrated in vitro. The biofilm-study authors themselves warn that simultaneous isolation from sputum does not prove the two share the same part of the airway. No per-serotype Aspergillus link exists for any of the 23 serotypes — the literature names *S. pneumoniae* as a species only. **[GAP — his to fill]**

What is *his* is the thesis that **almost all things connect back to this triangle** — that claim is the expansion below, and the gaps section marks where the record ends.

## The simplest connection

The shortest documented path linking all three nodes runs through **one damaged lung**:

> **TB cavitates the lung → the cavity admits *Aspergillus* (Edge 1) → the damaged architecture admits pneumococcal superinfection (Edge 2).**

Two edges, three organisms, one building. The British 1960s cohort gives it numbers (22% aspergillomas at three years); the vaccine-probe trial gives it the pneumo arrow (43.4% fewer TB hospitalizations with pneumococcal vaccine). Nothing in this path is inferred — every step is measured. This is the 88%-bar connection at its simplest: **three pillars, one ruined architecture.**
