## Record B: The arbovirus panels — two panels, six weeks apart

Both panels hold exactly **8 analytes**: California (La Crosse), Eastern Equine, St. Louis, Western Equine × IgG/IgM [D]. Panel mechanics [D]: **2-stage indirect IFA** (Focus Diagnostics kit) — first stage, patient serum diluted and added to slide wells carrying the viral substrate, incubated, washed; second stage, fluorescein-labeled anti-IgM and anti-IgG overlaid, incubated, washed, and read by fluorescence microscopy, where positive reactions appear as cells showing bright apple-green cytoplasmic fluorescence against a background of red negative control cells; semi-quantitative endpoint titers by serial dilution. Controls = control cells + kit reference sera [D]. Reference range on every analyte: **<1:10** [D]. Timing, stated generally [D]: IgM is reliably detected within 1 to 3 weeks of onset, peaking and declining rapidly within 3 months; IgG is detectable within 1 to 3 weeks, peaking within 1 to 2 months and declining slowly; a single-serum IgG of 1:10 or greater indicates exposure; a 4-fold or greater rise between acute and convalescent sera indicates recent infection. **No coronavirus on either panel** [D]. Unusual, documented [D]: in the United States it is unusual for any patient to show positive reactions to more than one of the arboviral antigens — a fact that sits beside his top/bottom pattern without explaining it.

### The control and the zero-point (why Panel 2 matters more than Panel 1 for his method)

A control is what an experiment is measured against: the run in which the thing being tested is absent, so that any signal in the real run can be judged against silence [D]. His Panel 2 is fully negative on all eight analytes — the silence [D]. His method reads Panel 1 against that silence: one analyte speaking (California (La Crosse) IgG 1:20) where the control says nothing [H].
