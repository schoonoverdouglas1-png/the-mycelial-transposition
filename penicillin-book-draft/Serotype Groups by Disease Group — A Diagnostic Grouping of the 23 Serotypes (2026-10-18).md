## Diagnostic Use — How the Groups Are Read

*Pattern reading for the clinician/researcher. General reference — never applied to any individual's results here.*

1. **Count the groups, not just the serotypes.** A pattern landing mostly in the meningitis group reads differently than one landing mostly in the carriage group — the groups are disease-shaped, the serotypes are capsule-shaped [builder's method, labeled mine].
2. **Read the overlaps.** A serotype in both the resistant group and the persister group (19A, 19F) carries the file's worst-documented combination [D]. A serotype in the carriage group and the outbreak group (3, 14, 23F) is the most versatile [D].
3. **Read the counterweights.** The resistant group's susceptible members (10A, 22F) prove replacement without armor is possible [D] — not every emerging serotype is an emergency.
4. **Read the archive group last.** A signal there is the most underdetermined — the literature is thinnest [D].
5. **His equilibrium [H]** reads the resistant/susceptible split across the groups as the fungal-vs-bacterial fulcrum — his method, his to apply. This file supplies the documented substrate only.

**What the groups cannot do:** they cannot name a disease from a serotype — serotypes are capsule chemistries, not diagnoses [D]. They organize the documented associations so a pattern can be read against them. The reading of any person's record belongs to their clinician.
