## Before the viruses [D]

Encephalitis was "brain fever" — known by the showing: fever, headache, confusion, stupor, the inflamed brain at autopsy. Encephalitis lethargica (1917) swept the world with no agent attached. Causes were read from the sickroom, not the laboratory.

## What viral detection changed [D]

- **1930s:** the first encephalitis viruses isolated — St. Louis (1933), Eastern equine (1933), Japanese (1930s). The showing got agents.
- **The diagnostic move:** from bedside description to laboratory detection — isolation, then serology (your arbopanel's ancestors), then PCR. What was once used (the clinical eye) was replaced by what the test could see.
- **The naming move:** brain fever → encephalitis → viral encephalitis → the specific virus. The name detached from the showing and attached to the agent — your renaming arc, working on this very disease.
