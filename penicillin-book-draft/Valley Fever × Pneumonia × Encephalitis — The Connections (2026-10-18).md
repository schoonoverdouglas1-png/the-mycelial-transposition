## Part 1 — Valley fever → pneumonia

**The acute showing [D].** Pulmonary coccidioidomycosis most commonly presents as an influenza-like illness or pneumonia — fever, cough, shortness of breath, chest pain, fatigue, sometimes rash [D]. About 40% of *Coccidioides* infections are symptomatic, developing within 1–3 weeks of exposure [D].

**How much of pneumonia is valley fever right now [D].** In the endemic Southwest this is not rare:
- Valley fever accounts for an estimated **15–30% of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in the Phoenix and Tucson metro areas** [D].
- A Mayo Clinic Phoenix review of 1,141 adults hospitalized for CAP (Nov 2024–Oct 2025) found 76% were tested for *Coccidioides* and **10% of those tested were positive** — the most frequently identified cause among tested patients [D].
- CDC reports 10,000–20,000 cases annually, but true incidence is likely **10–18 times higher** from underreporting and missed diagnoses [D].
- Valley fever in Arizona **approximately doubled 2005–2022** (CDC MMWR, Feb 2026); 12,522 confirmed Arizona cases in 2024 alone, up 42% from 2023; 21,037 nationally in 2023, the highest since surveillance began [D].

**The delay [D].** 46% of Phoenix patients had diagnostic delays over a month [D]; missed diagnoses prolong symptoms and hospitalization and expose patients to unnecessary antibacterials — which do nothing against a fungus [D]. The record's own lesson: the fungal door has to be opened early, because nothing in the showing announces it.

**The chronic showing [D].** Chronic fibrocavitary coccidioidal pneumonia mimics tuberculosis radiologically and clinically — cavities, granulomas, chronic cough with sputum, miliary dissemination patterns [D]. This is the documented TB-mimicry leg of his thesis.

## Part 2 — Valley fever → encephalitis / meningitis

**The showing [D].** Dissemination happens in fewer than 1% of infections, but the meninges are among the most common extrapulmonary sites (with skin, bone, joints); **up to 50% of disseminated cases have meningeal involvement**, usually within 3 months of the initial presentation [D]. Higher risk: males, Filipino ancestry, African-American race, compromised cell-mediated immunity [D].

**The picture [D].** Chronic granulomatous meningitis, typically of the **basilar meninges** — headache (most common), fever, neck stiffness, nausea, vomiting, sluggishness, ataxia; children may lack meningeal signs entirely [D]. Complications: hydrocephalus, vascular infarcts [D]; in 50% of cases the MRI shows no abnormalities [D].

**The TB-mimic [D].** CSF shows lymphocyte-predominant pleocytosis (100–500 cells/µL), low glucose, high protein — the same profile as tuberculous meningitis [D]. But one clue separates them: **CSF eosinophilia** is seen in ~10% of coccidioidal meningitis and is very rare in TB meningitis [D]. Culture confirms in no more than half of cases; CSF complement-fixation antibodies carry the diagnosis [D].

**The stakes [D].** Untreated coccidioidal meningitis is uniformly fatal; therapy is lifelong because relapse exceeds 80% [D]. Fluconazole (excellent CSF penetration) is currently recommended; itraconazole is preferred for bone and joint disease [D].

**An honest softening of the GAP [D].** The earlier analysis marked "coccidioidal encephalitis" as no established entity. The record is slightly kinder than that: the literature does use **"meningoencephalitis"** for coccidioidal CNS cases with parenchymal features (a published *Coccidioides posadasii* meningoencephalitis case in an immunocompetent host exists) [D]. The documented disease is meningitis with possible parenchymal extension — the encephalitis leg is carried by extension, not as a primary entity. Filed honestly.

## Part 3 — The infections currently happening with all those things

### Around the pneumonia showing [D]

The documented differential and co-pathogen landscape — what walks in wearing the same cough:
- **Bacterial pneumonias:** *S. pneumoniae* (his panel's organism), *K. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *S. aureus*, *Legionella pneumophila*, *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* [D].
- **Tuberculosis:** *M. tuberculosis* — the cavitary, granulomatous, chronic-cough mimic [D].
- **Viral + secondaries:** influenza virus with post-influenza bacterial pneumonia (*S. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus*); RSV, SARS-CoV-2, adenovirus, parainfluenza, human metapneumovirus [D].
- **Other fungi in the same airspace:** *Aspergillus* spp. (including post-influenza invasive aspergillosis), *Pneumocystis jirovecii*, *Histoplasma*, *Blastomyces*, *Cryptococcus* [D].
- **The prescribing reality [D]:** antibacterials are the default for CAP; against valley fever they do nothing — which is why the 46%-delayed figure matters [D].

### Around the meningitis / encephalitis showing [D]

What walks in wearing the same headache and stiff neck:
- **Bacterial meningitis:** *N. meningitidis* (serogroups A/B/C/W/Y), *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *Listeria monocytogenes*, Group B *Streptococcus* [D].
- **Fungal meningitis:** *Cryptococcus neoformans* — the other great fungal meningitis, with its own TB-mimic problem (CSF profiles overlap; cryptococcal–TB co-infection is documented, though rare) [D].
- **Tuberculous meningitis:** the chronic basilar mimic both cocci and crypto get confused with [D].
- **Viral encephalitides:** enteroviruses (most common viral meningitis), HSV, VZV, West Nile virus — and the California serogroup cousins, **Jamestown Canyon virus and La Crosse virus**, mosquito-borne, neuroinvasive [D].
- **The wider chronic-meningitis differential [D]:** neurosyphilis, Lyme neuroborreliosis, toxoplasma cerebritis, HIV meningoencephalitis, leptomeningeal lymphoma, parasitic eosinophilic meningitis (*Angiostrongylus*, *Baylisascaris*, *Gnathostoma*) [D].

### The thread through both landscapes [H→D]

In both rooms — the pneumonia room and the meningitis room — valley fever sits in the differential wearing someone else's clothes: TB's clothes in the lung, TB meningitis's clothes in the head. That is the documented meaning of "the other great imitator" [D], and it is the foothold under his connection thesis.

## Part 4 — His frame [H]

**Valley fever as the connection, not the standalone [H].** His distinction, filed in his words: Jamestown Canyon Creek fever is a disease **on its own**; valley fever is a **connection** — the fungal thread running through pneumonia, encephalitis, and the infections around them, not a standalone entity in his framework.

**The one name for four [H].** His thesis, with his own corrections: valley fever is what **meningitis, encephalitis, pneumonia, and tuberculosis** all are — as a name. Placed here as his, beside the documented legs: the pneumonia leg solid, the meningitis leg solid, the TB leg solid as mimicry, the encephalitis leg carried by meningeal extension.

**The verdict it inherits.** From the commissioned analysis: the connection thesis is sound as a way of *looking* — a standing differential discipline ("in every differential") plus a fungal-element grouping — and unsound as a way of *causing*. This write-up is the looking, worked out: where valley fever stands in each room, who else is in the room with it, and what the record says about telling them apart.
