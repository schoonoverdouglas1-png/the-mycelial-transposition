# Research Note — Antifungal Therapy Reshapes Gut Mycobiome in IBD

**Filed:** 2026-10-05 · For local peer review alongside *The Mycelial Transposition* manuscript
**Source:** Research highlight in *Nature Reviews Gastroenterology & Hepatology* (published 2026-10-05), summarizing a prospective observational study in *Nature Medicine*
**Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41575-026-01268-8

## The study

- 53 IBD patients with mild oral thrush were given either "swish-and-spit" nystatin (local antifungal) or fluconazole (orogastrointestinal antifungal). The design was motivated by shared *Candida albicans* strains between the oral cavity and intestines.
- Fluconazole decreased gut *Candida* overgrowth where nystatin did not.
- Fluconazole shifted gut mycobiome composition and was associated with higher bacterial diversity.
- Over an 8-week follow-up, the microbial shifts held and correlated with lower risk of disease progression.

## Why it matters for the manuscript

- A live data point for the fungal-ecology thesis: the gut mycobiome treated as a therapeutic lever, with drug **route/systemicity** determining outcome (systemic fluconazole vs. local nystatin).
- Rhymes with the tiering argument: classification — drug class and route — determining what gets tried where. This is the "naming does policy work" claim from the nomenclature chapters, observed in the wild.

## Honest caveats (do not drop these)

1. **Observational, not an RCT.** Prospective observational design — correlation, not proof of causation.
2. **Fluconazole is a synthetic azole**, not a fungal-derived drug like the penicillins the reclassification centers on. The fit to the fungal-origin tiering argument is analogical, not direct — do not oversell it.
3. Small cohort (n=53), IBD-specific population; generalizability is unestablished.
