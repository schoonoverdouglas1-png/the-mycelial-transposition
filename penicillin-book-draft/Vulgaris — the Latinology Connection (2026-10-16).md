## What "vulgaris" actually does

*Vulgaris* is Latin for **"common, ordinary"** — the taxonomist's way of marking the typical form of a thing. The same word does the same job across unrelated diseases. That is the real Latinology connection: not a shared mechanism, but a shared *labeling habit* from old medicine. The book can claim the pattern honestly — the word is doing identical work in all six names.

Old-medicine gem, verified: an 1874 Royal College of Surgeons dermatology lecture series lists psoriasis as **"lepra vulgaris"** — the old name wearing the same *vulgaris* tag.

## Two anchors for his book

1. **Lupus vulgaris is tuberculosis of the skin.** The vulgaris pattern reaches straight into his TB pillar — old medicine filed skin TB under the same "common" tag as acne and warts.
2. **Pemphigus vulgaris is genuinely immunoglobulin-driven.** It is caused by IgG autoantibodies against desmoglein (the skin's cell-to-cell glue); direct immunofluorescence — the test that lights up those antibodies in the skin — is how it's diagnosed. Of the whole vulgaris family, this is the one where his immunoglobulin thesis has a documented foothold.

## Honest line for the manuscript

The *word* marks commonness, not a shared cause — acne, warts, and skin TB are not one disease. His interconnection thesis (immunoglobulins + inclusions) is his to argue, and the book is strongest if it states the naming fact first and then argues the mechanism, rather than implying the name proves the mechanism. Pemphigus vulgaris is the beachhead: start the argument there, where the antibodies are on record.
