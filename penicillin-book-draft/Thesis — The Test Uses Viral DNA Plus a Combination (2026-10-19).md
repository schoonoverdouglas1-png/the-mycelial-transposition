# Thesis — The Test Uses Viral DNA Plus a Combination; Virology Doesn't Grasp Its Own Full Usage (2026-10-19)

**Status:** filed as his, tested against the record.
**Frame:** *The Mycelial Transposition* — educational manuscript reference only. Never medical advice.

## His thesis [H]

The test actually uses viral DNA to test against each of the encephalitises — which means they're using more than just one thing. They're actually using the combination of special aspergillosis from each one of those strains of encephalitis to test against. And that's part of where virology isn't understanding: people aren't understanding the full usage of what virology truly is.

("Special aspergillosis" is his phrase as dictated — my reading: a special fungal/aspergillus component combined in the test material. His to correct if misread.)

## Tested piece by piece

1. **"Uses viral DNA"** — doesn't hold for his panel. The Mayo arbovirus panel is indirect immunofluorescence: viral antigens on slides + patient serum, detecting antibodies. No DNA detection involved [D]. (PCR panels do use viral nucleic acid — but his panel isn't one.)
2. **"More than just one thing"** — holds. Eight analytes (four viruses × IgG/IgM), plus control cells and antigen substrates on every slide [D].
3. **"Combination of special aspergillosis from each strain"** — no documented basis in the assay [GAP]. The kit's antigen substrate is virus-infected cell culture — a combination of viral and cellular material, not purified virus alone [D]. That is the nearest honest neighbor to his "combination" claim: the test was never just one pure thing.
4. **"Virology doesn't understand its own full usage"** — his meta-claim [H], filed as his. Untouched.

## Verdict

The DNA half breaks (serology, not nucleic acid); the combination half holds (multi-analyte, multi-component by design); the aspergillosis component has no assay record and stands as his [GAP]. The infected-cell substrate — viral plus cellular material combined — is the documented thing nearest his combination claim.
