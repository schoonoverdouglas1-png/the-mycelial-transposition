The textbooks split this across filtration, excretion, immune clearance, and detoxification; his framing unifies it under one image. ### The TB equation [H]

Read as a detection system, the four pillars stop being categories [H]: pneumonia and TB at the ends, encephalitis/encephalopathy between, serology as the light. His quantum-medicine structure doing diagnostic work.

## Chapter 7 — The Pre-WWII Triad

### The naming-dates check — Theorem [D]

His claim was that all three were named before WWII. The record confirms it, with room to spare:

All three triad members were named and described before WWII — the oldest more than two centuries before it, the youngest fifty-seven years before it. His "named before World War Two" is not approximate; it is comfortably true.

### The three edges — all drawn in the literature

**Edge 1 — TB → Aspergillus: the cavity is the house** [D]. The strongest edge. Residual cavities persist in 20–40% of lungs after treated pulmonary TB; inhaled *Aspergillus* spores colonize the cavity, and a fungal ball (aspergilloma) may form. The 1960s British cohort: 544 patients with persistent cavities — at three years, 22% had aspergillomas. Modern studies: chronic pulmonary aspergillosis in 9–13% of TB patients, up to 48% in patients with persistent post-treatment symptoms; treated TB is the underlying condition in 17–93% of all CPA cases. Plain language: TB hollows the lung; *Aspergillus* moves into the hollow. Structural, not speculative — the fungus colonizes the architecture TB left behind.

**Edge 2 — *S. pneumoniae* ↔ *M. tuberculosis*: co-infection, both directions** [D]. The vaccine-probe trial: in a phase III PCV9 trial in South Africa (39,836 children), hospitalization for culture-confirmed pulmonary TB was 43.4% less likely among pneumococcal vaccinees than placebo — suggesting much childhood PTB presenting as acute pneumonia carries superimposed pneumococcal infection. Both directions hypothesized: pneumococcal pneumonia may provide "fertile ground" for latent TB to reactivate; or subclinical TB with impaired lung architecture may invite aggressive pneumococcal infection. Plain language: these two bacteria share the lung, the patient population, and the clinical presentation — and each can open the door for the other. Which door opened first is case-by-case; that the doors connect is settled.

**Edge 3 — *S. pneumoniae* ↔ *Aspergillus*: chemical warfare, both directions** [D — in vitro]. The most mechanistic edge. Pneumococcus attacks the fungus: *S. pneumoniae* suppresses and fragments preformed *A. fumigatus* biofilms via hydrogen peroxide — the mycelial network breaks into fine filaments and the fungal extracellular matrix disappears. The fungus attacks back: *A. fumigatus* gliotoxin inhibits pneumococcal growth and biofilms — and synergizes with cefotaxime and vancomycin. The fungus makes its own antibiotic. Honest boundary: the warfare is demonstrated in vitro; no per-serotype Aspergillus link exists for any of the 23 serotypes — the literature names *S. pneumoniae* as a species only [GAP — his to fill].

## Chapter 15 — The Twenty-Three

### What a serotype is

*Streptococcus pneumoniae* wears a coat [D]. The coat is made of sugar — a capsule of polysaccharide — and no two coats are chemically alike [D]. A **serotype** is a coat number: the bacterium is sorted by the shape and chemistry of the sugar it wears [D]. The Danish numbering system named them in discovery order, which is why the numbers carry no biology in them — 19A and 19F are cousins by discovery, not by behavior [D].

This matters because the immune system learns coats: the vaccines teach them, and the panel in later chapters measures what it remembers [D]. Everything below is about the coats — what they look like, which the vaccines taught, which carry resistance, and which the literature has studied.

### The 23 in narrative groups

Over one hundred serotypes are known [D]. His panel — Mayo's 23-serotype IgG panel — tests exactly the 23 coats inside the 1983 polysaccharide vaccine, PPSV23 [D]. Here they are in groups, the way they actually behave.

**The invasive specialists [D].** Serotypes **1** and **5** cause epidemics. Serotype 1 drives meningitis outbreaks in the African meningitis belt with the seasonality and clonal sweep of a meningococcal epidemic [D — northern Ghana outbreak]. Serotypes 1 and 5 together caused more than 30% of invasive disease across all ages in The Gambia [D — PMC2587476]. Both are rarely found in carriage studies — they show up in disease far more than in noses, the signature of high invasive potential [D]. Serotype 5 also travels in crowded settings (camps, prisons, shelters) alongside 1 and 12F [D]. Serotype **4** is highly invasive [D]; **7F** highly invasive, rarely carried, epidemic pattern [D].

**The carriage specialists [D].** Serotypes **6B**, **19F**, and **23F** live in the nose — the great colonists, carriage first, disease second — and they dominated otitis media before the conjugate vaccines arrived [D]. They are also three of the great resisters: 6B the classic penicillin-resistant/MDR serotype (clone Spain6B-2), 19F at 42% MDR in one U.S. adult study, 23F the classic resistant colonizer (clone Spain23F-1) [D]. The carriage specialists are where resistance lived first.

**The resistant set [D].** Six serotypes are the classic resistant set: **6B, 9V, 14, 19A, 19F, 23F** [D]. Serotype **14** is classic MDR — clone CC230 — and a top meningitis serotype [D]. Serotype **19A** is the replacement-disease serotype: not in PCV7, it emerged and expanded after PCV7 cleared its seven, becoming the predominant post-PCV7 cause of invasive disease in children and the elderly [D — CDC Active Bacterial Core Surveillance]. 19A's resistance is famous — the ST320 clone carries resistance to penicillin, cotrimoxazole, carbapenems, macrolides, and tetracyclines, and by 2008 serotype 19A caused 43.7% of penicillin-nonsusceptible invasive disease in the United States [D]. The mechanism of its emergence is capsular switching: the resistant 19F clone Taiwan19F-ST236 changed its coat and walked into the room vaccination emptied [D]. Beyond the six, resistance rides with **11A** (clone CC156; a resistant lineage in a switched capsule [D]), **15B** (penicillin/macrolide non-susceptibility [D]), **33F** (erythromycin association, OR 11.1 [D]), and **8** [D]. Ten of his 23 carry documented resistance associations [D].

**The orphans.** Four serotypes that PPSV23 carried and the early conjugate vaccines never picked up. Serotype **2** is historical — rare in modern disease, the classic lab strain D39's serotype — and PPSV23 carries it almost as an archive [D]. Serotypes **9N** and **17F** were PPSV23-only until PCV21; 9N's clones are generally susceptible [D], 17F's modern profile is thin [GAP]. Serotype **20** remains PPSV23-only; PCV21 carries 20A, and whether 20A cross-protects against serotype 20 is not established [GAP] [D].

**The serotype-3 persister [D].** In all four vaccines — PPSV23, PCV15, PCV20, PCV21 — and persisting anyway [D]. After PCV13, its incidence was "not substantially altered from the pre-PCV era," and it remains a major cause of adult invasive disease [D — PMC9764711]. The reason is capsule biology, not classic resistance: it sheds capsule polysaccharide from its surface, and the shed material inhibits antibody-mediated killing [D]. It caused 26.9% of Belgian PCV13-era vaccine failures and ~40% of PCV13 failures in one systematic review [D], and it wears the thickest capsule on record — 428% of reference in mucoid isolates [D]. The serotype the vaccines keep including and keep failing to stop.

## Chapter 17 — Susceptibility and the Honest Box

### The honest box: two frameworks, two organisms [D]

The box his commission required — the record draws a line the manuscript will not cross:

- **MSSA vs MRSA** is a *Staphylococcus aureus* framework. MSSA = methicillin-susceptible *S. aureus*. MRSA = methicillin-resistant *S. aureus*, carrying the *mecA* gene on the mobile element SCC*mec*, producing PBP2a — an additional penicillin-binding protein with decreased affinity for beta-lactams [D].
- **PSSP / PISP / PRSP (or PNSP)** is the *Streptococcus pneumoniae* framework. PSSP = penicillin-susceptible *S. pneumoniae*; PISP = intermediate; PRSP = resistant; PNSP = penicillin-nonsusceptible (intermediate plus resistant) [D].

Laid side by side **without conflating them**: staph has no serotypes (spa typing, MLST, SCC*mec*), so "MRSA serotypes" is not a documented category [D]. His expectation — *"that will tell me all things about what medicines work with each serotype and serotype sickness"* — is filed verbatim as his [H]; the honest line beside it: susceptibility patterns describe the organism in the lab, not the medicine for a person [D]. No treatment recommendations live in this chapter.

### The two resistance machines [D]

The record holds two documented machines — the kinship between them is the substrate of his resistance thesis:

- **Machine 1: pneumococcal penicillin resistance.** Penicillin kills by binding penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs 1a, 2b, 2x) — the enzymes that build the cell wall [D]. Resistant pneumococci carry PBPs with reduced affinity — the drug cannot grip its target [D]. The altered *pbp* genes are **mosaics**: native DNA recombined with blocks picked up from related streptococci (*S. mitis*, *S. oralis*), which *S. pneumoniae* splices into its own chromosome because it is naturally transformable [D]. Selection does the rest: the altered survive and spread as named international clones (Spain23F-1, Spain6B-2) [D]. His framing — "borrowed armor" — is filed as his [H].
- **Machine 2: staphylococcal methicillin resistance.** The *mecA* gene encodes PBP2a, an added substitute protein that keeps building the wall with the drug present — same target class as Machine 1, different route (an added substitute, not a rewritten one) [D].

The documented kinship: both machines work by changing the drug's grip on the wall-building enzymes [D]. The step from there to his thesis is his own.

### The breakpoints the table uses [D]

The CLSI penicillin breakpoints for *S. pneumoniae* (revised 2008) depend on where the drug is working, because the same bacterium in a different compartment faces a different drug concentration [D]:

- **Meningitis (IV penicillin):** Susceptible ≤0.06 µg/mL; no intermediate category; Resistant ≥0.12 µg/mL.
- **Non-meningitis (IV):** S ≤2; I = 4; R ≥8 µg/mL.
- **Non-meningitis (oral):** S ≤0.06; I 0.12–1; R ≥2 µg/mL.

Multidrug-resistant (MDR) = non-susceptible to three or more classes [D]. The anchor for the whole table: CDC Active Bacterial Core Surveillance, 2024, 3,013 isolates — penicillin 95.8% susceptible, erythromycin 23.9% resistant, levofloxacin 99.8% susceptible, vancomycin 100% susceptible [D].

## The Parable of the Three Fields
