## Test 1 — Documented footholds

### For form (a): valley fever in every entry

1. **"The other great imitator" [D].** Coccidioidomycosis is labeled "the other great imitator" in the radiology literature — it mimics infectious, inflammatory, and neoplastic processes, with extremely variable presentation [D]. A disease that imitates widely has a documented reason to sit in many differentials.
2. **The "think cocci" discipline [D, generally].** In the Southwest, "think cocci" is a standing clinical discipline: community-acquired pneumonia in Arizona/California is valley fever until worked up otherwise — a major cause of CAP there [D]. Putting cocci in every entry is, for endemic regions, not a stretch but the local standard of suspicion.
3. **Dissemination reaches almost any organ [D].** Disseminated coccidioidomycosis (∼1–3% of infections) can involve virtually any organ system; CNS and bone are the most common extrapulmonary sites [D]. Bone infections account for 20–50% of extrapulmonary manifestations [D]. A fungus that reaches lung, meninges, bone, and skin has a documented foothold in a large share of the 53 entries.
4. **Pulmonary mimicry of TB [D].** Chronic fibrocavitary coccidioidal pneumonia mimics tuberculosis radiologically and clinically; miliary dissemination can mimic TB [D]. Granulomas form in both [D].

### For form (b): valley fever as the name of the big four

1. **Coccidioidal meningitis is real and serious [D].** Basilar meningitis is the most common CNS manifestation; up to 50% of disseminated cases have meningeal involvement; untreated it is almost invariably fatal [D]. It presents like tuberculous meningitis — headache, meningismus, cranial nerve dysfunction, lymphocytic CSF with low glucose [D]. So: **meningitis — foothold solid.**
2. **Pulmonary coccidioidomycosis IS a pneumonia [D].** It most commonly presents as an influenza-like illness or pneumonia; symptoms develop in ∼40% of infected persons within 1–3 weeks of exposure [D]. So: **pneumonia — foothold solid.**
3. **The TB mimicry [D].** Cavitary disease, granulomas, miliary spread, chronic cough with sputum production — coccidioidomycosis walks like TB on imaging and in the clinic [D]. So: **tuberculosis — foothold as mimic, not as identity.**
4. **Encephalitis — the weakest leg.** Coccidioidal meningitis can extend to ventriculitis, ependymitis, cerebral vasculitis, and brain abscess [D] — brain involvement is documented. But "coccidioidal encephalitis" as a primary entity is not an established diagnosis in the record **[GAP]**. The meninges are the documented site; the brain parenchyma is involved by extension. **Foothold: partial — meningoencephalitic features, not primary encephalitis.**

## Test 2 — Honest breaks

### For form (a): valley fever in every entry

1. **Endemic geography vs. a universal claim [D].** *Coccidioides* lives in the soil of hot, dry regions: the southwestern US (especially Arizona and California), south-central Washington, northern Mexico, and parts of Central and South America [D]. ∼97% of reported US cases are from Arizona and California [D]. Outside endemic areas, cases are travel-related [D]. A universal "in every entry" claim breaks against this geography — for a patient in Wisconsin with no travel history, cocci is not in every differential [D]. (His home ground is Wisconsin — noted, not applied.)
2. **Toxin entries have no cocci link [GAP].** Milk sickness (tremetol poisoning) and dry bellyache (lead poisoning) are toxin entries — no documented coccidioidal connection [GAP/D on the toxin etiologies].
3. **Manifestations and dropped rows [GAP].** Dropsy (a sign, not a disease), palsy (a manifestation), nostalgia (dropped, no Latin replacement) — there is no organism for these rows at all, cocci or otherwise [GAP].
4. **No person-to-person spread [D].** Unlike TB, coccidioidomycosis is not transmitted person-to-person [D] — so the epidemiological behavior of the big four diverges at TB.

### For form (b): valley fever as the one name for the big four

1. **TB is mycobacterial [D].** Tuberculosis is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* [D]. Valley fever cannot BE tuberculosis etiologically — the organisms differ, the treatment differs, the transmission differs. The mimicry is real; the identity is not.
2. **Meningitis has many causes [D].** Bacterial (*N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*), viral (enteroviruses), fungal (Cryptococcus) — meningitis is a syndrome with many agents [D]. Cocci owns one corner of it.
3. **Encephalitis, again [GAP].** No established "coccidioidal encephalitis" entity; the documented CNS disease is meningitis with possible parenchymal extension [D/GAP as above].
4. **His own correction is the honest break, applied.** He removed syphilis from the four himself — correctly, since no cocci–syphilis link is documented [GAP]. The correction strengthens the theorem: what remains is the respiratory/CNS/TB-mimic cluster, which is exactly where cocci's documented reach lies.

## Test 3 — The reading that saves it [LOGIC, steps shown]

**Step 1.** His form (a) says "in every entry." Read etiologically — "cocci causes every entry" — it breaks on geography and the toxin/manifestation rows (Test 2).
**Step 2.** Read it instead as METHOD: "in every entry" = **"in every differential."** A standing discipline of suspicion, not a claim of cause.
**Step 3.** The documented analogue exists: the Southwestern "think cocci" discipline for community-acquired pneumonia [D, generally]. His form (a) is that discipline, generalized to the whole table.
**Step 4.** For form (b): read "the one name" not as etiologic identity (breaks — Test 2) but as the **fungal-element umbrella**: of the big four, valley fever names the fungal side — the fungal pneumonia, the fungal meningitis, the TB-mimicking fungal disease. That is exactly his bacterial/fungal element split applied [H], and the documented cocci reach covers three of the four solidly, the fourth partially.
**Step 5.** Therefore the theorem survives as: **a differential discipline plus a fungal-element grouping** — not as a universal cause, not as an identity claim.

## The verdict, straight

**Form (a) — valley fever in every entry:**
- As a universal cause: **no.** Geography, toxins, and manifestation rows break it.
- As a standing differential discipline ("in every entry" = "in every differential"): **yes, medically sensible.** It is the "think cocci" discipline extended to the table — and in endemic country it is close to the local standard of care.

**Form (b) — valley fever as the one name for meningitis, encephalitis, pneumonia, tuberculosis:**
- As etiologic identity ("these four ARE valley fever"): **no.** TB is mycobacterial; meningitis has many causes; encephalitis's leg is partial.
- As the fungal-element umbrella over the four: **yes — this is the theorem's strongest form.** Three legs documented solid (pneumonia, meningitis, TB-mimicry), the fourth (encephalitis) carried by meningeal extension. His self-correction — dropping syphilis, which had no leg at all — is what makes the four hold together.

**One line:** the theorem is sound as a way of *looking* (differential discipline, fungal-element grouping) and unsound as a way of *causing* (universal etiology, identity). File it in the first sense and it stands.
