# The First Five — A Testing Model for the Sick-Unknown (2026-10-17)

*Built at Douglas's commission. An informational model — manuscript reference, never medical advice. Testing decisions belong to the clinician. Prices are rough self-pay ranges (Quest-published; vary by location, insurance, and ordering route). All five are commodity tests in every major reference lab's catalog — Mayo Clinic Laboratories, Quest Diagnostics, Sanford, Marshfield.*

## The First Five — cheapest first, most information per dollar

**1. CBC with differential — the blood's census.** Red cells, white cells (with the differential: neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, basophils), platelets. One tube tells you: infection fighting (high whites), which arm is fighting (neutrophils = bacterial-leaning, lymphocytes = viral-leaning, eosinophils = parasitic/allergic-leaning), anemia, platelet trouble. Rough self-pay: ~$25–50. [D — the highest-yield cheap test in medicine]

**2. Comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP) — the chemistry.** Glucose, kidney (creatinine, BUN), liver (ALT, AST, bilirubin, albumin), electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, CO₂), calcium, protein. The organs' report card in one draw. Rough self-pay: ~$40–90. [D]

**3. CRP — the inflammation signal.** C-reactive protein rises fast with inflammation and infection, falls fast when they ease. The "is something on fire, and is it going out" number. Tens of dollars self-pay. [D]

**4. ESR — the slow inflammation signal.** Erythrocyte sedimentation rate: the older, slower marker — rises and falls over days to weeks where CRP moves in hours. The pair (CRP + ESR) separates the acute from the smoldering. Tens of dollars self-pay. [D]

**5. Urinalysis — the kidney's report.** Protein, blood, white cells, bacteria, glucose, ketones in the urine. Catches kidney trouble, urinary infection, and diabetic spillover for the price of a cup. Rough self-pay: ~$25–50 (Quest's shoppable floor lists it near ~$6 at the lowest negotiated tier). [D]

**The five together: ~$150–300 self-pay, two tubes and a cup** — and they answer the first questions of every sick-unknown: Is there infection? Which arm? Are the organs holding? Is there inflammation, acute or smoldering? Is the kidney in it?

## The model — what happens after the five (his pillars as tier two)

The five are the sorting floor. What they show picks the fork — and the forks are his four pillars:

- **Infectious/inflammatory signals → the infectious fork:** blood cultures ×2 before antibiotics (his Oct 2), the arboviral panel (his between — encephalitis serology), the PN23M pneumococcal serotype panel (his panel rule), the fungal workup (his Quest fungal searches), chest imaging if the lungs are in it.
- **The reflex principle (his Fix List #1):** every abnormal result reflexes — the lab doctor's job. No wasted blood, no wasted testing.
- **The nourishment note (his 139):** starved bodies break tests — feed the patient, retest. The five get re-run after nourishment, not just after treatment.
- **The panel rule (his 92):** partials examined, never discarded, never upgraded. The five are the start of the record, not the verdict.

## Honest lines

- Prices move: Quest's own shoppable file shows the same CBC priced from ~$3 to ~$40 across negotiated tiers — the ranges above are walk-in self-pay, not quotes.
- Sanford's and Marshfield's individual menus weren't separately verified here; the five are universal catalog tests, which is why the model starts with them instead of anything exotic.
