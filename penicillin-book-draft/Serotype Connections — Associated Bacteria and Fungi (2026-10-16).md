# Serotype Connections — Associated Bacteria and Fungi (2026-10-16)

*Researched at Douglas's direction for The Mycelial Transposition. Association reference only — never medical advice, no treatment or diagnostic content.*

**Scope note:** Douglas recalled reading that Staphylococcus, Candida auris, and other organisms connect to pneumococcal serotypes. This file reports what the literature actually documents. Almost all connections are at the **species level or vaccine-serotype-group level** (PCV7/PCV13 serotypes vs. non-vaccine types) — genuinely serotype-specific organism links are rare. Nothing here is invented; thin spots are marked.

| Organism | Connected to which serotypes | Nature of connection | Key source |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Staphylococcus aureus* | Pneumococcus generally; vaccine-serotype vs non-vaccine groups | **Competition/antagonism.** *S. pneumoniae* kills *S. aureus* in vitro via hydrogen peroxide (SpxB pyruvate oxidase), converted to hydroxyl radicals. Epidemiologic studies show negative carriage association (co-colonization aOR 0.53 for vaccine serotypes, 0.67 for non-vaccine). *S. aureus* fights back with catalase and a fatty acid (h18:0) toxic to pneumococcus. Caveat: a neonatal-rat study found H2O2 had no significant effect in vivo — the mechanism is debated outside the dish. | J. Bacteriology (2006, 2019); PLoS ONE PCV7 RCT (van Gils et al.) |
| *Staphylococcus aureus* | PCV13 serotypes 3, 14, 18C, 19F, 19A (named in study); non-PCV13 types 8, 11A, 24F | **Mixed biofilm.** *S. aureus*–*S. pneumoniae* polymicrobial biofilms studied; post-PCV13 immune sera reduced pneumococcal biofilms of the listed serotypes but did not reduce the *S. aureus* population. Pilus-island-carrying pneumococci negatively associated with *S. aureus* colonization. | Front. Immunology (2024, Sempere et al.) |
| *Staphylococcus aureus* | Vaccine serotypes cleared by PCV7 | **Replacement.** PCV7 caused a temporary doubling of *S. aureus* nasal colonization in children (10.1% vs 5.0% at 12 months) — clearing vaccine serotypes opened the niche. The "clearing one thing opens room" mechanism, documented. | PLoS ONE (2011, van Gils et al.) |
| *Haemophilus influenzae* (nontypeable) | Pneumococcus generally | **Both competition and synergy.** Negative: pneumo H2O2 inhibits *H. influenzae*; pneumo neuraminidase enhances complement clearance of *H. influenzae*; arginine competition; acidic metabolites from pneumo lysis kill *H. influenzae*. Positive: co-carriage synergistic in healthy children; dual-species biofilms produce more biofilm than either alone; co-occurrence linked to higher pneumonia odds. | J. Infection; PLoS ONE (2022, triple-species arginine study); Lancet scoping review |
| *Moraxella catarrhalis* | Pneumococcus generally | **Co-carriage synergy + biofilm.** Positive carriage association *S. pneumoniae*–*M. catarrhalis* (OR 3.07); *M. catarrhalis* increased *H. influenzae* growth in dual/triple-species biofilms. Negative association *M. catarrhalis*–*S. aureus* (OR 0.06). Densities of all three positively correlated when co-present. | PLoS ONE (2018, Indonesian children carriage study) |
| *Candida* spp. | *S. pneumoniae* (species level, one case) | **Co-infection, single case report.** A necrotic lung ball (fungus ball) attributed to *Candida* + *S. pneumoniae* co-infection in one patient. No carriage, biofilm, or interaction mechanism studied between *Candida* and *S. pneumoniae*. | PMC (case report) |
| *Candida albicans* | Oral streptococci (*S. gordonii, S. oralis, S. sanguinis, S. mutans*) — **not** *S. pneumoniae* | **Biofilm synergy (oral niche).** *C. albicans* coaggregates with oral streptococci, enhancing bacterial colonization/biofilm and becoming more invasive itself. Frequently cited in cross-kingdom literature — but the streptococci are oral species, not pneumococcus. | PLoS Pathogens (Candida–streptococcal review); NCBI Bookshelf (polymicrobial diseases) |
| *Candida auris* | None documented with pneumococcus or any serotype | **Concurrent bloodstream infections only.** In *C. auris* BSI series, concurrent bacterial BSIs occur (e.g., *S. capitis*, *P. aeruginosa* in 2/11 patients); ICU-cohort analysis shows candidemia co-varies with *S. aureus* (0.47), *P. aeruginosa* (0.4), *Acinetobacter* spp. (0.69) — these are co-occurrence statistics, not interactions, and none involve *S. pneumoniae*. | BMC Infect. Dis. (*C. auris* BSI series); Med. Mycology (ICU co-variance); Cureus (Oman cohort) |
| *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* | Pneumococcus generally (already on file) | **Co-infection, both directions.** Documented in the serotype-trees research; included here for completeness — pneumo pneumonia can reactivate latent TB; TB-damaged lung invites pneumo superinfection. | Case reports; cohort studies (filed Oct 16) |

## What was NOT found

- **No literature links *Candida auris* to *S. pneumoniae* or to any specific serotype.** The *C. auris*–bacteria literature is about concurrent bloodstream infections in ICU patients (shared risk factors: lines, catheters, critical illness), not biological interaction with pneumococcus.
- **No *Candida*–*S. pneumoniae* interaction literature beyond one co-infection case report.** The rich *Candida*–streptococcus biofilm literature concerns oral streptococci, a different neighborhood of the genus.
- **No per-serotype organism-connection map exists in the literature.** Connections are studied at species level, or at vaccine-group level (PCV7/PCV13 serotypes vs. non-vaccine types). The one exception names serotypes 3, 14, 18C, 19F, 19A in a mixed *S. aureus* biofilm study.
- **Douglas's recollection** of reading about Staphylococcus/Candida auris "connected to the serotypes" most plausibly traces to the **PCV replacement literature** (clearing vaccine serotypes shifts *S. aureus* carriage) and the **species-level antagonism studies** above — real connections, but at group/species level, not per-serotype.

**For the book:** the documented web is real — antagonism (pneumo vs. *S. aureus*), synergy (pneumo + *H. influenzae* + *M. catarrhalis* biofilms), and replacement (vaccines clearing serotypes reshuffle the niche). The per-serotype organism map Douglas envisions does not exist yet in the literature — which makes it his to build, honestly labeled as his analysis, not as established science.
