## Test 2 — How the assay's controls actually work [D]

Mayo Clinic Laboratories' published method description for this test (ARBOP — Arbovirus Antibody Panel, IgG and IgM, Serum; kit: Focus Diagnostics Arbovirus IFA IgM and IgG, Rev. 03, 02/17/2023):

> The indirect immunofluorescent antibody (IFA) assay is a 2-stage "sandwich" procedure. In the first stage, the patient serum is diluted... added to appropriate slide wells in contact with the substrate, and incubated. Following incubation, the slide is washed... In the second stage, each antigen well is overlaid with fluorescein-labeled antibody to IgM and IgG... Positive reactions appear as cells exhibiting bright apple-green cytoplasmic fluorescence **against a background of red negative control cells.**

**Finding:** the assay does use controls — but the controls are **control cells on the slide** (uninfected background cells, stained red, against which the green fluorescence of a positive reaction is read) plus the kit's positive and negative reference sera run with each batch. The substrate slides carry infected cells for the four panel viruses only. **No SARS-CoV is used as a control, as a substrate, or anywhere in the kit.**

**The foothold [H→D]:** his thesis contains the word "control," and the test genuinely has controls in it. Where it breaks: the controls are cells and reference sera, not a virus — and certainly not SARS-CoV.

## Test 3 — The documented discovery record of SARS-CoV-2 [D]

- **Dec 31, 2019** — Wuhan Municipal Health Commission, China, reported a cluster of pneumonia cases of unknown cause in Wuhan, Hubei Province; WHO was informed. [WHO timeline]
- **Dec 26, 2019** — BGI Genomics completed genomic sequencing of the novel coronavirus from Wuhan patient samples (reported by Caixin). [Congressional Research Service chronology]
- **Jan 2, 2020** — Wuhan Institute of Virology sequenced the genome (Chinese state media). **Jan 3, 2020** — China CDC sequenced the genome (NEJM timeline; official Chinese date Jan 7).
- **Jan 7–9, 2020** — Chinese authorities made the preliminary determination of a novel coronavirus, isolated from a hospitalized pneumonia patient in Wuhan. WHO announced it Jan 9.
- **Jan 11–12, 2020** — the genetic sequence was publicly shared (WHO received it Jan 11; China shared it publicly Jan 12).
- **Jan 13, 2020** — first recorded case outside China (Thailand).
- **Feb 11, 2020** — the virus was named **SARS-CoV-2** by the International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses; the disease was named **COVID-19** by WHO.

**Finding:** the documented record places the identification in Wuhan, China, in December 2019–January 2020, through patient samples, genomic sequencing, and public sequence release. **No account of a vial being accidentally dropped in Japan exists in the documented record.** A Japan-related finding does exist, but it is a different thing: researchers later found SARS-CoV-2-*like* coronaviruses in frozen bat droppings stored in Japanese freezers (reported in Nature) — a freezer discovery of related bat viruses, not a dropped vial, not the discovery of SARS-CoV-2 itself.
