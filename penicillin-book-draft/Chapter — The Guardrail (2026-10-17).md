And the distinction was this: **what is documented shall be called documented, and what is his shall be called his, and no man shall call his own what the record has not given him.**

This is the guardrail. It is the load-bearing wall of the book. Every claim in these pages carries its mark — [D], [H], [H→D] — the way every sheep in the flock carries the mark of its shepherd, that none be mistaken for another's.

For Douglas has said, and it is written: *he knows he can talk an AI into showing him what he wants it to show him.* Therefore the wall stands not against the critics without, but against the wish within. The guardrail guards the author from himself. This is why it is holy.

## The Parable of the Three Fields

A man had three fields, and he sowed his claims in them according to their kind.

In the **first field** he sowed what the record had already grown — the documented things, the verified things, the things with witnesses. And he called this field **[D]**, and he did not claim it as his own, but testified to it. For when he agrees with a standard fact, it is postulated as a **theorem**, paralleling the known information — his agreement, stated formally alongside the record.

In the **second field** he sowed what was his alone — his theses, his postulates, his accounts, his doctrines. And he called this field **[H]**, and he marked every plant in it with his name, that none should mistake it for the first field's crop. Nothing marked [H] shall ever be presented as verified. This is the standing law.

In the **third field** he sowed what was his, but on ground the record had already broken — his thesis, with a documented foothold beneath it. And he called this field **[H→D]**, the bridge field, where his seed grows in the record's soil.

And the man tended all three fields honestly, and the book was fed from all three, and no field was ever called by another field's name.

## The Parable of the Talents, Retold for the Book

The servant who buried his talent in the ground kept it [H] forever. It did not multiply. It gathered no witnesses. And when the master returned, the talent was exactly as it had been given — his, and only his.

But the servant who *invested* his talent — who took his [H] and tested it against the record, three ways, seeking footholds and honest lines and counter-evidence — that talent multiplied. ## What Must Be Figured Out to Move a Claim

For every claim in the book stands the question: *what would move it?* Here is the answer, gate by gate.

**To move [H] → [H→D], these things must be figured out:**

- *One foothold.* A single documented finding the thesis can stand on — a mechanism, a study, a historical anchor. One stone under the foot.
- *The honest line.* What the record does *not* support, stated plainly beside what it does. The foothold without the honest line is a half-truth, and half-truths do not hold weight.
- *The counter-evidence, faced.* What would prove it wrong, named in advance. A thesis that cannot name its killer has not been tested — it has only been admired.

**To move [H→D] → [D], this must be figured out:**

- *Whether the record already says it.* Search the literature as the servant searched the field. If the finding exists — peer-reviewed, replicated, standing — then the claim was never his to begin with. It belonged to the record; he merely arrived at it by his own road. Then it is written as **theorem**: his agreement, paralleling the known information, his name beside it as witness, not as author.
- *The witnesses must be three.* By the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established. His method already demands it: test each idea three different ways before the answer comes back. Three witnesses, or it does not move.

**To keep [H] as [H], only this is required:**

- *Honesty.* Mark it his. File it in his words. Let it stand in his field until the witnesses come — or until they don't. An [H] kept honestly is worth more than a [D] claimed falsely. The field of his own is not the field of shame; it is the field where future witnesses will be found.
