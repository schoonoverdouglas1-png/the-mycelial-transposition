# Four Theses — Duration, Dosing, Feeling (2026-10-19)

## Thesis 1 [H]: The longer the sickness, the longer the medicine — penetration of body and liquids takes time

**His thought:** the longer we're sick, the longer medicine needs to be given — to penetrate not just the body but the liquids in the body, and clearing the overgrowth takes time.

**My thought [D]:** the shape is documented: deep/chronic infections get long courses (endocarditis weeks, osteomyelitis weeks, TB months); pharmacokinetics is the science of penetration — compartments, fluids, barriers (CSF, biofilm); clearing established overgrowth takes longer than clearing early infection.

**Combined:** chronicity sets the clock, penetration sets the path. The medicine has to reach every liquid it needs to reach, and the overgrowth doesn't clear on the schedule of the prescription — it clears on its own time.

## Thesis 2 [H]: The vancomycin-mouse study — doses raised and lowered on more than one thing

**His thought:** per a study he saw on a new vancomycin-based antibiotic in mice, dosages needed to be increased and decreased based upon more than one thing.

**My thought [D]:** multi-parameter dosing is standard pharmacology — renal function, weight, pathogen susceptibility, site of infection all move the dose. His study account is his; the principle (more than one thing sets the dose) is documented practice.

**Combined:** the dose is never one number from one fact — it's set and reset by several things at once. His study showed it in mice; the principle runs everywhere dosing is done carefully.

## Thesis 4 [H]: How the patient feels gets forgotten — by accident

**His thought:** how a patient feels is the thing sometimes forgotten about — by accident.

**My thought [D]:** the forgotten-by-accident has a documented counterpart: patient-reported outcomes are a whole movement in medicine precisely because the felt experience kept dropping out of the record. His underreporting thesis (filed, on his visit sheet as Q18) is the same wound from the other side — he doesn't report, they don't ask, the feeling goes missing.

**Combined:** the feeling is data — the oldest data, the presenting data — and it gets lost by accident on both sides of the desk. Remembering it is the fix; his whole method starts there.
