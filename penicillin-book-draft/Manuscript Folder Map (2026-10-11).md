## By disease

### Candidemia / bloodstream yeast (C. auris, Candidozyma auris)
- Research Note — C. auris Mortality Meta-Analysis and the Name That Carries Dread (2026-10-08). Frontiers meta-analysis: 16 studies, 16,525 candidemia patients, pooled crude mortality OR 0.77 (lower for C. auris than non-auris). Caveats: crude/unadjusted, all observational, signal absent in South Asia and Africa; the authors' vigilance hypothesis is theirs, not measured. *Filed behind the scenes; delivered to you Oct 9.*
- Research Note — Candidozyma Cohort vs the Mortality Meta-Analysis (2026-10-09). Ankara cohort: 77 Candidozyma auris vs 224 non-auris, crude 30-day mortality tied (~62% both), adjusted OR 1.97, p=0.067 (not significant). This paper adopts Candidozyma in the title; the meta-analysis used Candida. *Filed behind the scenes; delivered to you Oct 10.*
- Research Note — Binomials as Compressed Field Notes — Candida auris albicans (2026-10-09). Candida = "shining white" (colony color); auris = "ear" (first 2009 isolate, ear canal, Japan); albicans = "becoming white." Names as field notes, not illness descriptions. *Based on your input — your Oct 9 etymology thread.*
- Research Note — Nanopore Sequencing Fungal Bloodstream Infections (2026-10-05). Chiba study: species-level fungal ID from still-incubating blood cultures in ~7 hours. *Filed behind the scenes; disclosed to you Oct 5.*

### C. glabrata
- Research Note — Nakaseomyces glabratus and the Name Kept Anyway (2026-10-09). Paper titles itself "Candida glabrata (Nakaseomyces glabratus)" — acknowledges the reclassification, keeps the old name anyway; epithet shifts glabrata → glabratus. Caveat: in-vitro chalcone study; the naming move is the point, not the compound. *Filed behind the scenes; delivered to you Oct 10.*

### Oral thrush / IBD gut mycobiome
- Research Note — Antifungal Mycobiome Study in IBD (2026-10-05). 53 IBD patients with mild oral thrush: swish-and-spit nystatin vs fluconazole; fluconazole reduced gut Candida overgrowth, shifted mycobiome, raised bacterial diversity; effects persisted 8 weeks, tracked with lower progression risk. Caveats: observational, not an RCT; fluconazole is a synthetic azole, not a fungal-derived drug. *Based on your input — you tapped "File a note on it for peer review," Oct 5.*
