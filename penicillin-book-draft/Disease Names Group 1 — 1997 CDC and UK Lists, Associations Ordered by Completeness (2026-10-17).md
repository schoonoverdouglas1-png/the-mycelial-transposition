## Provenance note — which 1997 lists, honestly

- **The CDC list [D]:** Table 1, "Infectious diseases designated as notifiable at the national level — United States, 1997," in MMWR 1997;46(No. RR-10), *Case Definitions for Infectious Conditions Under Public Health Surveillance* (May 2, 1997) — verified against the archived document. 52 diseases. This is the American accepted-disease-names list, 1997, primary throughout per his namology.
- **The UK list [D]:** The Public Health (Infectious Diseases) Regulations 1988, Schedule 1, as in force in 1997 — verified via legislation.gov.uk — plus the five diseases named in the Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984 itself (cholera, plague, relapsing fever, smallpox, typhus). ~29 diseases. UK names shown alongside where they differ; UK-only diseases get their own rows.
- **No substitution was needed.** Both lists were verifiable from primary sources. Where a 1997 vaccine-licensure date is given, it is from FDA/CDC licensure records via his 2010 Vaccine Dissection build.
- **"Viral kuzanin"** — his dictation; read by the builder as **viral cousin-ing** (drawing each virus's cousin relationships, per his relatedness thesis) and/or paralleling. The reading is the builder's; his correction is open.
- **First group:** this is Group 1 of his larger organization — the 1997 notifiable-disease names. Later groups will follow at his direction.

## How to read the rows

- **Sort:** most complete information first, narrowing to the least — but **the connection is marked at every row, even the thin end**, per his explicit order. Completeness score = documented cells out of 6 (fungi / bacteria / viruses / vaccines / serotypes / viral cousin-ing). [GAP] scores 0; [N/A] with a stated reason scores 1 (it is information).
- **Tier tags** on every claim: [D] documented · [H] his · [H→D] his with a documented foothold · [GAP] empty, his to fill · [N/A] not applicable, reason given.
- **Five tracks** never collapsed: vaccine ≠ virus ≠ disease ≠ illness ≠ sickness.
- **Honest note:** seasonal influenza was **not** nationally notifiable in the US in 1997 — it is absent from the CDC list. It appears as an unnumbered coda row, marked, because his thesis centers it and the sort must be honest about the gap.

## The rows, most complete first

### 1. Tuberculosis — completeness 6/6

- **UK (1997):** same — Tuberculosis.
- **Latin / old names:** *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* complex; consumption, phthisis, white plague, king's evil (scrofula), Pott's disease [D].
- **Fungi [D]:** *Aspergillus* colonizes 20–40% of post-TB cavities; chronic pulmonary aspergillosis in 9–13% of TB patients, up to 48% of symptomatic post-TB; ~1.2M global post-TB CPA (his Triad tree). **Histoplasmosis** is TB's great mimic — routinely misdiagnosed as TB in the Ohio-Mississippi Valley [D].
- **Bacteria [D]:** the TB complex kin — *M. bovis*, *M. africanum*; *M. leprae* (leprosy) as the cousin in the genus. Secondary bacterial pneumonia in cavitary lung [D].
- **Viruses [D]:** HIV→TB (the defining co-epidemic); influenza→pneumococcus→TB reactivation chain [D, both directions per the vaccine-probe trial: 43.4% fewer TB hospitalizations].
- **Vaccines [D]:** BCG — the only TB vaccine, live attenuated *M. bovis* (1924); five major substrains; protection is cell-mediated (Th1/IFN-γ), NOT antibody [D]. Not US-routine in 1997 or now.
- **Serotypes [N/A — reason]:** TB has **lineages (L1–L9), not serotypes** [D]. The distinction is load-bearing in his manuscript.
- **Viral cousin-ing [N/A — bacterial pathogen]:** mycobacterial kin noted above; no viral cousins.

### The unhiding — top 10 rows

**1. Tuberculosis.** Hidden Latin: *tuberculum* — "little swelling," the diminutive of *tuber* (lump) — + *-osis* (condition) = **"the condition of little swellings"** [D]. The name was the pathology report all along: the tubercles, named before the bacillus. American beside it: **consumption** — what it did to the patient, not what it was in the lung [D]. Two namologies, one disease: Latin said the lesion, American said the wasting.

**2. Pneumonia (Streptococcus pneumoniae).** Hidden Latin/Greek: *pneumon* = lung + *-ia* = **"the lung condition"** [D]; *streptos* (twisted) + *kokkos* (berry) = **"the twisted berry"** [D]; *-iae* = "of the lung condition" — the organism named FROM the showing [D]. American beside it: **pneumonia** — the Latin became the American unchanged; the hidden part is that every speaker saying "pneumonia" is speaking Greek about a lung [D].

**3. Measles.** Hidden Latin: *morbilli* — diminutive of *morbus* (disease) = **"the little disease"** [D]. The old doctors' joke inside the name: the least of diseases, diminutive-sized.
