## Part 2 — Everything since tuberculosis

**Tuberculosis — names across languages.** *Tuberculosis — Names Across Languages and Associated Diseases*: pathogen names, the disease in 13 languages, 8 site-specific forms, folk names. Findings: *myco-* = fungus inside the TB name since 1882; *tuberculum* (small nodule) echoing his "small protein nodules"; the consumptive→tubercular rename changed how patients *felt* (his symptomology thesis in a history lecture); scrofula/lupus were believed different diseases until the bacillus proved one. Clarified: no standard "tuberculosis vulgaris" — the vulgaris-form TB is lupus vulgaris.

**Vulgaris family.** *Vulgaris — the Latinology Connection*: acne, psoriasis, pemphigus, lupus, verruca, ichthyosis vulgaris — *vulgaris* = "common, ordinary," the same labeling habit across unrelated diseases (1874 lectures: psoriasis as *lepra vulgaris*). Anchors: lupus vulgaris is skin TB (his TB pillar); **pemphigus vulgaris is genuinely IgG-autoantibody-driven** (his beachhead); the word marks commonness, not cause.

**"Inclusions" defined (his glossary):** tiny hardened inclusions in skin and tissues where the body puts toxins and reacts with them to make immunity. Anchor: the tuberculous tubercle/granuloma is literally that — a hardened nodule walling off and reacting to the bacillus.

**Scrofula ("Skrulla").** Medieval Latin diminutive of *scrofa* (brood sow); TB of the neck lymph nodes; the king's evil (Edward the Confessor, St. Louis; Queen Anne touched Samuel Johnson, 1712); believed a different disease from consumption until Koch 1882. Associated: tabes mesenterica, plague buboes, lymphoma in the same territory. Note: old medicine named the swelling, the site, and the cure — everything but the cause.

**Serotype trees.** *Serotype Trees — Streptococcus pneumoniae and TB Complex*: pneumo has 100+ Danish-system serotypes — the "trees" are vaccine-coverage maps (PCV7→PCV21); his Mayo PN23M = exactly the PPSV23 23. Notables: 3 (breakthrough), 19A/8 (serotype replacement — the tree re-grew around the medicine). **TB has no serotypes** — its tree is MTBC species + lineages L1–L8, named for places/peoples (his rejected convention).

**Virus theses (his).** Viruses as both friend and enemy — misunderstood, part of healing and inflammation, which tissue regeneration requires. Viruses and the immunoglobulin system not understood to his satisfaction nor the old doctors'. The **embedded immune system**: viruses as indicators/markers — grow when sick, shrink when better; dormant in wellness, latent and felt in sickness; must be *linked* to fungal and bacterial things, showing when those bother living things. **Duality of infection**: the arbopanel testing down through one virus to find a second; the panel connects tests to find the two from the same vial of blood. **Activation → shared sickness → shared recovery → cleansing**: the universal bugs get sick with our sicknesses, get better when we do, and carry toxins out that the blood alone cannot. **Systemic link**: the two California serogroup viruses linked to ALS and Lyme/Lou Gehrig's — the convergence of his naming, virus, and family threads; the book's central knot, marked as hypothesis.

**Over-vaccination thesis (his).** Over-vaccinating creates imbalance, rooted in smallpox vaccination and fear; scarification was "shoving pus under people's skin." Honest anchor: smallpox was eradicated (WHO 1980) — the book reckons with that directly. His counter-thesis: smallpox latent, not eradicated; the old vaccinations forced ***Coccidioides***. Name check: *Coccidioides* (kok-sid-ee-OY-deez) — coccidioidomycosis = valley fever = cocci = California fever = desert rheumatism = San Joaquin Valley fever; Southwest soil fungus. Live wire: valley fever is treated with fluconazole, itraconazole, amphotericin B — his own drugs.

**Arbopanel verification.** Marshfield vs Mayo testing; the name-connection (California encephalitis/La Crosse, Jamestown Canyon, "canyon fever") associated with one test. **Verified:** all are California serogroup orthobunyaviruses — one viral family; documented cross-reactivity can blur the names (his "one test, many names" has a mechanism). Jamestown Canyon: isolated 1961 near Jamestown, CO; WI+MN report over half of U.S. cases. **Co-occurrence observation (his):** whichever you test first, both show active — "when you have one, you automatically almost always have the other." Honest note: that is exactly the cross-reactivity pattern; present it first. Honest line throughout: valley fever is fungal, not an arbovirus — that association is his thesis, not the test's.
