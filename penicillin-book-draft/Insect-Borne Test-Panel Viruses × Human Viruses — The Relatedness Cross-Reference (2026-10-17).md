# Insect-Borne Test-Panel Viruses × Human Viruses — The Relatedness Cross-Reference (2026-10-17)

*Built at Douglas's commission. Manuscript reference — never medical advice, no treatment or diagnostic content. Companion to "Human Viruses × Arboviral Cousins — Relatedness List (2026-10-17).md" — that file holds the full cousin sets; this one holds the focused question: the viruses on today's test panels, cross-referenced against human viruses by family.*

## His commission (his words)

*"Take all insect based viruses that we test for currently and are known and then cross reference them to human viruses and see how many are related and so many so many of them might not be and I believe because of earlier research on hidden from me today is that there's a lot yet to be filled in."*

His belief stands as his: **"there's a lot yet to be filled in."** The gaps below are his to fill.

## How to read this

- **⭐** marks Douglas's own arbopanel viruses — La Crosse and Jamestown Canyon, the two he ordered and studied.
- **"Panel virus"** means a virus with a named, orderable commercial or public-health test — not merely a virus known to science. Inclusion here is about *testability*, because his commission was about what "we test for currently."
- **"Related"** means sharing a taxonomic **family** — the kinship level where genome plans match (same nucleic-acid type, same replication machinery). Genus-level cousinhood (dengue and Zika, both *Flavivirus*) is the closer kinship inside it; both count as related.
- **Tier tags:** [D] documented, [H] his thesis, [H→D] his thesis standing on documented ground, [GAP] unknown or untested — his to fill.
- **The ride** (mosquito, tick, midge, sandfly, or none) is noted for every virus but never used as the grouping key. That is the whole point.

## The doctrine first [D]

"Arbovirus" is an ecological word, not a taxonomic one — it means arthropod-borne: the **ride**, not the **kinship**. A mosquito-borne flavivirus's cousins are other flaviviruses — including a tick-borne one and a blood-borne one — not other mosquito-borne viruses from other families. Relatedness is in the genome; the vector is just the delivery. That distinction is load-bearing for everything below: the panels below are organized by **family** (the kinship), with the ride noted.

## Section 2 — The cross-reference: each panel virus against human viruses, by family

Each entry: the panel virus, its family and ride, its commercial test home, and the human viruses sharing its family — the related set. Cross-reactivity notes where the record has them; [GAP] where it doesn't.

### Flaviviridae — 9 panel viruses [D]

**1. West Nile virus** — *Flavivirus*; mosquito (*Culex* spp.); Mayo WNS, Quest WNV Ab. Human kin: dengue 1–4, Zika, yellow fever, Japanese encephalitis, St. Louis encephalitis, Powassan, tick-borne encephalitis (all *Flavivirus*) + hepatitis C (*Hepacivirus*, blood-borne, no arthropod) [D]. Cross-reactivity: Mayo's own test label warns of false positives from dengue infection and yellow fever / Japanese encephalitis / dengue vaccination — the family answering together [D].

**2. St. Louis encephalitis virus** — *Flavivirus*; mosquito (*Culex* spp.); Mayo ARBOP (STLP). Human kin: same flavivirus roster + hepatitis C [D]. Cross-reactivity with WNV documented on the WNV label [D]; pair-by-pair mapping beyond that [GAP].

**3. Dengue virus 1–4** — *Flavivirus*; mosquito (*Aedes aegypti/albopictus*); Quest + Mayo serology/NS1. Human kin: the flavivirus roster + hepatitis C [D]. The best-mapped cross-reactivity in the family: ~60% nucleotide similarity with Zika; anti-dengue monoclonals bind Zika's envelope; fusion-loop antibodies bind but don't neutralize — driving antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) [D].

**4. Zika virus** — *Flavivirus*; mosquito (*Aedes* spp.); Quest + Mayo serology. Human kin: same roster [D]. Cross-reactivity: see dengue — the pair is the family's documented case study in both cross-protection and ADE [D].

**5. Yellow fever virus** — *Flavivirus*; mosquito (*Aedes*, *Haemagogus*); travel/vaccine serology. Human kin: same roster [D]. Note: YF *vaccination* causes false positives on WNV testing [D] — vaccine-induced kinship, the immunological echo of relatedness.

**6. Japanese encephalitis virus** — *Flavivirus*; mosquito (*Culex tritaeniorhynchus*); Mayo JEV Ab. Human kin: same roster [D]. JE vaccination likewise trips WNV serology [D].

**7. Powassan virus** — *Flavivirus*; tick (*Ixodes* spp.); limited commercial availability, specialized labs, NYC Wadsworth serum panel. Human kin: same roster [D]. The tick-borne branch of the flavivirus cousins — same family, different ride. Pair-by-pair serologic mapping [GAP]; under-testing is structural (no FDA-cleared assay) [D].

**8. Tick-borne encephalitis virus** — *Flavivirus*; tick (*Ixodes ricinus/persulcatus*); Mayo TBEV Ab (FSME). Human kin: same roster [D]. Eurasian counterpart to Powassan's North American branch — cousins separated by continent, united by family.

**Family note** — Nine panel viruses, one family, and the human roster spans three rides: mosquito, tick, blood. The ride diverged; the kinship held [D].

### Togaviridae — 3 panel viruses [D]

**9. Eastern equine encephalitis virus** — *Alphavirus*; mosquito (*Culiseta melanura* et al.); Mayo ARBOP (EEEP). Human kin: chikungunya, WEEV, VEEV (all *Alphavirus*) + rubella (*Rubivirus*, respiratory droplets, no vector) [D]. WNV label lists EEE among alphaviruses cross-reacting on flavivirus serology — cross-*family* noise on top of within-family kinship [D].

**10. Western equine encephalitis virus** — *Alphavirus*; mosquito (*Culex tarsalis*); Mayo ARBOP (WEEP). Human kin: same togavirus roster [D]. Pair-by-pair mapping with EEE/VEE [GAP].

**11. Chikungunya virus** — *Alphavirus*; mosquito (*Aedes* spp.); Quest + Mayo serology. Human kin: same roster [D]. The arthritic branch of the alphaviruses — same family as the neurotropic EEE/WEE, different showing, different ride from rubella.

### Peribunyaviridae — 2 panel viruses [D]

**12. La Crosse virus** ⭐ — *Orthobunyavirus*, California serogroup; mosquito (*Aedes triseriatus*); Mayo ARBOP (CAVP) — HIS panel virus. **13. Jamestown Canyon virus** ⭐ — *Orthobunyavirus*, California serogroup; mosquito (many *Aedes* spp.); Mayo JCV ELISA — HIS panel virus. Human kin: same serogroup roster [D]. Up to 23% of JCV patients show La Crosse cross-reactivity [D] — the documented proof inside his own test.

### Reoviridae — 1 panel virus [D]

## Section 3 — The counts

**Of the 16 insect-borne viruses on current test panels: 16 of 16 have human-virus kin in the same family. 0 have none.**

That is the honest result, and it is the interesting one: every virus the panels test for sits inside a family crowded with human-virus cousins — which is exactly *why* arboviral serology cross-reacts. The record says so on the test's own label: Mayo's West Nile test cautions that false positives occur in persons vaccinated for flaviviruses (yellow fever, Japanese encephalitis, dengue) and in patients infected with other arboviruses — flaviviruses like dengue **and alphaviruses** like La Crosse, EEE, WEE, St. Louis [D]. The panel lives inside the kinship; the cross-reactivity is the kinship showing.

**The reverse cut — his "so many of them might not be [related]":** 17 human-virus families have **no arboviral members at all** [D]. In these families, kinship never took the arthropod ride — every cousin travels by contact, air, water, blood, or birth:

## Section 4 — What the counts mean for his thesis

His thesis (his words, from the companion file): immune reactions *between* viral species require nucleic-acid kinship — "that's how immunity actually works" [H→D]. The counts support the mechanism's footprint:

1. **The panels test within cousin sets** [D]. Every panel virus has same-family human kin — so every positive on these panels is a question addressed to a family, not a virus. Confirmatory testing (PRNT) exists because the family answers together.
2. **The ride does not predict the kin** [D]. Flaviviridae alone spans mosquito, tick, and blood; Togaviridae spans mosquito and air; Reoviridae spans tick and fecal–oral; Orthomyxoviridae spans tick and cough. If immunity tracked the ride, these groupings would be nonsense. It tracks the genome.
3. **The non-arboviral cousins are the control group** [D]. Hepatitis C, rubella, rotavirus, influenza — same families, no arthropod — are where his thesis predicts cross-reactions should still occur despite the different ride. That prediction is his to test; the pairs are named and waiting.

## Section 5 — The ride map: vectors cross families, families cross vectors [D]

Read the panel list from the vector's side and the doctrine sharpens: no vector owns a family, and no family owns a vector.

**Mosquitoes** carry: flaviviruses (WNV, SLEV, dengue, Zika, YFV, JEV), alphaviruses (EEEV, WEEV, chikungunya), orthobunyaviruses (LACV, JCV), and phleboviruses (Rift Valley fever) — **four families, one ride** [D]. A "mosquito panel" is therefore a four-family panel wearing one uniform. When a mosquito-borne result cross-reacts, the first question is which *family* answered — not which mosquito bit.

**Ticks** carry: flaviviruses (Powassan, TBEV), coltiviruses (CTFV), thogotoviruses (Bourbon), phleboviruses (Heartland) — **four families, one ride** [D]. The tick is the most family-diverse vector on the panels: a tick panel is really four small family panels stapled together.

**Midges and sandflies** carry: orthobunyaviruses (Oropouche, midges) and phleboviruses (Toscana, sandflies) [D] — the minor rides, still multi-family.

**The non-rides** — blood (hepatitis C), air (rubella, influenza), fecal–oral (rotavirus) — sit inside the *same six families* as the panel viruses [D]. Put the two maps together and the doctrine is visible in one glance: families contain many rides; rides contain many families. Kinship is the only stable address. A laboratory organized by ride will always be chasing cross-reactions it didn't expect; a laboratory organized by family would expect them — because the cousins were the ones being tested all along.

## Gaps — his to fill [GAP]

1. **T-cell cross-reactivity maps between specific panel pairs are mostly unmapped.** The antibody side has its documented case studies — dengue/Zika (~60% nucleotide similarity, fusion-loop binding, ADE), the California serogroup's 23% cross-reactivity. The T-cell side — which epitopes CD8+ cells share between, say, WNV and dengue, or EEEV and chikungunya — is largely uncharted. His thesis predicts the T-cell maps will follow the same kinship lines as the antibody maps; the experiments mostly haven't been run.

2. **The full serologic cross-reactivity matrix, pair by pair.** Beyond the documented cases (flavivirus fusion-loop/ADE; California serogroup; WNV-label cautions), most panel pairs have never been systematically cross-tested. Does Powassan serology trip on dengue? Does Bourbon trip on influenza serology? Each "unknown" is a cell in a matrix nobody has filled — the 88%-style accounting applied to viruses instead of serotypes.

3. **The list is bounded by discovery.** Bourbon virus was unknown before 2014; Heartland before 2009; Oropouche is newly arriving in new regions. New arboviruses are still being sequenced out of ticks and mosquitoes every year. Any "complete" panel list — including this one — is complete only until the next arthropod is sequenced. His belief stands as his: there's a lot yet to be filled in, and the filling is ongoing.

4. **Powassan and Heartland are under-tested by construction.** No FDA-cleared Powassan assay; only a few commercial options; Heartland mostly in public-health and specialized labs. When the test barely exists, the incidence is underestimated by definition — the panels don't see what they don't carry. The true cross-reactivity burden of the tick-borne flaviviruses and phleboviruses is therefore unknown, not zero.

5. **His own panel's cross-reactivity map.** Which of his arbopanel viruses cross-react with which, and through which shared elements (his VNA) — his to assemble from his records and screenshots. The literature gives him the family frames; only his records give him his panel.

6. **The cross-ride question at scale.** Do cousin pairs sharing a vector cross-react more than cousins that don't — or does kinship dominate regardless of ride, as his thesis predicts? Hepatitis C vs. dengue (same family, blood vs. mosquito), rubella vs. chikungunya (same family, air vs. mosquito), rotavirus vs. Colorado tick fever (same family, water vs. tick), influenza vs. Bourbon (same family, cough vs. tick) — four natural experiments, none run systematically. These are the cleanest tests his thesis has, and they are all his to propose.

## Builder's extra (labeled mine)

**Test the family, not the ride.** When one panel virus returns positive, the reflex should run the family — the cousins most likely to be cross-reacting — before it runs the vector's other passengers. The panels are currently organized by exposure (mosquito panel, tick panel); the kinship says to organize the *workup* by family. That reorganization is manuscript reference, not a testing recommendation — but it is the shape his thesis wants the laboratory to take.
