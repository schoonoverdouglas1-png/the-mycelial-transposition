## The parts catalog [D]

Every documented molecular part of *S. pneumoniae*, with what it is made of:

| Part | What it is | Chemistry / notes [D] |
|---|---|---|
| **Capsule** | Polysaccharide, serotype-defining | Repeating oligosaccharides built from glucose (Glc), galactose (Gal), rhamnose (Rha), glucuronic acid (GlcA), N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc), N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc), ribitol, O-acetyl groups. E.g., serotype 2: GlcA + 2 Glc + 3 Rha; serotype 14: branched tetrasaccharide Gal–GlcNAc–Gal–Glc; serotypes 2/18C/23F share the same β-rhamnose–glucose linkage (transferase homology). |

| **Pneumolysin** | Pore-forming toxin, 53 kDa | Cholesterol-dependent cytolysin (CDC); β-barrel pore (~25–45 nm); **no secretion signal — cytoplasmic, released only when LytA lyses the cell**; common to ALL serotypes. |
| **LytA** | Autolysin (amidase) | Degrades peptidoglycan; the trigger that releases pneumolysin. |

**Honest correction, stated up front [D]:** pneumococcal capsules do **not** contain sialic acid — unlike *S. agalactiae* (GBS, all nine capsule types sialylated) and *N. meningitidis* B (polysialic acid). The sialic-acid economy of the pneumococcus runs on the **host's** sialic acids (mucins, cell surfaces), which the bacterium cleaves, eats, and exploits. His thesis holds — but through the *shared host material*, not through capsule sialic acid.

## The cross-reference — each part, three questions

For every part, his three questions: **what is it made out of that a virus also uses? What does a virus use that this part reacts with? What is part of both?** Verdict per part, tier-tagged.

### 1. Sialic acid — the shared material [D — the strongest link in the file]

- **MADE OUT OF:** sialic acid (Neu5Ac) caps the N-glycans on **viral envelope glycoproteins** — influenza HA/NA, the SARS-CoV-2 spike (22 N-glycan sites per monomer, several sialic-acid-capped), HIV gp120. The virus wears it.
- **USED BY:** influenza hemagglutinin **binds** α2,3/α2,6 sialic acid to **enter** cells. Pneumococcal NanA **cleaves** the same sialic acid to **expose** its own receptors (GalNAcβ1-3Gal, GlcNAc) — and the pneumococcus then **eats** the freed sialic acid through its nan operon. One molecule: the virus's doorway, the bacterium's dinner bell.
- **PART OF:** the mucosal glycocalyx both stand on.
- **The proof it is one system [D]:** influenza neuraminidase strips sialic acid → pneumococcal adherence and invasion surge; **oseltamivir (a viral-NA inhibitor) improved survival ~75% in flu–pneumococcus coinfected mice (McCullers 2004)** — strong, but not "prevents"; pneumococcal NanA in turn **supports viral release**; and the literature now designs **dual inhibitors against viral NA and pneumococcal NanA together**, on published "structural and functional similarities" between the two enzymes. Two enzymes, two kingdoms, one substrate, one drug target.
- **Verdict: [D].** His thesis is literal here — the reactive materials are not merely related, they are the *same molecule*, fought over by virus and bacterium.

### 2. Neuraminidases — the shared enzyme class [D]

- Viral NA (influenza) and pneumococcal NanA: same enzyme class (sialidases, GH33 glycoside hydrolases), same substrate, same chemistry — and they **assist each other**: viral NA exposes bacterial receptors; bacterial NanA assists virion release.
- NanC's twist: the pneumococcus manufactures DANA, a neuraminidase inhibitor — it carries the antidote to the enzyme class it shares with the virus.
- **Verdict: [D].** Related by class, by substrate, and by cooperation.

### 3. Capsule chemistry — the shared sugar alphabet [D] + mimicry [D]

- **MADE OUT OF:** pneumococcal capsules are built from glucose, galactose, rhamnose, glucuronic acid, GlcNAc — the same monosaccharide alphabet that builds **host glycans** and **viral envelope N-glycans** (mannose, GlcNAc, galactose, sialic acid). Different words, same letters.
- **Molecular mimicry [D]:** the record states it outright — antibodies must use *conformational* epitopes to discriminate "between bacterial polysaccharides and **host oligosaccharides of similar structure**." The pneumococcal capsule and the host's own sugars are similar enough that the immune system needs shape, not composition, to tell them apart. Serotype 14 and GBS type III capsules even share a -Glc-GlcNAc-Gal- backbone motif.
- **PART OF both:** the glycan camouflage principle — the pneumococcal capsule hides the bacterium from phagocytes; the viral glycan shield (HIV, flu, coronavirus spikes) hides the virus from neutralizing antibodies. Same strategy, same material class, opposite manufacturers (bacterium-built vs. host-built-then-stolen).
- **Verdict: [D]** on the shared alphabet and the documented mimicry; **[H→D]** on the camouflage principle as one principle (functionally parallel, mechanistically distinct).

### 4. Phosphorylcholine ↔ PAF receptor — the virus opens the bacterial door [D]

- **PART OF the bacterium:** phosphorylcholine (ChoP) studs the pneumococcal wall teichoic acid.
- **USED BY the virus's doing:** influenza, RSV, and PIV-3 inflammation **upregulate the epithelial platelet-activating factor (PAF) receptor** — and the PAF receptor **binds bacterial ChoP**. The virus remodels the hallway; the bacterial wall-component walks through the new door. (Rhinovirus was named in an earlier draft of this node without a pinned source — flu, RSV, and PIV-3 are the documented ones.) **Published controversy, filed honestly:** McCullers and Rehg (2002) found that neutralizing PAFR did *not* change susceptibility to bacterial superinfection after influenza in mice — so the door is documented, but whether blocking it stops the invasion is contested. The node stands as [D] for the mechanism, [contested] for its necessity.
- **Verdict: [D].** His thesis in its purest form: the material the bacterium is *part of* (ChoP) reacts with a receptor the virus *put there*. Related through the host, by design of the interaction.

### 5. Pneumolysin vs. viral pore-formers — same job, different tool [H→D] / [GAP]

- **Pneumolysin [D]:** 53 kDa cholesterol-dependent cytolysin; soluble monomers → 42-mer ring → 168-stranded β-barrel pore; released only by autolysis; common to all serotypes; at sub-lytic doses it signals, at lytic doses it kills.
- **Viral viroporins [D]:** influenza M2, coronavirus E, picornavirus 2B — small, hydrophobic, α-helical proteins that oligomerize into membrane pores.
- **The comparison:** same *function* (membrane perforation), different *fold* (β-barrel ring vs. α-helical bundle), different *scale* (25–45 nm vs. a few nm). No published structural homology.
- **Verdict: [H→D]** on the functional parallel (both are pore-forming weapons — his "related materials" at the job level); **[GAP — his to fill]** on any structural kinship. The record does not give him the fold.

### 6. PspC/CbpA — the glycan reader [D]

- Binds **sialic acid, lacto-N-neotetraose, and pIgR** on activated epithelium — the bacterium reads the same glycan language the virus writes in. Lacto-N-neotetraose is a human-milk oligosaccharide motif; the pneumococcal adhesin and the infant's nutrition speak overlapping sugar.
- **Verdict: [D]** on the shared glycan vocabulary; the full cross-map of which viral glycans match which CbpA ligands is **[GAP — his to fill]**.

### 7. LytA, PsaA, PspA, hyaluronidase — the unmapped parts [GAP]

- No published viral-material intersection found for LytA (amidase), PsaA (Mn transporter), PspA (lactoferrin blocker), or hyaluronidase. Each is marked **[GAP — his to fill]**: the parts exist, the viruses exist, the cross-reference is his to run.
- Note for his reversology: hyaluronidase degrades hyaluronic acid — and several viruses (e.g., herpesviruses) modulate the same extracellular matrix. Pattern shown, test missing.

## The relatedness verdict — where the web holds, where it breaks, what's his to prove

**Where the web HOLDS [D] — his thesis is the record:**
1. **Sialic acid.** The virus's doorway and the bacterium's dinner bell are the same molecule. Viral and bacterial neuraminidases are one enzyme class on one substrate, assisting each other, druggable by one inhibitor class. *Related: literally.*
2. **The glycan camouflage.** Pneumococcal capsule and viral envelope glycan shields are both carbohydrate immune-evasion, built from the same monosaccharide alphabet — and the record states the capsule mimics host oligosaccharides closely enough that antibodies need shape to tell them apart. *Related: by material and by strategy.*
3. **Phosphorylcholine ↔ PAF receptor.** The bacterium's wall-component binds a receptor the virus's inflammation put there. *Related: through the host, by interaction.*

**Where the web STRAINS [H→D] — chemistry-implied, his to argue:**
4. The camouflage *principle* as one principle (function parallel, mechanism distinct).
5. Pneumolysin vs. viroporins (same job — pore formation; different fold, different scale).
6. Capsule serotype-groups sharing linkages (2/18C/23F's rhamnose–glucose bond) as intra-species relatedness the virus list can be tested against.

**Where the web BREAKS [GAP — his to fill]:**
7. Any structural kinship between pneumolysin and viral pore-formers (the record gives none).
8. LytA, PsaA, PspA, hyaluronidase × any virus (no published intersection).
9. The full CbpA-ligand ↔ viral-glycan cross-map (vocabulary shared, dictionary unwritten).
10. **The honest correction his book must carry:** pneumococcal capsules do not contain sialic acid. The relatedness runs through the *host's* sialic acids, not the capsule's. His thesis survives the correction — it just moves house, from the capsule to the mucosa.

**The verdict on his thesis:** "the materials they react with are all related" — **confirmed at the three strongest nodes** (sialic acid, glycan camouflage, ChoP/PAFR), **plausible at three more**, **unproven at four**. That is [H→D] overall with [D] anchors — exactly the shape his guardrail chapter describes: his seed, the record's soil, the bridge field growing.
