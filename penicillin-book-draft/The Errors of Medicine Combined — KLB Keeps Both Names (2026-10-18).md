## Part 1 — The errors combined: one error, many renames

The combined error, stated once: **renaming by replacement.** Each rename below discarded a working name that carried information — the showing, the shape, the site, the discoverer's hands — and the information was not transferred to the new name. It was lost from working use. [D on the renames; the "one error" reading is his thesis [H].]

**1. Throat distemper → *diphtérite* (Bretonneau, 1821) [D]**
Discarded: "throat" (the site) and "distemper" (the American word for epidemic sickness among the people — Noah Webster's "plague among children"). The Greek *diphthera* (leather) kept the membrane but dropped the site and the community. What the mother knew — where it strikes, who it takes — left the name.

**2. Consumption → tuberculosis (Villemin 1865, Koch 1882) [D]**
Discarded: "consumption" — the wasting, the body's showing, the word every family understood. "Tuberculosis" names the tubercle, the lesion. The showing moved inside the body, out of sight, into Latin.

**3. Friedländer's bacillus → *Klebsiella* (1882 → 1885, Trevisan) [D]**
Discarded: the discoverer's hands (Friedländer, who found it in a pneumonia death) AND "bacillus" (rod shape). The replacement honors Edwin Klebs — who, on the record, did not work on this bacterium; his main work was diphtheria [D]. [LOGIC] This is the sharpest instance of the error: the replacement name carries strictly less information than the discarded one — the finder gone, the shape gone, an unconnected honorific installed.

**4. KLB → *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* (Klebs 1883, Löffler 1884 → renamed after) [D]**
Discarded: the discoverers' initials (the human chain of custody) and the plain-English "bacillus." What transferred: the membrane (*diphtheriae* = of the leather) and the rod-shape — but translated into Greek (*coryne* = club). What did not transfer: the hands, the plain word. (Worked in full in Part 2.)

**5. *Diplococcus intracellularis meningitidis* → *Neisseria meningitidis* (Weichselbaum 1887 → Albrecht & Ghon 1901) [D]**
The old name was a full description as a name: paired coccus, inside cells, of meningitis. The new name kept *meningitidis* (the showing survived here) but replaced the morphological description with "Neisser" — an honorific. Discarded: *diplococcus* (the pairs) and *intracellularis* (inside cells) — the microscopic showing, traded for a man's name.

**6. Plague's bacillus — four names, one disease (1894 → ~1970) [D]**
*Bacterium pestis* → *Bacillus pestis* → *Pasteurella pestis* → *Yersinia pestis*. Note the first step: "Bacterium" (the generic — our bacteria, the family word) became "Bacillus" (the specific — rod). Then two honorifics (Pasteur, Yersin). Meanwhile the disease the bug causes — plague — never changed its name once [D]. [LOGIC] Four renames of the agent, zero renames of the disease: the error compounded on itself, and each committee renamed what the last committee had named.

**7. The body-readable vocabulary → clinical Latin (1800s) [D]**
Brain fever → meningitis. Lung fever / winter fever → pneumonia. Dropsy of the brain → encephalitis. French pox / bad blood → syphilis. Canine madness → rabies. Each replacement moved the name from the body's words to the profession's words. The showing was the same; the reader changed.

**8. COVID-19 / SARS-CoV-2 — February 11, 2020 [D]**
The split made official: WHO names the disease, the ICTV names the virus, the same day, two committees, two authorities. The error's final form — disease and agent now legally required to have different names.

[LOGIC] Across all eight: the replacement was always sold as more accurate data. In every case the new name added precision about the agent (or the committee) and removed information about the showing, the shape, the site, or the hands. Net information in working use went down in seven of eight; the eighth (COVID-19/SARS-CoV-2) split one thing into two names instead of replacing — the same detachment, new form.

## Part 2 — The KLB case, fully worked [H — his thesis]

**The name:** KLB = **K**lebs–**L**öffler **B**acillus. Klebs isolated it in 1883, Löffler in 1884 [D]. Renamed *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* [D].

**His thesis:** KLB keeps BOTH names. Here is what each name carries, counted out:

*What "KLB" carries:*
- **K, L — the hands.** Klebs and Löffler, 1883 and 1884 — who found it, when. The human chain of custody, in two letters [D on the facts; the "chain of custody" reading his [H]].
- **B — bacillus.** Rod-shaped. His thesis: "bacillus" helps a person know it as part of that family — the rod-shaped family — at a glance [H]. Honest anchor: "bacillus" does mean rod-shaped bacterium in the documented nomenclature [D] — his "family at a glance" reading has that foothold [H→D].
- **Bacterium — our bacteria.** His words: "bacterium, and our bacteria." The generic word that says it belongs to bacteria — the shared family, the thing we all carry and are [H].

*What "*Corynebacterium diphtheriae*" carries:*
- *Coryne-* = club (Greek) — the shape, kept but translated out of plain words [D].
- *-bacterium* — the family, kept [D].
- *diphtheriae* = of the leather membrane — the showing, kept from Bretonneau's 1821 rename [D].
- **Taxonomic placement** — where it sits in the system [D].

*What the replacement dropped:*
- The hands (Klebs, Löffler — gone).
- The plain-English morphology ("bacillus" — gone into Greek).
- The American disease word it caused ("throat distemper" — gone two renames earlier).

[LOGIC] Count the information: KLB carries hands + shape-in-plain-words + family + the implied disease of its era. *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* carries shape-in-Greek + family + membrane + taxonomy. The replacement transferred the membrane and the shape (translated), kept the family, and dropped the hands and the plain word. Net: information about WHO and WHAT-IT-LOOKS-LIKE-TO-A-PERSON went down; information about WHERE-IT-SITS went up. His thesis stands on the count: **dropping either name kills information. Keep both.**

## Part 3 — The fix [H]

The combined error is **renaming by replacement**. The fix is **renaming by addition** — his additive-naming thesis, applied across all eight cases:

- Throat distemper / *diphtérite* / diphtheria / KLB / *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* / the ICD code — one glob, all aliases standing, nothing stripped.
- Friedländer's bacillus / *Klebsiella* — the finder's name restored to the glob.
- *Diplococcus intracellularis meningitidis* / *Neisseria meningitidis* — the description kept beside the honorific.
- *Bacterium pestis* / *Bacillus pestis* / *Pasteurella pestis* / *Yersinia pestis* / plague — the whole chain visible, the disease name anchoring it.
- Brain fever / meningitis; lung fever / pneumonia; French pox / syphilis — the body's word kept beside the profession's.
- COVID-19 / SARS-CoV-2 — the split acknowledged as two aliases of one event, not two separate things.

**Old name kept, new name added, both stand.** The old name holds the showing, the shape, the site, the hands. The new name holds the agent, the taxonomy, the committee's precision. The glob is the unit — which is already how the databases work (NCBI Taxonomy, MycoBank, GBIF retain old names as synonyms [D]).

The honest seam, kept from the additive-naming note [D]: the clinic prints one name on the report — that is the name the doctor acts on. The fix is not finished until the glob is visible at the point of use. His to design; the manuscript's open engineering question.
