# The Fifteen-Minute Paper — Serotypes, Elements, Tests, Names

**Filed:** 2026-10-18 · For *The Mycelial Transposition* — manuscript reference only. EDUCATIONAL reference. **Never medical advice.** No personal result is interpreted anywhere in this paper; testing and treatment decisions belong to clinicians.

**Tier tags:** [D] = documented · [H] = his, filed as his · [H→D] = his with documented foothold · [GAP] = unknown. Seven sections, each condensing one thread of the October 18 session. The full workups live in the manuscript folder; this is the readable whole.

## Abstract

One session's work, one arc. A 23-serotype pneumococcal antibody panel (22 of 23 values below the lab's 1.0 mcg/mL line) [D] is set against everything documented about each serotype — vaccines, diseases, resistance, biofilms. From it, his element split is drawn: bacteremia and pneumonia are the bacterial element; toxemia and meningitis the fungal element, each needing its own treatment set [H]. A test map organizes fungal meningitis, bacterial meningitis, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, and every connected pneumonia around a handful of distinguishing tests, with viruses as forward/backtrack markers. Twenty-five *-itis* diseases are tested against his universal *-itis*/-osis parallel: 14 documented fungal counterparts, 4 thin, 7 gaps clustering in the abdomen's hollow organs. A namology thread coins *osteomyelosis*, reclaims *bacteriosis* (a real dictionary word), and files the honest lines. The arbopanel (California/La Crosse IgG 1:20 abnormal, all else negative) [D] is placed without interpretation, beside his combination thesis: antibody testing *along with* the arbopanel [H].

## 1. The Panel × The Serotypes [D on the record; [H] on his frames]

The record: Mayo Clinic, *Streptococcus pneumoniae* IgG Antibodies, 23 Serotypes, collected Oct 29, 2025, ordered by Rick Klein, D.O. Lab threshold: ≥1.0 mcg/mL. His values, quoted as numbers only: 22 of 23 below 1.0; **10A = 1.6** the lone value above. No interpretation of his results appears anywhere in the manuscript files — that belongs to Kelsey Meyer, NP, at the Oct 19 visit.

Against the panel, every serotype was worked up: vaccine membership (PPSV23 1983, PCV15, PCV20, PCV21) [D], documented disease associations [D], resistance [D], biofilm behavior [D]. The load-bearing findings: the resistant core — 6B, 9V, 14, 19A, 19F, 23F — carries the classic multidrug-resistant clones [D]; serotype 3 is the strongest biofilm former and persists despite conjugate vaccines by shedding its capsule [D]; 19F biofilms mutate capsules at 55× the planktonic rate [D]; 19A/11A carry documented capsular-switch stories [D]; meningitis-linked serotypes within the 23 include 1, 5, 12F, 14 [D]. Every pneumococcal vaccine's polysaccharides are grown in soy-based medium [D] — his soy-polysaccharide note holds at the manufacturing level.

His frames, filed as his: a **0.1 mcg/mL grouping line** on his values, reading above-0.1 as showing immunoassay reaction [H] — the lab's line is ≥1.0; his line is his. His account: he received 23-valent PPSV ~5–6 years before the panel (~2019–2020) [H], and used the pneumo antibody test correlated with his Mayo arbopanel to determine his conditions — bacteremia, toxemia, meningitis, pneumonia — his account, filed without interpretation [H].

## 2. The Element Split [H]

His classification: **bacteremia and pneumonia are the bacterial element; toxemia and meningitis are the fungal element** [H]. Each subset, he holds, requires its own set of treatment choices [H]. Bacteremia and pneumonia are documented bacterial phenomena [D]; assigning toxemia and meningitis to a "fungal element" is his analytic frame, his to evidence — and it is the frame the rest of the session was built to test. The meningitis section of the serology paper carries the fungal-meningitis chapter (§6.6) at his request: the cryptococcal antigen lateral flow assay at ~98–99% accuracy [D], the antigen-not-antibody inversion (the test finds the organism's shed pieces, not the patient's response) [D], and the cryptococcal capsule as the polysaccharide parallel to the pneumococcal capsules [D].

## 3. The Test Map: Three Anchors, Ten Tests, One Viral Thread [D; his marker thesis [H]]

The map's three anchor nodes: fungal meningitis (Cryptococcus neoformans/gattii leading; Candida, Aspergillus, Histoplasma, Coccidioides) [D]; bacterial meningitis (pneumococcus, meningococcus, Hib, GBS, Listeria, E. coli K1) [D]; *S. pneumoniae*'s 23 serotypes [D]. Connected to them: every known pneumonia type — bacterial (pneumococcal, Klebsiella, Pseudomonas, Mycoplasma, Chlamydophila, Legionella), viral (influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV), fungal (PJP, Aspergillus, pulmonary Cryptococcus, endemic dimorphics) [D].

The handful of tests, each with what it detects and which nodes it separates: the **CSF profile** (the orienting test — neutrophils/low glucose vs. lymphocytes patterns) [D]; the **BioFire ME 14-target panel** (6 bacteria, 7 viruses, 1 yeast in ~1 hour; Candida/Aspergillus outside its targets) [D]; **CrAg** serum/CSF (~98–99% sens/spec) [D]; **(1→3)-β-D-glucan** with its discriminator logic — **BG-positive + CrAg-negative → non-crypto fungus; BG-negative + CrAg-positive → cryptococcal** [D]; **galactomannan** with documented cross-reactivities (Histoplasma, Penicillium, Fusarium) [D]; **pneumococcal urinary antigen** (C-polysaccharide, all strains, not serotype-specific, positive for weeks) [D]; **Legionella urinary antigen** (serogroup 1 only — the map marks what each test cannot see) [D]; blood cultures, Mycoplasma PCR/serology, and the **respiratory viral multiplex panel** [D]. The map never recommends ordering anything — that is the clinician's decision.

Viruses as forward/backtrack markers: the documented pair is influenza→pneumococcus — three mechanisms (PspA binding dying lung cells; type I interferon sabotaging IL-17/neutrophil clearance; pneumococcal neuraminidases feeding viral replication) [D]. His marker thesis — viral findings used to point forward to bacterial/fungal elements and backtrack from them — is filed [H] with the honest line: documented for specific pairs, the general method his to evidence.

## 4. The *-itis*–*-osis* Parallel: 25 Diseases Tested [D; postulate [H]]

His universal postulate: all of medicine viewed through the osteomyelosis/osteomyelitis parallel — every *-itis* disease has a fungal/-osis counterpart worth examining [H]. Twenty-five significant *-itis* entities were tested case by case. Result: **14 documented parallels** (meningitis, osteomyelitis, arthritis, endocarditis, sinusitis, dermatitis, encephalitis, nephritis, peritonitis, bronchitis, pharyngitis, vaginitis, conjunctivitis/keratitis — and pneumonia on substance, handled honestly since it is *-ia*, not *-itis*) [D]; **4 thin but real** at case-report level (cellulitis, tonsillitis, otitis media, gastritis, colitis narrow) [D]; **7 gaps his postulate predicts** — hepatitis, urethritis, pancreatitis, cholecystitis, appendicitis, diverticulitis. The gaps cluster in the **abdomen's hollow organs**, where bacterial *-itis* is commonest and fungal investigation rarest — absence of disease or absence of looking is the open question [GAP]. The morphology held: *-itis* is inflammation, not bacterial (viral encephalitis, autoimmune hepatitis noted) [D]; *-osis*/*-iasis* genuinely is the fungal suffix across the documented rows [D]. The postulate stays his; the list is the test, returning mostly documented, partly thin, partly open — a result, not a failure.

## 5. Names: The *-osis* Shelf [H coinages; [D] on the documented words]

Three entries now sit on his *-osis* shelf. **Osteomyelosis** — his coinage, dictionary entry drafted: *osteo-* (bone) + *myelo-* (marrow) + *-osis* (condition; the fungal suffix) [H]. Well-formed per the morphology above; a 2023 systematic review of fungal osteomyelitis (1,072 patients, Aspergillus 26.5%, Candida 20.7%, Mucor 16.8%) [D] gives it documented ground. His diseaseology ordering: *osteomyelosis* first, *osteomyelitis* second — the fungal element primary [H]. **Bacteriosis** — his umbrella term for encephalitis, meningitis, and pneumonia as bacterial-element conditions [H] — with the happy finding that the word is already in the dictionaries: Collins, "any bacterial disease" [D]. His move is reclamation, not invention. **Mycosis** — the established fungal umbrella [D].

Two genuine catches: the **SLE/SLE collision** — his arbopanel lists "SLE" as St. Louis encephalitis; SLE is also systemic lupus erythematosus [D] — a real acronym collision worth its line wherever his lupus-symptomology thesis (Lyme + California/La Crosse encephalitis symptomology = systemic lupus symptomology [H]) appears. And the La Crosse = Lyme namology via Dr. Obaid [H], filed with Obaid credited and the honest line: "La Crosse" is French (*la crosse*, the hooked stick), not Latin; Lyme is named for Lyme, Connecticut; no documented etymological equivalence [D]. The foothold: both are vector-borne, upper-Midwest prominent [H→D]. His worldwide note stands with it: disease and bacteria exist worldwide — the organism is not confined to its name-place [H], against geographic naming. His additive-naming thesis: renaming adds an alias, it does not replace [H]. His syndrome framing — "it's possible that we're talking about syndromes" [H] — is the paper's closing lens.

## 7. Closing Synthesis: What the Session Built

Six tracks, held separate: vaccines, viruses, diseases, illnesses, sicknesses, infections [H]. The session's arc runs from the **capsule** (23 serotype chemistries, the sugar that vaccines read) through the **elements** (bacterial vs. fungal, his split, tested 25 ways) to the **tests** (ten of them, each aimed) to the **names** (the *-osis* shelf, the alias rule, the honest collisions). The through-line is the polysaccharide: pneumococcal capsules, cryptococcal capsule, β-D-glucan, galactomannan — four sugar structures doing diagnostic work, with documented cross-reactivities where the sugars resemble each other [D]. The honest ledger of the session: mostly documented, partly thin, partly open. The open parts are his to evidence — and they are marked, every one, so the reader always knows which is which.
