## 1. The centerpiece: two different Latin words

The near-homonym is a coincidence. *Auris* and *aureus* are two different Latin words that happen to sound alike.

**Candida auris — "auris" = EAR** [D]
- First described in 2009 (Satoh et al.) after isolation from the **external ear canal** of a 70-year-old woman at Tokyo Metropolitan Geriatric Hospital, Japan. Phenotypic, chemotaxonomic, and phylogenetic analyses established it as a new species of the genus *Candida*. [D — Osei Sekyere 2018, *MicrobiologyOpen* (Wiley), doi:10.1002/mbo3.578; also PMC9986882, PMID:36891046]
- The species epithet was chosen for the isolation site: "auris" is the Latin word for **ear**. [D — same sources; Wikipedia *Candida auris*]
- Note the rename now in play: transferred to the novel genus *Candidozyma* in 2024 (Liu et al., *Persoonia*) — *Candidozyma auris*. The epithet *auris* survives the genus change; the "ear" meaning travels with it. [D — from the filed retention-case note, 2026-10-14]

**Staphylococcus aureus — "aureus" = GOLDEN** [D]
- In 1884, German physician Friedrich Julius Rosenbach differentiated staphylococci by colony color: *S. aureus* (from the Latin *aurum*, **gold**) for the golden colonies, and *S. albus* (Latin for white) for the white ones. *S. albus* was later renamed *S. epidermidis*. [D — CDC *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, "Etymologia: Staphylococcus," Licitra 2013, doi:10.3201/eid1909.ET1909; PMC3810938]
- LPSN (the nomenclatural authority) gives the etymology as: L. masc. adj. *aureus* (aureus – aurea – aureum), **golden**. Name: *Staphylococcus aureus* Rosenbach 1884 (Approved Lists 1980). [D — https://lpsn.dsmz.de/species/Staphylococcus-aureus]
- The genus name is Greek, not Latin: *staphyle* (bunch of grapes) + *kokkos* (berry) — the grape-cluster coccus. So the full binomial reads "golden grape-cluster berry." [D — CDC EID, same piece]
- The golden color is a carotenoid pigment, later shown to be a virulence factor (antioxidant, resists neutrophil killing). [D — PMC2213009]

**The honest line on the pair:** *auris* (ear) and *aureus* (golden) share no root, no meaning, and no lineage. One names a body site where a yeast was first found; the other names a colony color of a bacterium described 125 years earlier. The resemblance is phonetic accident — exactly the kind of name-collision a nomenclature audit should flag, because both organisms now circulate in the same clinical conversation as bloodstream pathogens in ICUs.

## 2. What the literature documents: C. auris × S. aureus

### Dual-species biofilms (in vitro, documented)

- A dual-species biofilm model of **C. auris with S. aureus and S. epidermidis** was developed to test antiseptics (chlorhexidine, H₂O₂, povidone iodine). Finding: shifting ratios of *S. aureus* did not significantly alter biofilm biomass, and *Staphylococcus* spp. **did not inhibit C. auris growth** — the two coexist in the biofilm. [D — PMC9312070, "Investigating Dual-Species Candida auris and Staphylococcal Biofilm Antiseptic Challenge"]
- Triazole-based small-molecule inhibitors (compound T3) were tested against **dual-species biofilms of C. auris and S. aureus**; the authors note theirs is "one of the few" studies on anti-biofilm efficacy against this pairing. T3 disrupted biofilm formation and reduced viability in both single- and dual-species conditions. [D — *Pharmaceutics* 2024, 16(12):1570, doi via mdpi.com/1999-4923/16/12/1570]
- Mono- vs. co-culture biofilms of *C. auris* or *C. albicans* with *S. aureus* on oral keratinocyte cell lines: co-culture biofilm-conditioned media of **C. auris + S. aureus increased IL-6 expression** vs. mono-culture *C. auris*; the pairing modulates inflammatory signaling differently than either organism alone. [D — PMC12521062]
- The broader pattern is not auris-specific: *Candida* spp. (tropicalis, albicans, rugosa) co-isolated with *S. aureus* from cystic fibrosis sputum form mixed biofilms with the highest biomass in the *C. tropicalis/S. aureus* pairing. [D — *Diagnostic Microbiology and Infectious Disease*, ScienceDirect S0732889324001500]

### Clinical co-occurrence (documented, with the right caveats)

- **C. auris candidemia + MDR bacterial co-infection (CANDI-MDR study):** among C. auris candidemia patients, those with MDR bacterial co-infection had 87.2% mortality vs. 61.2% without (p=0.007); co-infection was an independent mortality risk factor (adjusted OR 3.19). [D — thefreelibrary.com, "Patterns and Predictors of Candida auris Candidemia with Multidrug-Resistant Bacterial Co-Infections"]
- **Qatar C. auris bloodstream-infection cohort (36 patients):** 30/36 had other hospital-acquired resistant organisms isolated, including **MRSA in 13.8%** of cases; *Stenotrophomonas maltophilia* (41.6%) and MDR *Pseudomonas* (25%) were more common. [D — PMC10405369]
- **Greek ICU three-year C. auris study (32 patients):** crude mortality 50% in the six candidemia cases, but death was attributable to *C. auris* in only one — "in the rest of cases, death was associated with multidrug-resistant bacteria." [D — PMC12030536]
- **Mixed Candida/bacterial bloodstream infections generally:** in a retrospective study, *S. aureus* was 8.2% (7/85) of concomitant bacteria in mixed Candida/bacterial BSIs; *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (30.6%) led. In a separate *C. albicans*/bacterial BSI cohort, *S. aureus* was 16.0% of copathogens. [D — PMC9628097; PMC7648279]
- A case report of MRSA + *C. albicans* bloodstream co-infection notes as a learning point that "*Candida* spp. seem to facilitate *Staphylococcus aureus* concomitant infection." [D — PMC7806297 — note: *albicans*, not *auris*; do not over-read]

**Honest line on this section:** the literature documents *pairing* — shared biofilms, shared patients, shared ICUs. It does not document that one causes the other, or that the pairing is specific to *auris* rather than a general Candida–staph phenomenon.

## 3. The serotype biofilm study (the one his file referenced)

His 23-row comparison file cites "the mixed *S. aureus* biofilm study (serotypes 3, 14, 18C, 19F, 19A)." The study is real and identified:

- **Frontiers in Immunology, 2024:** "PCV13 vaccine prevents pneumococcal biofilms without affecting *Staphylococcus aureus* population within the polymicrobial biofilm." doi:10.3389/fimmu.2024.1495932 [D]
- What it did: opsonophagocytosis assays on monospecific biofilms and **S. aureus–S. pneumoniae mixed biofilms**, using pre/post-immune serum from PCV13-vaccinated adults. [D]
- What it found: immune sera reduced pneumococcal biofilms of **serotypes 3, 14, 18C, 19F, and 19A** (all PCV13 types), but not non-PCV13 serotypes 8, 11A, 24F. **PCV13 serum had no effect on the S. aureus population** in monospecific biofilms (MSSA or MRSA) **or** in the mixed biofilm — while it did reduce the pneumococcal 19A population *within* the mixed biofilm. [D]
- Companion finding: in nasopharyngeal carriage studies, **serotype 19A had the highest S. aureus co-carriage rate** among pneumococcal serotypes in day-care children. [D — *Epidemiology and Infection*, Cambridge, doi via 8699F1BE216081480E87E425D504F587]
- Related: antioxidants N-acetylcysteine and cysteamine cleared mixed MSSA/MRSA–*S. pneumoniae* biofilms in vitro (5 mg/ml NAC nearly eradicated the pneumococcus, killed ~94% MSSA and ~99% MRSA); methicillin-resistance background did not change the effect. [D — PMC9035182]

**Why it matters here:** this is the documented three-way — pneumococcal serotype × *S. aureus* × biofilm — sitting underneath his serotype work. It does not mention *C. auris*; the auris–aureus link and the serotype–aureus link are two separate documented pairings, not one triangle. [D]

## 4. The other "aureuses"

**Aspergillus aureus — the golden mold** [D]
- A real species: *Aspergillus aureus* Nakazawa. The name comes from colony color — Nakazawa isolated molds from awamori koji in Okinawa and named the black cultures *A. awamori* and the **golden-yellow** cultures *A. aureus*. [D — PLOS ONE 2013, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0063769; PMC3665839]
- Still isolated today (e.g., from biodeterioration of monuments; ITS-confirmed as *A. aureus* strain NMEC–SW.8). [D — PMC11540613]
- Clinical relevance: minor — an environmental/industrial mold, not a major human pathogen. Included because it is the *fungal* "aureus": same epithet, same golden-color logic as *S. aureus*, no relation to either. [D]

**The epithet across the tree of life** [D]
- *aureus* as a species epithet is used far beyond microbes: *Scleropages aureus* (a fish, Asian arowana), *Senecio aureus* (golden ragwort, a plant), *Sericulus aureus* (flame bowerbird), *Somatogyrus aureus* (golden pebblesnail). [D — Wikipedia, "S. aureus (disambiguation)"]
- The point for the namology thread: *aureus* is a **descriptor** (golden), not a lineage marker. Taxonomy reuses it everywhere something looks gold. Shared epithet ≠ shared biology — the same lesson as the auris/aureus collision, one level up.

**Honest line:** among clinically relevant pathogens, *Staphylococcus aureus* is the only "aureus" that matters. The rest are color descriptions wearing Latin.

## 6. Honest lines (do not drop)

1. **auris ≠ aureus.** Ear vs. golden. Two Latin words, no shared root, no shared lineage. The near-homonym is coincidence — and a live collision risk in clinical communication, since both are ICU bloodstream pathogens.
2. ***S. aureus* is a bacterium.** Gram-positive coccus, grape-like clusters, golden colonies. Staphylococci do not have serotypes.
3. **Co-occurrence is not causation.** Biofilm co-culture, co-carriage, and co-infection document that the organisms appear together. None of it shows that one causes, becomes, or explains the other.
4. **The auris–aureus pairing is not auris-specific.** The documented Candida–staph pairing literature is mostly *albicans* and *tropicalis*; the *auris*–*aureus* dual-biofilm work is newer and thinner ("one of the few" studies, per its own authors).
