# Fungi — Serotype Relationships and Fluid-Borne Sickness Associations (2026-10-16)

*Researched at Douglas's direction for The Mycelial Transposition. Association reference only — never medical advice, no treatment or diagnostic content.*

**His request, interpreted:** "associate fungi with all known things that can make you sick liquid-wise" is read here as **bodily-fluid-associated sickness** — blood, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), sputum/respiratory secretions, and urine. That interpretation is stated so the book can keep or correct it.

**Scope note:** two companion files already cover the narrow ground — *Serotype Connections — Associated Bacteria and Fungi* (bacteria/fungi vs. serotypes; honest thin spots) and *Serotype–Yeast Associations* (Candida/pneumo: one case report; Candida auris: one sequential case; Cryptococcus: two co-infections). This file goes **broader**: fungi in the bloodstream literature and fungi mapped by fluid. Nothing invented; thin spots marked.

## (a) Fungi × the 23 serotypes — what exists

| Fungus | Serotype relationship | Nature of link | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Candida* spp. | None at serotype level | One co-infection case report (*Candida* + *S. pneumoniae* lung ball); no per-serotype data | See *Serotype–Yeast Associations* (2026-10-16) |
| *Candida auris* | None at serotype level | One sequential-infection case (pneumococcal meningitis → *C. auris* fungemia); shared risk, not interaction | See *Serotype–Yeast Associations* (2026-10-16) |
| *Cryptococcus neoformans* | None at serotype level | Two documented co-infections; one false-positive panel | See *Serotype–Yeast Associations* (2026-10-16) |
| Any fungus | Serotype 14 detected in pleural effusion + blood alongside respiratory virus (one severe-pneumonia case) | Single-patient translational finding, not a general association | *Sci. Reports* (IP-10 pneumonia study) |

**Verdict:** no fungus maps to any specific serotype in the literature. The serotype–fungus relationship, at any resolution, is his to build.

## (b) Fungi × blood sicknesses

| Blood sickness | Fungal picture | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Candidemia** (Candida bloodstream infection) | ~23,000 US cases/year; incidence rising (42.3 → 51.2 per 100,000 admissions, 2015–2024); 30-day mortality ~31.5% | *C. albicans* leads (~40%); up to 95% of cases from five species: *C. albicans, C. glabrata* (*Nakaseomyces glabratus*), *C. parapsilosis, C. tropicalis, C. krusei* (*Pichia kudriavzevii*); *C. auris* rose to 4.7% by 2024 | CDC; CIDRAP/Houston Methodist 2025 |
| **Fungemia vs. bacteremia** (sepsis cohorts) | Fungemia patients present sicker: higher max norepinephrine dose, trend to higher lactate, poorer tissue perfusion | Fungal infection is an **independent risk factor for worse sepsis prognosis** (MIMIC-IV); neutropenia raises fungemia odds (OR 3.21); predictors of fungemia: documented non-bloodstream fungal infection, pyuria, liver disease, Hickman catheter, bowel perforation, altered mental status | J. Med. Assoc. Thai (SICU sepsis); BMC Infect. Dis. (MIMIC-IV); Cureus (NIS neutropenia); prediction-rule study |
| **Systemic fungal infection in sepsis** | 9.22% of sepsis infection sites nationally; third-leading cause of sepsis mortality by weighted deaths (153,027) | Genitourinary and lower-respiratory sites lead; systemic fungal infection outranks primary bacteremia in mortality contribution | PLoS ONE (nationwide sepsis sites study) |
| **Cryptococcemia** | Blood cultures positive in 47–70% of HIV cryptococcal meningitis cases | Blood and CSF carry the same organism in disseminated disease | HIV OI guidelines (clinicalinfo.hiv.gov) |

## (c) Fungi × fluid-borne sickness agents, by fluid

| Fluid | Fungus | What the literature documents |
|---|---|---|
| **Blood** | *Candida* spp. (albicans, glabrata, parapsilosis, tropicalis, krusei) | Candidemia — the bloodstream yeast infections; species list and resistance trends above |
| **Blood** | *Candida auris* | Multidrug-resistant yeast; rising share of candidemia (4.7% by 2024); concurrent bacterial BSIs in *C. auris* patients are shared-risk statistics, not interactions |
| **Blood** | *Cryptococcus neoformans* | Positive blood cultures in disseminated/HIV-associated disease |
| **CSF** | *Cryptococcus neoformans* | Cryptococcal meningitis: ~152,000 cases/year globally in people with HIV (~112,000 deaths); diagnosed by CSF India ink (encapsulated yeasts), cryptococcal antigen (CrAg), culture, PCR; opening pressure ≥25 cm CSF in 60–80% | EAT-g/Lancet model; HIV OI guidelines |
| **CSF** | *Coccidioides* spp. | Coccidioidal meningitis: lymphocytic pleocytosis (91%), CSF IgG detected (86%), CSF complement-fixation titers typically 1:4–1:16; hydrocephalus in ~31%; serum CF usually higher (1:16–1:64); serial CSF titers follow the disease course | PMC (133-patient meningitis cohort); UC Davis Valley Fever lab |
| **CSF** | *Candida* spp. | Candida meningitis exists but is far less documented than cryptococcal or coccidioidal meningitis (unverified beyond general reviews) |
| **Sputum / respiratory** | *Aspergillus* spp. | Sputum culture + sputum galactomannan (cut-offs ~0.67–0.71) distinguish colonization from chronic/invasive pulmonary aspergillosis; serum galactomannan and BAL galactomannan are the blood/airway antigen markers | *Sci. Reports* (CF cohort); *J. Fungi*/MDPI; PMC (EORTC/MSG criteria) |
| **Sputum / respiratory** | *Coccidioides* spp. | Sputum culture is a definitive diagnostic method — the organism grows on common media in 3–5 days (BSL-3); this is the test family behind Douglas's great-grandma's account | Medscape workup |
| **Sputum / respiratory** | *Pneumocystis jirovecii* | Diagnosed from induced sputum (up to 80% sensitive) or bronchoalveolar lavage (>95%); blood marker is serum (1→3)-β-D-glucan | Sanford/EACS; MSD Manual |
| **Sputum / respiratory** | *Histoplasma capsulatum* | Endemic dimorphic fungus; respiratory exposure route (unverified beyond general fungal lists) |
| **Sputum / respiratory** | *Candida* spp. | Airway Candida in pneumonia/ICU cohorts is usually tracheobronchial **colonization, not infection** — even when *S. pneumoniae* is the pneumonia pathogen | Autopsy/cohort literature (see *Serotype–Yeast Associations*) |
| **Urine** | *Candida* spp. (candiduria) | Usually **colonization, not infection** (catheter, vaginal contamination); IDSA: do not treat asymptomatic candiduria; catheter removal alone clears ~40–50%; progression candiduria → candidemia only 1–8% even in high-risk patients | IDSA 2016; ECMM/ISHAM/ASM 2025; Wiley case review |

## What was NOT found

- **No fungus–serotype relationships exist in the literature** for any of the 23 serotypes. The single serotype-14 fluid detection is one patient, not an association.
- **No studied mechanism** links any yeast or mold to pneumococcal carriage, biofilm, or virulence at serotype resolution.
- **No respiratory-mycobiome carriage studies** link airway yeasts to pneumococcal serotype carriage.
- **No general "sick bugs leaching shared products" framework** exists as a studied mechanism; the closest documented footholds remain metabolic cross-feeding in mixed biofilms and immunoglobulin embedded in biofilm matrices (filed in *Serotype–Yeast Associations*).

## For the book

The fluids are where Douglas's thesis already lives in practice: blood cultures grow the yeasts, CSF titers track the meningitis, sputum grows the *Coccidioides* — his great-grandma's story is the family version of section (c). The documented map is fluid-by-fluid and organism-by-organism; the **serotype column is empty everywhere**, which is precisely the gap his two-panel synthesis claims to fill. Old and new together: the CF titer his great-grandma's era used is the same test UC Davis runs today.
