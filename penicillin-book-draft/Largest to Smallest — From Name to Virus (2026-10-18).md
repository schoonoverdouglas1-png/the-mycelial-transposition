# Largest to Smallest — From Name to Virus (2026-10-18)

**Filed:** 2026-10-18 · For *The Mycelial Transposition* (creative manuscript)
**Frame:** educational manuscript reference only. Never medical advice. No diagnosis, no application to any person.

## The scale [H — his frame]

Largest to smallest: **NAME** (largest — American name + Latin name, both kept) → **DISEASE** (the entity) → **SYMPTOMOLOGY** (the showing) → **BACTERIA / FUNGI / YEAST / MOLD** (the organisms in between) → **VIRUS** (smallest — the small pieces). The names are literally connected with the viruses as the small pieces; the organisms are stuck to the chain in between [H]. Every entry below walks the scale and breaks the name into pieces.

**Naming directive [H]:** "Lou Gehrig's disease" first, Latin second — American first, Latin second, medicine/science after.

## Entry 0 — Throat distemper × KLB × *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* (template)

- **NAME pieces:** American — *throat* (site) + *distemper* (disordered state, from Latin *distemperare*, to mix badly) [D]. Latin — *coryne-* (club, Greek *korune*) + *bacterium* (rod) [D]; *diphtheriae* (of diphtheria, Greek *diphthera*, leather — the membrane) [D]. KLB — *Klebs-Löffler Bacillus*: the discoverers' hands + *bacillus* (rod, Latin *baculus*, staff) [D].
- **Connections:** site + disorder (American) meets shape + membrane (Latin) meets hands + rod (KLB). Three namings, three different informations, one throat.
- **Parallels:** *distemper* parallels every disordered-state name (sore throat distemper); *bacillus* parallels the whole rod family (*B. anthracis*, *Klebsiella*); *diphthera* (leather) parallels every membrane name (bladder in throat, membranous croup) [D on the parallels].
- **Scale walk:** NAME (three kept) → DISEASE (diphtheria) → SYMPTOMOLOGY (the closing throat, the membrane) → ORGANISMS (*C. diphtheriae*, *S. pyogenes*; *Candida albicans* as the membrane differential) → VIRUS [GAP].

## Entry 1 — Consumption × *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*

- **NAME pieces:** American — *con-* (thoroughly) + *sumere* (to take): "to take completely" — the disease that takes the whole person [D]. Latin — *myco-* (fungus/waxy, Greek *mykes*) + *bacterium* (rod) [D]; *tuberculosis* (of the tubercles, Latin *tuberculum*, small swelling) [D].
- **Connections:** the American name says what the disease DOES (consumes); the Latin says what the organism IS (waxy rod) and what it MAKES (tubercles). Action meets structure meets product.
- **Parallels:** *consumption* parallels galloping consumption, white plague, marasmus, phthisis — the whole wasting family [D]; *myco-* (waxy) parallels the acid-fast staining that makes TB visible [D]; *tubercle* parallels every granuloma name in the list [H→D].

## The parallels — pieces that echo across more than one thing [D on the recurrences; his lens on the readings]

**Site pieces** (brain, lung, throat, hip, belly, womb, neck): the same site recurs across different agents — lung in lung fever (*S. pneumoniae*) and lung sickness (*M. tuberculosis*); throat in five diphtheria names. Site is the American track; agent is the Latin track [D].

**Showing pieces** (fever, pox, flux, spots, colors): fever attaches to nine entries with a different qualifier each — the fever family is the list's largest parallel structure [D]. Colors (yellow, white, black, green) diagnose across entries [D].

**Shape pieces** (bacillus/rod, coccus/berry, twisted, club, corkscrew, spindle): the shape vocabulary is almost entirely Latin-side — the American names rarely say shape (bladder and eel excepted). His "bacillus tells the family" thesis sits here [H→D].

**Action pieces** (falling, seizing, gripping, galloping, creeping, dancing): the American verbs diagnose by behavior — the behavior-as-diagnosis family [H].

**Eponym pieces** (Neisseria, Rickettsia, Brucella, Shigella, Bordetella, Klebsiella, Yersinia, Salmonella, Listeria, Jírovec): the Latin side memorializes discoverers; the American side memorializes patients, trades, and places. Two different memorials, one list [D].

**The chain, smallest end:** where the virus is known, it is the smallest named piece — rabies virus (*lyssa*, rage), dengue (*dinga*, cramp), parainfluenza (beside-influenza), poliovirus (gray matter), influenza, yellow fever virus. The small pieces carry symptom-words too (*lyssa*, *dinga*) — the American track reaches all the way down [H→D].
