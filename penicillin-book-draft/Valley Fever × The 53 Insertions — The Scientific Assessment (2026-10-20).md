## 2. The documented record the grading stands on

Verified Oct 20, 2026. Everything in the grading table below draws on this base and on the Long List's own bacterial and fungal columns.

- **Geography [D].** *Coccidioides* is a soil fungus of hot, dry regions — the southwestern US (Arizona, California, New Mexico, Utah, Nevada, western Texas), south-central Washington, Mexico, and parts of Central and South America. **~97% of reported US cases are from Arizona and California** (CDC MMWR, PMC5687785; StatPearls, Coccidioidomycosis). It is **not transmitted person-to-person** [D] (StatPearls).
- **The pneumonia share [D].** Coccidioidomycosis accounts for an estimated **15–30% of community-acquired pneumonia in the Phoenix and Tucson metropolitan areas** (StatPearls; a contemporary review gives 17–29%). A Mayo Clinic Phoenix review of 1,141 adults hospitalized for CAP (Nov 2024–Oct 2025) found 76% were tested for *Coccidioides* and **10% of those tested were positive** — the most frequently identified cause among tested patients (CIDRAP, reporting *Open Forum Infectious Diseases*).
- **The TB mimicry [D].** Chronic fibrocavitary coccidioidal pneumonia mimics tuberculosis radiologically and clinically; **miliary coccidioidomycosis produces 3–4 mm nodules indistinguishable from miliary tuberculosis** (IBCC Coccidioidomycosis, citing Murray 2022). Granulomas form in both [D]. Cocci is called "the other great imitator" in the literature [D].
- **The meningitis [D].** Dissemination occurs in ~1% of infections; **up to 50% of disseminated cases have meningeal involvement**, usually within 3 months of the initial presentation (Medscape, "Two Enigmatic Cases of Meningitis"). Coccidioidal meningitis is a chronic granulomatous, typically **basilar** meningitis that **presents like tuberculous meningitis** — the CSF profiles overlap (lymphocytic pleocytosis, low glucose, high protein) [D]. The separating tell: **CSF eosinophilia**, seen with 6–20% eosinophils in a lymphocyte-predominant CSF, "particularly with coccidioidal meningitis," and very rare in TB meningitis [D] (Neupsy Key, Chronic and Recurrent Meningitis). CSF cultures are positive in no more than half of cases [D]. Hydrocephalus complicates 30–50% of coccidioidal meningitis; **cerebral infarction from vasculitis appears in 15–20%** [D] (Medscape reference). Untreated, coccidioidal meningitis is almost invariably fatal [D].
- **Dissemination sites [D].** Of disseminated cases: skin/subcutaneous tissue ~50%, neurologic ~33%, bone and joint ~25% (IBCC). **Skeletal involvement accounts for 20–50% of extrapulmonary dissemination, with the vertebrae the most common skeletal site** (Nakhla 2018, *Case Reports in Infectious Diseases*). Lymph nodes, spleen, liver, kidneys, and adrenal glands are also documented dissemination sites (Adam et al. series, via PMC osteoarticular review). Bone disease mimics malignancy and tuberculosis [D].
- **The encephalitis seam [D/GAP].** The documented CNS disease is meningitis. The literature does use "meningoencephalitis" for coccidioidal cases with parenchymal features, and a *C. posadasii* meningoencephalitis case in an immunocompetent host is published [D, per the Oct 18 Connections paper]. A primary entity "coccidioidal encephalitis" is not established **[GAP]**. The leg is **partial** — carried by meningeal extension — and is reported as partial throughout this paper, per his standing rule: partials are examined, never discarded, never upgraded.
- **Jamestown Canyon virus [D].** An orthobunyavirus of the California serogroup, first isolated in 1961 from *Culiseta inornata* mosquitoes collected in Jamestown Canyon near Boulder, Colorado; white-tailed deer are the main amplifying host (CID 2025, JCV seroprevalence review). From 2011–2022, **282 cases were reported to CDC, 187 (66%) neuroinvasive, and 73% reported from Minnesota and Wisconsin** [D] (same source). CDC 2000–2013: 31 cases, ~two-thirds neuroinvasive, about half hospitalized, no deaths (Pastula et al.). JCV causes acute febrile illness, meningitis, or meningoencephalitis; it is widespread through temperate North America and likely under-recognized [D].
- **Bacterial company [D].** A published case (Fresno, California) documents **simultaneous bacterial (*Mycoplasma pneumoniae*), viral (coronavirus NL63), and fungal (*Coccidioides* spp.) pneumonia in one immunocompetent adult** (Medscape, 2025). BMJ Best Practice notes a coccidioidal cavity "may… become a nidus for suprainfection" [D]. Empiric antibacterials are the default first move in cocci CAP — 46% of Phoenix patients had diagnostic delays over a month while receiving them (PMC6711213).

## 4. The framework, tested three ways — expanded

### (a) As differential discipline — "think cocci": HOLDS [D]

Read as a rule of suspicion — *in every differential, in endemic country, ask the fungal question* — the insertion is not fringe; it is close to the documented local standard. The record: cocci is 15–30% of CAP in Phoenix/Tucson [D]; it mimics TB in the lung and TB meningitis in the head, with CSF eosinophilia as the documented tell [D]; it disseminates to bone and joints, where it mimics malignancy and TB again [D]; and the cost of *not* thinking of it is measured — 46% of Phoenix patients waited over a month for the diagnosis, receiving antibacterials that do nothing against a fungus [D]. The Mayo Phoenix CAP review's authors called for testing *all* pneumonia patients in endemic areas [D]. His insertion, read this way, is that discipline generalized from the pneumonia room to the whole table — an extension of documented practice, wearing his filing system. **Verdict: holds, and passes the 51% bar outright.**

### (b) As etiologic identity — "one name for four": DOES NOT HOLD as identity; HOLDS as umbrella

The prior verdict (Oct 18) stands, and this paper does not contradict it: **no** as universal cause, **no** as identity. Tuberculosis is mycobacterial [D]; meningitis has many documented causes [D]; pneumonia has many [D]; and cocci's own geography (~97% of US cases in two states [D]) breaks any universal claim before it starts — for a patient with no endemic exposure, cocci is not in every differential, and the entries graded FORMAL above are the grading-table form of that break. **Yes** as the fungal-element umbrella over the big four: valley fever names the fungal side of the four — the fungal pneumonia (solid [D]), the fungal meningitis (solid [D]), the TB-mimicking fungal disease (solid as mimicry [D]), and the encephalitis leg **PARTIAL** — cocci's CNS disease is meningitis; parenchymal involvement comes by extension and under the name meningoencephalitis in case literature [D]; a primary coccidioidal encephalitis entity remains [GAP]. Partial reported as partial, per his standing rule — examined, not discarded, not upgraded. His own Oct 18 correction — removing syphilis from the four when no cocci link could be documented — is what keeps this form honest: the four that remain are the four where legs exist.

### (c) Under his updated framing — valley fever = CONNECTION, Jamestown Canyon = the disease: THE STRONGEST VERSION [H→D]

On Oct 18 (~9:45 PM) Douglas restated the framework (Thesis Note, filed in his words): *"Jamestown Canyon Creek fever is a disease on its own. Valley fever is a connection — not a standalone disease entity in his framework, but the fungal thread that ties things together."* [H]

This update is the scientifically strongest version of his framework, for three reasons, each checkable:

**Reason 1 — it changes the claim type from identity to contact, and contact is gradable.** An identity claim ("cocci IS this entry's disease") is true or false, and it is false at 45 of 53 sites. A connection claim ("cocci belongs in the web around this entry") is judged by documented contact — mimicry, co-infection, co-presence, shared ground — which comes in degrees. The grading table in §3 is only possible under the updated framing: the same insertion that fails as identity at entry 0 *stands, honestly labeled formal,* as connection-by-rule. The update converts a claim that breaks into a claim that can be mapped.

**Reason 2 — it matches how each organism actually behaves in the record.** Jamestown Canyon virus is documented as a standalone disease entity in exactly his sense: a named virus, a named disease, 282 reported cases 2011–2022, two-thirds neuroinvasive, diagnosed as itself [D]. Valley fever, by contrast, is documented as the thing found *while looking for something else* — the pneumonia that doesn't respond to antibacterials, the "TB" that isn't, the chronic meningitis that turns out fungal on the eosinophil tell [D]. The 46%-delayed-diagnosis figure [D] is the statistical shape of a connection: cocci is encountered inside other diseases' workups. His framing assigns each organism the role the record shows it playing.

**Reason 3 — it survives the geography break that killed the identity form.** Identity required cocci everywhere; the map says AZ/CA [D]. Connection requires only that where cocci *is*, it connects — and that the discipline of asking travels with the patient (travel-related cases outside endemic areas are documented [D]). A connection can be absent at a site without the framework breaking; an identity cannot. The FORMAL grades in §3 are therefore not refutations of the updated framework — they are its honest zero-points, the places where the thread, this time, has nothing documented to tie to. A framework that can say "nothing here" at entry 52 and "iron" at entry 5 is a framework making distinctions, and distinctions are what make a framework scientific rather than incantatory.
