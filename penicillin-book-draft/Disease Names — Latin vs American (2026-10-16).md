# Disease Names — Latin vs American (2026-10-16)

*Manuscript reference for The Mycelial Transposition. Each entry pairs the Latin/scientific name with the American common name and tells the naming story: whether the American name kept, translated, or replaced the Latin one, and why. **Scope note:** this is a working core list of the diseases Douglas knows best — from his own history, his research notes, and everyday life — not literally every known disease (the ICD catalogs tens of thousands). **This is a naming reference only — never medical advice, and it contains no treatment or diagnostic information.***

**Label key — "Same or different?"**
- **same** — the American name kept the Latin/scientific form, sometimes clipped (flu, MS, A-fib)
- **translated** — the American name renders the Latin meaning in plain English (high blood pressure, kidney stones)
- **eponym** — named for a person (Lou Gehrig's, Parkinson's)
- **geographic** — named for a place (Lyme disease)
- **different** — the American name comes from a different image or story altogether (ringworm, shingles)

## Infectious diseases

| Latin / scientific name | American common name(s) | Same or different? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tuberculosis | TB | same | Latin *tuberculum* = small swelling or nodule — the disease is literally named for its small nodules (tubercles). |
| Pertussis | whooping cough | translated | Latin *per-* (intense) + *tussis* (cough); the American name describes the whooping sound of the cough instead. |
| Rubeola | measles | translated | Latin *rubeola* = "little red one"; the origin of the English word "measles" is uncertain (possibly Middle Dutch *masel*, "spot"). |
| Rubella | German measles | geographic | Latin *rubella* = "little red one"; "German" because German physicians first distinguished it from measles. |
| Varicella | chickenpox | different | Latin *varicella* = diminutive of *variola* (smallpox); "chickenpox" origin is debated — chickpea-sized lesions, a "chicken" (mild) smallpox, or Old English *giccin* (itch). |
| Variola (virus) | smallpox | different | Latin *varius* = spotted; "smallpox" distinguished it from the "great pox" (syphilis). |
| Vaccinia (virus) | cowpox | different | Latin *vacca* = cow; English "cowpox" says the same thing — and "vaccine" / "vaccination" come from this word. |
| Mumps orthorubulavirus | mumps | different | Taxonomic Latin name vs. 16th-century English *mump* (a grimace) — named for the swollen face. |
| *Borrelia burgdorferi* infection | Lyme disease | geographic | Named for Lyme, Connecticut (1975); the organism itself honors discoverer Willy Burgdorfer (an eponym inside the Latin). |
| Candidiasis | yeast infection / thrush | translated | *Candida* from Latin *candidus* (white) + *-iasis* (condition); American names describe the white patches (thrush) or the organism (yeast). |
| *Candidozyma auris* (syn. *Candida auris*) | Candida auris | same | Renamed under the recent taxonomic revision; American reporting still uses "Candida auris." |
| *Streptococcus pneumoniae* infection | pneumonia / pneumococcal pneumonia | same | "Pneumonia" from Greek *pneumōn* (lung); American kept the ancient word and adds the organism. |
| Influenza | flu | same | Italian for "influence" (of the stars); American "flu" is just clipped. |
| Cholera | cholera | same | Greek *cholera* from *cholē* (bile) — an ancient name the Americans kept. |
| Malaria | malaria | same | Italian *mala aria* ("bad air") — a disproven miasma theory fossilized in the name. |
| *Streptococcus pyogenes* pharyngitis | strep throat | different | American clips "Streptococcus" to "strep" and names the site; it doesn't translate *pyogenes* ("pus-producing"). |
| Coryza (rhinovirus infection) | common cold | different | Medical Latin *coryza* (Greek *koryza*, head cold) fell out of use; "common cold" marks how ordinary it is. |
| Gastroenteritis (viral) | stomach flu | different | Greek *gastro-* + *entero-* (stomach, intestine) + *-itis*; "stomach flu" borrows "flu" from influenza — no flu virus involved. |
| *Clostridioides difficile* infection | C. diff | same | American name is just the scientific name clipped to an initialism. |
| Osteomyelitis | osteomyelitis / bone infection | same | *Osteo-* (bone) + *myelos* (marrow) + *-itis*; American medicine usually says "osteomyelitis" — "bone infection" is the plain translation. |
| Otitis media | ear infection / middle ear infection | translated | Greek *oto-* (ear) + *-itis*, *media* (middle); the American name translates it. |
| Sinusitis | sinus infection | translated | "Sinus" kept; "-itis" becomes "infection." |

## Neurological

| Latin / scientific name | American common name(s) | Same or different? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Encephalitis | encephalitis | same | Greek *enkephalos* (brain) + *-itis*; Americans kept the Latin/Greek form. |
| Encephalopathy | encephalopathy | same | *Enkephalo-* (brain) + *-pathy* (suffering); kept. |
| Meningitis | meningitis | same | *Meninx* (membrane) + *-itis*; kept. |
| Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis | Lou Gehrig's disease / ALS | eponym | Clinical name from Greek roots (*a-* no, *myo-* muscle, *trophē* nourishment; lateral sclerosis = side hardening); American common name honors baseball player Lou Gehrig, diagnosed 1939. |
| Multiple sclerosis | MS | same | Greek/Latin *sclerosis* (hardening); American clips to initials. |
| Parkinson's disease | Parkinson's | eponym | Named for James Parkinson, who described it in 1817. |
| Alzheimer's disease | Alzheimer's | eponym | Named for Alois Alzheimer, who described it in 1906. |
| Epilepsy | epilepsy | same | Greek *epilēpsia* (seizure, literally "a taking hold"); American kept the ancient word. |

## Cardiovascular

| Latin / scientific name | American common name(s) | Same or different? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertension | high blood pressure | translated | *Hyper-* (over) + *tensio* (tension, stretching); the American name translates to plain words. |
| Myocardial infarction | heart attack | translated | *Myo-* (muscle) + *cardia* (heart) + *infarctus* (stuffed, blocked); "heart attack" names the event, not the tissue. |
| Atherosclerosis | hardening of the arteries | translated | Greek *athero-* (gruel, paste) + *sclerosis* (hardening); the American name describes the effect. |
| Angina pectoris | angina | same | Latin *angina* (choking, strangling) + *pectoris* (of the chest); American clips it. |
| Atrial fibrillation | A-fib | same | Clipped to initials; same name underneath. |

## Endocrine and metabolic

| Latin / scientific name | American common name(s) | Same or different? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diabetes mellitus | diabetes | same | Greek *dia-* (through) + *bainein* (to go) — "a siphon"; Latin *mellitus* (honey-sweet, for the sugary urine); American clips it. |
| Diabetes insipidus | diabetes insipidus | same | *Insipidus* (Latin, tasteless) vs. *mellitus* (honey-sweet) — the Latin names the urine's taste. |
| Hypothyroidism | underactive thyroid / low thyroid | translated | *Hypo-* (under) + thyroid (Greek *thyreoeides*, shield-shaped); American translates. |
| Hyperthyroidism | overactive thyroid | translated | *Hyper-* (over); American translates. |
| Gout | gout | same | From Latin *gutta* (drop) via Old French — medieval belief that humors "dropped" into the joints; the origin is forgotten but the word survived. |

## Autoimmune and rheumatologic

| Latin / scientific name | American common name(s) | Same or different? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rheumatoid arthritis | rheumatoid arthritis / RA | same | Greek *rheuma* (flowing, of humors) + *arthritis* (joint inflammation); American clips to initials. |
| Osteoarthritis | arthritis | same | *Osteo-* (bone) + arthritis; American speech usually just says "arthritis" and drops the *osteo-*. |
| Systemic lupus erythematosus | lupus | same | Latin *lupus* (wolf) — the facial rash was thought to resemble wolf bites; American clips it. |
| Psoriasis | psoriasis | same | Greek *psōra* (itch); kept. |

## Cancers

| Latin / scientific name | American common name(s) | Same or different? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carcinoma | cancer | same | Greek *karkinos* (crab, Hippocrates) became Latin *carcinoma*; American "cancer" is the Latin word anglicized. |
| Melanoma | melanoma / skin cancer | same | Greek *melas* (black) + *-oma* (tumor); American often just says "skin cancer." |
| Leukemia | leukemia / blood cancer | same | Greek *leukos* (white) + *haima* (blood); "blood cancer" is plain English. |
| Lymphoma | lymphoma | same | Lymph + *-oma*; kept. |
| Osteosarcoma | bone cancer | translated | *Osteo-* (bone) + *sarx* (flesh) + *-oma*; American translates. (Naming note for the book: a distinct category from osteomyelitis in standard medicine.) |

## Skin

| Latin / scientific name | American common name(s) | Same or different? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tinea corporis | ringworm | different | "Ringworm" is a folk name for the ring-shaped rash — no worm is involved; Latin *tinea* (gnawing moth) + *corporis* (of the body). |
| Onychomycosis | toenail fungus | translated | Greek *onycho-* (nail) + *mykōsis* (fungal); American translates. |
| Tinea pedis | athlete's foot | different | *Tinea pedis* = "foot tinea"; the American name comes from its spread in athletes' locker rooms. |
| Herpes zoster | shingles | different | *Herpes* (Greek, creeping) + *zoster* (girdle); "shingles" comes from Latin *cingulum* (belt) — the same belt image, arrived through a different door. |
| Herpes simplex | cold sores / fever blisters | different | *Herpes* = creeping (Greek); American names the site and the sensation. |
| Dermatitis | eczema | different | *Dermatitis* = skin inflammation; "eczema" from Greek *ekzein* (to boil over) — two Greek names for overlapping rashes. |
| Cellulitis | cellulitis | same | Latin *cellula* (small cell) + *-itis*; kept. |
| Acne vulgaris | acne | same | Greek *akmē* (point, peak); *vulgaris* (common); American clips it. |
