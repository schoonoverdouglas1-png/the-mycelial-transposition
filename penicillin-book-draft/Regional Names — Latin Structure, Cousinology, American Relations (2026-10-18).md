# Regional Names — Latin Structure, Cousinology, American Relations

**Filed:** 2026-10-18 · For *The Mycelial Transposition* (creative manuscript)
**Frame:** educational manuscript reference only. Never medical advice. No diagnosis, no application to any person.

## His commission [H]

Investigate the REGIONAL disease names — the ones with the same Latin structure, Latin cousinology (his coinage: the cousin-relations among Latin names), and Latin relations to the American naming of the same bacteria/bacterium/organism — and so on and so forth.

**Method:** every regional name below carries (1) the American regional name, (2) the Latin structure — what the Latin name actually says (place? man? shape? showing?), (3) the Latin cousinology — how the Latin names relate as cousins, (4) the Latin↔American relation for the same organism — where they translate each other and where they describe different things.

## Part 1 — The encephalitis cousins

### St. Louis encephalitis
- **American regional name:** St. Louis encephalitis — named for St. Louis, Missouri, where the first and largest epidemic occurred in 1933; virus isolated from human brain that year [D].
- **Latin structure:** *St. Louis encephalitis virus* — the Latin says nothing here; the place-name stands inside the Latin. Place, not shape, not showing [D].
- **Cousinology:** flavivirus, closely related to West Nile virus [D]. Cousin to the whole encephalitis family below.
- **Latin↔American:** both say the place. The American and the Latin agree — which is exactly the SLE/SLE collision: "SLE" is also systemic lupus erythematosus, and both sit on his arbopanel [D]. The shared acronym is real and documented — his namology catch, filed in the osteomyelosis thesis note.

### Western equine encephalitis
- **American regional name:** Western equine encephalitis (WEE) — first arbovirus work in the U.S.; virus isolated in California in 1930 by Karl Meyer from horse brain [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Western equine encephalitis virus* — PLACE (western) + ANIMAL (equine, horse) + SHOWING (encephalitis). Three pieces of information in one name [D].
- **Cousinology:** alphavirus; cousin to EEE and VEE — the three were separated geographically as distinct viruses in 1933 [D].
- **Latin↔American:** identical. The Latin carries the American place straight through.

### Eastern equine encephalitis
- **American regional name:** Eastern equine encephalitis (EEE) — first recorded in horses in Massachusetts in 1831 (75 horses died); virus isolated from horse brains in the coastal Delaware/Maryland/New Jersey/Virginia epizootic of 1933; first human cases 1938 in Massachusetts (38 cases, 25 dead) [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Eastern equine encephalomyelitis virus* — PLACE (eastern) + ANIMAL (equine) + SHOWING (encephalomyelitis — brain AND spinal cord) [D].
- **Cousinology:** alphavirus, the eastern cousin of WEE; Madariaga virus (Argentina, 1936, from a horse) is the South American cousin [D].
- **Latin↔American:** identical — except the Latin adds the *-myelitis* the American drops. The spinal cord is in the Latin and not in the American [D].

### Venezuelan equine encephalitis
- **American regional name:** Venezuelan equine encephalitis (VEE) — outbreaks in horses in Central America, South America, Mexico, occasionally the southern U.S. [D].
- **Latin structure:** PLACE (Venezuela) + ANIMAL (equine) + SHOWING (encephalitis) [D].
- **Cousinology:** alphavirus, the southern cousin of EEE/WEE [D].
- **Latin↔American:** identical. Three cousins, three compass points: western, eastern, Venezuelan [LOGIC — the pattern is mine, labeled].

### La Crosse encephalitis
- **American regional name:** La Crosse encephalitis — named for La Crosse, Wisconsin, where the virus was isolated in the early 1960s (1960–1964) [D]. "La Crosse" is French — *la crosse*, the hooked stick — not Latin [D].
- **Latin structure:** *La Crosse virus*, species *Orthobunyavirus lacrosseense* — the Latin says the PLACE (in French). California encephalitis virus is the species-group name under which it sits [D].
- **Cousinology:** orthobunyavirus, California serogroup — literal cousin of Jamestown Canyon virus [D]. His arbopanel #1 (Sep 16, 2025, Shannon Brown) carried California (La Crosse) IgG at 1:20, flagged abnormal [D, from his record].
- **Latin↔American:** the American says the French town; the Latin says the California group. Place in both, but DIFFERENT places — Wisconsin vs California [D]. The cousinology bridges them.

### Jamestown Canyon virus
- **American regional name:** Jamestown Canyon — first isolated 1961 from *Culiseta inornata* mosquitoes in Jamestown Canyon, Colorado [D, from the family-relations paper].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthobunyavirus jamestownense* — PLACE again [D].
- **Cousinology:** California serogroup — the Colorado cousin of the Wisconsin La Crosse virus [D]. Serologic cross-reactivity: cousins that mistake each other in the tube [D].
- **Latin↔American:** identical place. The brother/sister pair of the family-relations paper: Colorado brother 1961, Wisconsin sister 1960 — the place names doing the family work [H, his namology].

### West Nile virus
- **American regional name:** West Nile virus — from the West Nile province of Uganda, where the virus was isolated in 1937; first known use 1940 [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthoflavivirus nilense* — the Latin says the NILE (the river), the American says the WEST NILE (the province). The Latin kept the river and dropped the "west" [D].
- **Cousinology:** flavivirus — close cousin of St. Louis encephalitis virus [D]; cousin to Japanese encephalitis virus.
- **Latin↔American:** near-translation with a piece dropped. River in both [D].

### Japanese encephalitis
- **American regional name:** Japanese encephalitis — PLACE (Japan) + SHOWING [D]. Earliest record of epidemic viral encephalitis: Japan, 1871 [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthoflavivirus japonicum* — PLACE in Latin too [D].
- **Cousinology:** flavivirus — cousin to West Nile and St. Louis encephalitis [D].
- **Latin↔American:** the place translates cleanly.

### Powassan virus
- **American regional name:** Powassan virus — named for Powassan, Ontario, where the first known human case (a 5-year-old child with encephalitis) was found in 1958 [D]. Note the American name carries NO showing — just the town.
- **Latin structure:** *Orthoflavivirus powassanense* — PLACE [D]. Tick-borne (unlike its mosquito-borne flavivirus cousins) [D].
- **Cousinology:** flavivirus — cousin to West Nile and St. Louis encephalitis, but the tick-borne odd cousin [D].
- **Latin↔American:** identical place. The showing (encephalitis) is in the HISTORY, not in either name [D].

## Part 2 — The spotted-fever cousins (the Rickettsia family)

### Rocky Mountain spotted fever
- **American regional name:** Rocky Mountain spotted fever — recognized in the early 1800s; concentrated in Montana's Bitterroot Valley in the 1890s; first recorded Montana case 1873; called "black measles" for the characteristic rash [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Rickettsia rickettsii* — the MAN, twice: genus *Rickettsia* and species *rickettsii* both honor Howard T. Ricketts (1871–1910), the Chicago pathologist who identified the organism in 1906–1909 and died of typhus in Mexico in 1910 [D]. No place, no showing, no shape in the Latin — just the man [D].
- **Cousinology:** spotted fever group rickettsiae — cousin to *Rickettsia conorii* (Mediterranean spotted fever), *Rickettsia prowazekii* (typhus group) [D].
- **Latin↔American:** TOTAL DIVERGENCE — the American says PLACE + SHOWING (Rocky Mountains + spotted fever); the Latin says THE MAN (Ricketts, twice). The organism's name remembers the investigator; the disease's name remembers where it killed [LOGIC — the reading is mine, labeled].

### Mediterranean spotted fever
- **American regional name:** Mediterranean spotted fever — also called boutonneuse fever ("boutonneuse" = spotty/pimpled, French) [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Rickettsia conorii* — the MAN again: named for Conor [D, generally]. Genus says Ricketts; species says Conor.
- **Cousinology:** spotted fever group — cousin to *R. rickettsii* [D].
- **Latin↔American:** same divergence pattern: American = place + showing; Latin = men [LOGIC].

### Typhus (the cousin by genus)
- **American regional name:** typhus — from Greek *typhos*, smoke/haze (the clouded mind) [D, generally]. Jail fever, camp fever, ship fever — the American names say the CROWD, not the place [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Rickettsia prowazekii* — the MAN: Stanislaus von Prowazek [D]. Ricketts died of typhus — the genus-man killed by the cousin-organism [D].
- **Cousinology:** typhus group rickettsiae — cousin to the spotted fever group [D].
- **Latin↔American:** the American says the showing (smoke) or the crowd (jail/camp/ship); the Latin says the man [LOGIC].

## Part 3 — The valley and desert family

### Valley fever
- **American regional name:** valley fever — San Joaquin Valley fever; the American says the PLACE [D].
- **Latin structure:** *coccidioidomycosis* = *kokkis* (berry) + *-oides* (like) + *-mycosis* (fungal condition) = "the fungal condition of the berry-like ones" [D, from the word-broken-open paper]. The Latin says the SHAPE, not the place [D].
- **Cousinology:** the -mycosis idiom family — aspergillosis, candidiasis, cryptococcosis, histoplasmosis, blastomycosis, mucormycosis [D]. His thread: idiom hangs out with mycosis [H].
- **Latin↔American:** DIVERGENCE — American = place (the valley); Latin = shape (the berries). Neither says the showing. His thesis: valley fever as the one name for meningitis, encephalitis, pneumonia, tuberculosis [H] — filed in the thesis note with his transcription corrections.

### Desert rheumatism
- **American regional name:** desert rheumatism — ANOTHER American name for coccidioidomycosis [D, generally]. The American says the PLACE (desert) + the SHOWING (rheumatism — the joint pain) [D].
- **Latin structure:** same *coccidioidomycosis* — the shape [D].
- **Cousinology:** same -mycosis family [D].
- **Latin↔American:** one Latin, TWO Americans — valley fever (place) and desert rheumatism (place + showing). The additive-naming thesis in the wild: both American names kept, both true [H→D].

### Rift Valley fever
- **American regional name:** Rift Valley fever — the Rift Valley (Kenya) + fever [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** Rift Valley fever virus — place straight through [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** NOT a cousin of valley fever — different virus family entirely (phlebovirus), different continent, different disease. The shared word "valley" is coincidence, not cousinology [D, generally]. Filed so the record doesn't let the word do false family work [LOGIC — mine, labeled].

### Murray Valley encephalitis
- **American regional name:** Murray Valley encephalitis — the Murray Valley (Australia) + showing [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthoflavivirus murrayvalleyense* — place [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** flavivirus — cousin to West Nile, St. Louis encephalitis, Japanese encephalitis [D, generally].
- **Latin↔American:** place translates cleanly. The Australian cousin of the encephalitis family [LOGIC].

## Part 4 — The place-named viruses (the far family)

### Ebola
- **American regional name:** Ebola — named for the Ebola River near Yambuku, Zaire (now DRC), 1976. The commission deliberately chose the RIVER over the village (Yambuku) to avoid stigma — the Lassa precedent [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthoebolavirus* — the river, Latinized [D]. Filovirus: from Latin *filum*, "thread" — the SHAPE is in the family name [D].
- **Cousinology:** filovirus — cousin to Marburg virus [D].
- **Latin↔American:** the river in both. *L'Ebola* is a corruption of Ngbandi *Legbala*, "white water" or "pure water" [D] — the showing (pure water) buried three languages deep [LOGIC].

### Marburg
- **American regional name:** Marburg virus — Marburg, Germany, 1967 [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthomarburgvirus* — the PLACE [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** filovirus — the German cousin of Ebola [D].
- **Latin↔American:** identical place.

### Lassa
- **American regional name:** Lassa virus — Lassa, Nigeria [D, generally]. The naming that stigmatized the village — the reason Ebola got the river instead [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Lassa mammarenavirus* — the PLACE [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** arenavirus — cousin to Junín (Argentina), Machupo (Bolivia), Guanarito (Venezuela): the South American hemorrhagic fever cousins [D, generally].
- **Latin↔American:** identical place.

### Zika
- **American regional name:** Zika virus — the Zika forest, Uganda [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthoflavivirus zikaense* — the PLACE [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** flavivirus — cousin to West Nile, St. Louis encephalitis, Japanese encephalitis, Powassan [D, generally].
- **Latin↔American:** identical place. The forest cousin in the flavivirus family [LOGIC].

### Nipah and Hendra
- **American regional names:** Nipah virus (Nipah, Malaysia, 1998); Hendra virus (Hendra, Australia, 1994) [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Nipah henipavirus*, *Hendra henipavirus* — the places, Latinized [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** henipaviruses — the two are each other's closest cousins [D, generally].
- **Latin↔American:** identical places. The genus name *Henipavirus* is itself built from HENdra + NIPAh — the cousinology is IN the Latin [D, generally].

### Hantaan
- **American regional name:** Hantaan virus — the Hantan River, Korea [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthohantavirus hantanense* — the RIVER [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** hantaviruses — cousins: Sin Nombre ("the nameless," Four Corners USA), Seoul, Puumala, Dobrava [D, generally]. Sin Nombre is the cousin whose American name is a REFUSAL to name the place [LOGIC — mine, labeled].
- **Latin↔American:** the river in both.

### Dengue
- **American regional name:** dengue — from Swahili *ki denga pepo*, "a sudden overtaking by an evil spirit" (also rendered "cramp-like seizure"); or from Spanish *dengue*/Caribbean "dandy fever" — the stiff, affected gait [D]. American name: breakbone fever — the SHOWING [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthoflavivirus denguei* — no place, no showing, no shape in the Latin species name; the family (flavivirus) says yellow [D].
- **Cousinology:** flavivirus — cousin to West Nile, Zika, St. Louis encephalitis, Japanese encephalitis [D]. Four serotypes DENV-1 through DENV-4 [D].
- **Latin↔American:** DIVERGENCE — the American says the spirit or the broken bones; the Latin says almost nothing. The showing lives entirely in the American track [LOGIC].
- **His note [H]:** *dinga*/cramp — the virus carries the symptom-word. The American track reaches all the way down [H→D, from the coliseum paper].

### Chikungunya
- **American regional name:** chikungunya — from the Makonde language (northern Mozambique/southeast Tanzania; often misattributed to Swahili), "that which bends up" — the stooped posture of the arthritic showing [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Chikungunya alphavirus* — the showing-word, Latinized [D].
- **Cousinology:** alphavirus — cousin to EEE, WEE, VEE [D].
- **Latin↔American:** the SAME word in both — the showing survived translation intact. The rare case where the American IS the Latin's source [D].

## Part 5 — Showing-named and question-named

### Yellow fever
- **American regional name:** yellow fever — the SHOWING (jaundice) [D]. Also "American plague," "yellow jack," "bronze John" — the American names pile up [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Orthoflavivirus flavi* — *flavus*, yellow. The Latin says the SHOWING too [D].
- **Cousinology:** flavivirus — the name-giver of the whole family (*flavi* = yellow) [D].
- **Latin↔American:** AGREEMENT — both say yellow. The showing translates cleanly [D].

### Q fever
- **American regional name:** Q fever — "Q" for QUERY: named for the question, because the cause was unknown [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Coxiella burnetii* — the MEN: Cox and Burnet [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** none close among the regional names — the odd cousin [GAP].
- **Latin↔American:** the American says the QUESTION; the Latin says the men. The only disease named for not-knowing [LOGIC — mine, labeled].

### Malta fever
- **American regional name:** Malta fever — the PLACE (Malta); also undulant fever, milk fever [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Brucella melitensis* — *melitensis*, "of Malta" (Melita = Malta). The Latin says the PLACE TOO [D, generally].
- **Cousinology:** *Brucella abortus*, *B. suis* — the species cousins [D].
- **Latin↔American:** AGREEMENT — Malta in both, one in English, one in Latin [D].

### Trench fever
- **American regional name:** trench fever — the CROWD/PLACE (the trenches, WWI) [D, generally].
- **Latin structure:** *Bartonella quintana* — *quintana*, "of the fifth" (the five-day fever) [D, generally]. The Latin says the TIMING [D].
- **Cousinology:** *Bartonella henselae* (cat-scratch) — the cousin [D, generally].
- **Latin↔American:** DIVERGENCE — American says the trenches; Latin says the fifth day. Place vs time [LOGIC].

### Colorado tick fever
- **American regional name:** Colorado tick fever — the PLACE (Colorado) + the VECTOR (tick) + the SHOWING (fever) [D].
- **Latin structure:** *Coltivirus* — no place in the genus; the species name carries Colorado [D, generally]. Transmitted by the Rocky Mountain wood tick (*Dermacentor andersoni*) — the SAME tick as RMSF [D].
- **Cousinology:** reovirus — the only tick-borne reovirus of humans; not a cousin of RMSF at all (virus vs bacterium) despite sharing the tick and the mountains [D].
- **Latin↔American:** the place is in both, but the tick-sharing with RMSF is a false cousinology — same vector, different kingdoms [LOGIC — mine, labeled]. His warning stands: shared words don't make cousins.

## Part 6 — The pattern: what the regional names teach

### The four things a name can say [LOGIC — mine, labeled]

Every regional name says one or more of four things: the PLACE, the MAN, the SHAPE, the SHOWING.

- **Place-names:** valley fever, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, Lyme, La Crosse, St. Louis, WEE/EEE/VEE, Jamestown Canyon, Powassan, West Nile, Japanese encephalitis, Murray Valley, Ebola (river), Marburg, Lassa, Zika, Nipah, Hendra, Hantaan (river), Malta fever, Colorado tick fever [D].
- **Man-names:** *Rickettsia rickettsii* (Ricketts twice), *Rickettsia conorii* (Conor), *Rickettsia prowazekii* (Prowazek), *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Burgdorfer), *Coxiella burnetii* (Cox, Burnet) [D].
- **Shape-names:** *coccidioidomycosis* (the berries), *Streptococcus* (the chains), filovirus (*filum*, thread) [D].
- **Showing-names:** yellow fever, breakbone fever, chikungunya ("that which bends up"), dengue (the spirit/cramp), Q fever (the question), trench fever (the fifth day), desert rheumatism (the joint pain) [D].

### The cousinology rule [H→D]

His cousinology holds where the LATIN agrees: the encephalitis flaviviruses are cousins because the Latin says so (same genus/family) [D]; the spotted-fever rickettsiae are cousins because the Latin says so [D]; the -mycosis idiom family are cousins because the Latin says so [D]. Where only the AMERICAN agrees (Rift Valley fever vs valley fever — the shared "valley"), the cousinology FAILS — the Latin exposes the false kinship [D]. **The Latin is the cousin-test** [LOGIC — mine, labeled; his method underneath].

### The American-first reading [H]

His ordering stands: the American name says where it found the people (the valley, the mountains, the town, the river); the Latin says what the laboratory found (the man, the shape, the family). Both kept — the American for the showing, the Latin for the structure. The errors of medicine began when the renaming STRIPPED the American showing instead of ADDING the Latin structure [H, his renaming thesis].

### "La Crosse" is French [D]

Filed again because it matters to his American Latinology: "La Crosse" is French — *la crosse*, the hooked stick — not Latin [D]. The American track carries French, Spanish (*dengue*), Swahili (*denga*), Makonde (chikungunya), Ngbandi (*Legbala*). The "Latin" in-between is often not Latin at all — it's the world's languages wearing a Latin coat [LOGIC — mine, labeled].
