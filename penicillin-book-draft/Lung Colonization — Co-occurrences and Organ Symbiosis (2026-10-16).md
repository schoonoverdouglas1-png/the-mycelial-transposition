## 2. Appendix — what the science actually says

His taught claim ("the appendix is part of our immune system") has a real foothold:

- **Immune tissue is undisputed.** The appendix is rich in gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT); even the 2007 skeptics' sources note "it is known that there is immune system tissue in the appendix." (Duke/ScienceDaily 2007)
- **The "safe house" hypothesis.** Bollinger & Parker (Duke, *Journal of Theoretical Biology*, 2007): the appendix functions as a **safe house for beneficial gut bacteria** — a biofilm-lined reservoir behind the ileocecal valve where commensals ride out diarrheal illness and repopulate the gut afterward. Circumstantial but influential.
- **Follow-ups:** the appendix evolved independently **32 times** across mammalian species (Smith et al., 2013) — nature keeps reinventing it, which argues against "useless"; Im et al. (2011, *Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology*): patients *with* an appendix had lower *C. difficile* recurrence.
- **Status:** function still debated, but "vestigial" is no longer the scientific position. His teachers were ahead of the textbooks.

## 3. Spleen and pancreas — documented roles, and his symbiosis thesis

**Spleen (documented):** a secondary lymphoid organ and the body's largest blood filter — white pulp makes antibodies, red pulp filters blood. It is the specialist in clearing **encapsulated bacteria**: *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis*, *H. influenzae* type b. Without it (asplenia/hyposplenism), risk of overwhelming post-splenectomy infection; IgM memory B cells depleted. (Oxford tropical medicine review; clinicaltrials.eu) — **the spleen is the organ that clears his serotypes from the blood.** That is a direct, documented spleen–serotype link.

**Pancreas–lung (documented):** cystic fibrosis — one gene (CFTR), two organs. The chloride-channel defect dehydrates mucus in **both** the airways and the pancreatic ducts; thick mucus blocks both, inviting the same colonization (S. aureus, then Pseudomonas; Candida and Aspergillus fungi). The Pulmonology CF case above is the triangle in one patient: **pancreatic insufficiency + S. aureus + A. fumigatus**, together. (MDPI Microbiol. Res.; ResearchGate CFTR review)

**Gut–lung axis (documented field):** gut microbes communicate with the lungs via metabolites (short-chain fatty acids), immune cells, and vagus signaling; gut dysbiosis impairs pulmonary defense — including against *S. pneumoniae* (reduced SCFAs → vulnerability) and reshapes the lung's response to *A. fumigatus*. Bidirectional: lung disease can drive gut dysbiosis. (MDPI 2026 review; PubMed 40901024)

**Honest verdict on his symbiosis thesis:** no literature studies "lung colonization in symbiosis with the pancreas and spleen" as a general mechanism — that framing is his. What exists: (a) CF, where one defect couples lung and pancreas colonization; (b) the gut–lung axis, where gut microbes (and the appendix's reservoir) shape lung immunity; (c) the spleen as the blood-filter that clears pneumococci — the organ standing between lung colonization and bloodstream invasion. Three documented legs; the single "symbiosis" is his synthesis to evidence.

## What was NOT found

- No study of lung colonization "in symbiosis" with pancreas/spleen/appendix as one mechanism — the pieces exist separately, not as his unified thesis.
- No *S. aureus* + *Aspergillus* interaction mechanism studied at the molecular level comparable to the pneumo–Aspergillus H2O2/gliotoxin work — co-occurrence is documented, the chemistry is not.
- No per-serotype lung co-occurrence map (consistent with every prior file).
