# Serotype × Co-Pathogen Cross-Link Map (2026-10-17)

*Built at Douglas's commission (dictated-notes section 91). Manuscript reference — never medical advice, no treatment or diagnostic content.*

## The honesty statement — read this first

The published record documents **species-level** *S. pneumoniae* interactions thoroughly — with viruses, bacteria, and fungi. But **no per-serotype co-pathogen map exists in the literature**. Almost every interaction below was studied with *S. pneumoniae* named as a species (or with one or two lab strains), not across the 100+ serotypes.

This file is therefore the closest the record gets, built in three tiers:

- **[D-serotype]** — a study that actually named a specific serotype with a specific co-pathogen. Rare. These are the map's only true serotype cells.
- **[D-species]** — documented for pneumococcus as a species. Applies to the species; **not proven** to hold identically for any given serotype.
- **[GAP — his to fill]** — his thesis reaches here; no test exists. Pattern shown, gap marked, his to evidence.
- **[H→D]** — a *partial* relationship: a documented serotype property crossed with a documented species-level interaction. Reasoning, not finding — reported because his rule (section 92) says partial relationships must be looked upon when full ones can't be seen, by computer or by human. Labeled partial, never upgraded.

## His standing rule (section 92): test pneumonia against the panels

His rule: whenever pneumonia is discussed, test it against the **Mayo Clinic and Quest Laboratories *S. pneumoniae* panels** — the 23-serotype IgG antibody panels (Mayo PN23M; Quest's equivalent). The matrix's primary rows are those 23 panel serotypes: **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6B, 7F, 8, 9N, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 17F, 18C, 19A, 19F, 20, 22F, 23F, 33F** — every pneumonia claim in this file is tested against them. (His account: researching each serotype through those panels, he defined viruses and virus-connections from each serotype's relationships, full *and* partial — the partials are his cells to fill.)

## His frame: purposeful colonization (section 91)

His thesis: the body *keeps* colonization on purpose — bacteria, fungi, yeasts maintained as preparedness, and the colonists are part of pneumonia itself, serotypes included. The record's version, stated as **Theorem [D]**: nasopharyngeal colonization is the prerequisite step of pneumococcal pathogenesis — **carriage precedes invasion**, not the reverse; and germ-free animals develop defective immunity while normal colonization drives immune maturation and colonization resistance. The *function* (preparedness) is documented; the *purpose* (intent) is his word, filed as his. Every row below starts from carriage — the colonist layer his thesis says the body keeps on purpose.

## Part 2 — The species-level interaction library [D-species]

Every interaction below is documented for *S. pneumoniae* as a species. Direction is stated where the literature states it.

### Viruses → pneumococcus (the virus opens the door)

- **Influenza A → pneumococcus: the classic synergy [D-species].** Mechanisms, all documented: (a) epithelial damage + basement-membrane/fibrin exposure increases bacterial adherence; (b) **viral neuraminidase desialylates mucins**, exposing receptors (PAFr, laminin) — pneumococcal adhesins (PsaP, PsrP) bind the newly exposed surface; (c) crippled mucociliary clearance; (d) influenza-induced neutrophil apoptosis/dysfunction and depressed macrophage phagocytosis; (e) type I interferon suppressing antibacterial innate function. Modeling estimates **~100-fold increased susceptibility** to pneumococcal pneumonia post-influenza; 50–95% of past pandemic influenza deaths involved bacterial co-pathogens. **Reverse direction also documented:** bacterial proteases activate influenza infectivity; bacterial neuraminidase enhances virion release.
- **RSV → pneumococcus: acquisition and density [D-species].** Asymptomatic RSV infection significantly increased pneumococcal **acquisition**; RSV elevated pneumococcal **density** for up to 9 days post-challenge; high colonization density + nasal inflammation correlated with increased nasal **shedding** (transmission). Human challenge-model data.
- **Rhinovirus → pneumococcus: acquisition [D-species].** Asymptomatic rhinovirus infection significantly increased pneumococcal acquisition (same challenge program as RSV).
- **Human metapneumovirus + pneumococcus: co-detection [D-species, case-level].** Fatal pediatric case: serotype 19A pneumonia + hMPV + *M. pneumoniae* triple detection — each mild alone, fatal together.

### Pneumococcus → viruses (the bacterium pushes back)

- **Pneumococcus antagonizes RSV-analogue and influenza [D-species, mouse model].** In a novel infant-mouse nasopharyngeal model, pneumococcal colonization **reduced** pneumonia-virus-of-mice (RSV analogue) loads via accelerated viral clearance — and pneumococci also antagonized influenza virus. The effect was **capsule-dependent**: ethanol-killed pneumococci and a capsule-deficient mutant lost it; a naturally acapsular strain retained it. Magnitude was strain-dependent. Direction: **pneumo → virus (antagonism)** — the colonist suppressing the virus, a documented instance of his "colonists as preparedness" in action.

### Carriage bacteria ↔ pneumococcus

- ***S. aureus* — antagonism [D-species].** Pneumococcus kills *S. aureus* via hydrogen peroxide (SpxB pyruvate oxidase → hydroxyl radicals); negative carriage association (aOR 0.53–0.67); *S. aureus* retaliates with catalase and a toxic fatty acid. PCV7's clearance of vaccine serotypes **doubled** *S. aureus* nasal colonization in children (10.1% vs 5.0%) — the niche reshuffle, documented.
- ***H. influenzae* (nontypeable) — both [D-species].** Competition (H2O2, neuraminidase-enhanced complement clearance, arginine competition, acidic lysis products) AND synergy (co-carriage in healthy children; dual-species biofilms outproduce either alone; co-occurrence linked to higher pneumonia odds).
- ***M. catarrhalis* — synergy [D-species].** Positive co-carriage association (OR 3.07); densities of all three (pneumo + *H. influenzae* + *M. catarrhalis*) positively correlated when co-present; *M. catarrhalis*–*S. aureus* strongly negative (OR 0.06).
- ***M. tuberculosis* — both directions [D-species].** Pneumococcal pneumonia can reactivate latent TB; TB-damaged lung invites pneumococcal superinfection (vaccine-probe trial: 43.4% fewer TB hospitalizations among pneumococcal vaccinees).

### Fungi and yeasts ↔ pneumococcus

- ***Aspergillus fumigatus* — bidirectional chemical warfare [D-species].** Pneumo H2O2 fragments *A. fumigatus* biofilms (mycelial network shredded, ECM gone); fungal **gliotoxin** inhibits pneumococcal growth/biofilms and **synergizes with cefotaxime/vancomycin**. One clinical co-infection (IgM deficiency); the TB cavity is the shared house.
- ***Candida* spp. — one co-infection case [D-species].** Necrotic lung ball, *Candida* + pneumo (single case report).
- ***C. auris* — sequential, not interacting [D-species].** One myeloma patient: pneumococcal meningitis, then *C. auris* fungemia in hospital — shared destroyed immunity, no biological interaction.
- ***Cryptococcus* — rare co-infections + one false positive [D-species].** Two genuine dual-infection cases (post-traumatic meningitis; myeloma bacteremia→meningitis); one PCR-panel false positive erased by confirmatory testing — panels can report co-infection that isn't there.
- ***C. albicans* ↔ oral streptococci — rich synergy, wrong neighborhood [D-species].** Biofilm coaggregation documented — but with oral streptococci (*gordonii, oralis*), **not** pneumococcus. Do not import it.

## Part 3 — [D-serotype]: the genuinely serotype-specific interactions

These are the only cells in the literature where a named serotype meets a named co-pathogen:

1. **Serotype 3 (A66.1) + influenza — vaccine failure [D-serotype].** In mice, pneumococcal conjugate vaccine fully protected against serotype 3 pneumonia alone — but after influenza co-infection, protection collapsed to **30–40% survival**. Direction: influenza → vaccine-efficacy destruction, specifically against serotype 3.
2. **Serotype 2 (D39) + influenza — the kinetics model [D-serotype].** The Smith within-host model of IAV–pneumo coinfection ran on **type 2 D39** and type 3 A66.1 — the two most-used lab strains are serotypes 2 and 3; nearly all mechanistic influenza–pneumo data in existence rests on these two serotypes.
3. **Serotype 4 vs. 19F + influenza — invasiveness decides the outcome [D-serotype].** Sharma-Chawla 2016 compared serotypes head-to-head under influenza: highly invasive **serotype 4** went from 58%→100% pneumonia, 21%→90% bacteremia, 0%→79% mortality; carrier **serotype 19F** went 91%→100% pneumonia, 0%→33% bacteremia, 0%→63% mortality. Same virus, different serotype, different catastrophe — the capsule's invasiveness profile governs the co-infection.
4. **ICI serotypes overrepresented in post-viral pneumonia [D-serotype].** Song et al. (919 patients, Korea): infrequently-colonizing invasive serotypes — **4, 5, 7F/7A, 8, 9V/9A, 12F, 18C** — were significantly overrepresented in **post-viral** vs. primary pneumococcal pneumonia (69.7% of ICI cases were post-viral). The serotypes that rarely colonize are the ones the virus ushers in.
5. **Serotype 19A + hMPV + *M. pneumoniae* — fatal triple [D-serotype].** One child: 19A pneumonia + human metapneumovirus + *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* simultaneously detected; fatal despite vaccination and ICU care.
6. **Serotypes 3, 14, 18C, 19F, 19A + *S. aureus* — the biofilm five [D-serotype].** Sempere et al. 2024: mixed *S. aureus*–pneumococcus biofilms studied with exactly these five serotypes; post-PCV13 sera reduced the pneumococcal biofilms but not the staph.
7. **Capsule-dependence of virus antagonism [D-serotype-adjacent].** The PVM/influenza antagonism required capsule (capsule-deficient mutants lost it) — and capsule *thickness* is a serotype property (serotype 3: 428% of reference). The serotype with the most capsule may be the strongest virus-antagonist. Mechanism documented; serotype ranking not tested.

## Part 6 — Gaps & patterns: numbered, his to fill

1. **The entire fungi/yeasts column at serotype resolution.** No study pairs any serotype with any fungus or yeast. The [H→D] intersections (serotype 3's capsule vs. *Aspergillus* biofilms; 19A's MDR vs. gliotoxin synergy) are the obvious first experiments.
2. **Virus × serotype beyond influenza.** RSV, rhinovirus, hMPV interactions are species-level only; no serotype has been tested against them.
3. **The ICI pattern's mechanism.** Song's finding (invasive, rarely-carried serotypes overrepresented post-virus) has no established mechanism — his "virus ushers in the serotype" thesis fits here.
4. **Capsule thickness × virus antagonism.** Capsule-dependence is documented; the serotype ranking (3 > others?) is not.
5. **Carriage specialists × viral amplification.** 6B, 19F, 23F are the carriage serotypes; RSV/rhinovirus boost acquisition and density — the intersection is untested and his purposeful-colonization thesis (91) predicts it.
6. **Per-serotype *H. influenzae* / *M. catarrhalis* data.** The consortia literature never serotypes the pneumococcus.
7. **Serotype ↔ TB-lineage cross-link.** Documented at species level both directions; no serotype-to-lineage (L1–L8) mapping exists.
8. **The 46 unfilled cells of his panel.** His screenshots (his words, his analysis) are the intended contents of the [GAP] cells for the 23 PPSV23 serotypes — the map above is the scaffold they drop into.
