## Your thesis, up front [H]

In the beginning medicine was simply put: **you're sick, you're sick. If you're well, you're well.** There isn't a whole lot in between — but there's still a little bit of something. The renaming era sold itself as more accurate data; your read is the data became LESS accurate, because the names stopped saying sick-or-well and started saying committee Latin.

## Era 1 — 1776 to ~1860: the descriptive names [D]

American diseases named for what they looked like or did: **smallpox, yellow fever** (1793 Philadelphia epidemic), **cholera** (1832+ epidemics), **consumption** (tuberculosis), **diphtheria** (throat distemper), **scarlet fever, typhoid, ague** (malaria), **measles**. The name WAS the diagnosis — sick or well, readable on the body.

## Era 2 — ~1860s–1900s: germ theory renames everything [D]

Koch, Pasteur, and the bacteriologists re-anchored diseases to microbes, and the renaming began:
- The meningitis bacterium: *Diplococcus intracellularis meningitidis* (1887) → renamed *Neisseria meningitidis* (1901)
- Plague's bacillus: *Bacterium pestis* → *Bacillus pestis* → *Pasteurella pestis* → *Yersinia pestis* — four names for one bug
- Friedländer's bacillus (1882, from a pneumonia death) → *Klebsiella* (1885)
- Diphtheria's cause: KLB (Klebs-Löffler Bacillus) → *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*

The disease stopped being named for the showing and started being named for the agent — in Latin, by committee and by discoverer.

## Era 3 — 1893 → 1948 → now: the standardization [D]

- **1893:** Bertillon Classification of Causes of Death adopted in Chicago — the first international list
- **1948:** WHO takes over; ICD-6 adds morbidity (not just death) — the universe of disease becomes a hierarchy
- **1990/2022:** ICD-10, ICD-11 — thousands of codes; the name is now a number
- Modern: WHO names the disease (COVID-19) and the virus gets a separate name (2019-nCoV → SARS-CoV-2) — disease and agent officially split

## The "messed up" point — my recommendation [mine]

If I have to name when it started getting messed up: **the 1880s–1900s**, when the disease name detached from the patient's showing and attached to the laboratory's agent. The ICD then froze that detachment into law. Your additive-naming thesis is the reversal: keep the old sick-or-well name, ADD the accurate one — both stand.
