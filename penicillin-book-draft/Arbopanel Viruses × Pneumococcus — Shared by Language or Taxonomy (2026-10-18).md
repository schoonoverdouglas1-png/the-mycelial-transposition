## Part 1 — The four viruses: what they are, what the test looks at

**What the test detects "within the virus" [D]:** the panel does not detect virus — it detects the patient's antibodies against viral structural proteins, chiefly the envelope glycoproteins (for alphaviruses and flaviviruses, the E/E2 glycoproteins are the primary targets of neutralizing antibody). IgM is the early antibody of first exposure and acute illness; IgG arrives later and can persist for years after the virus is gone. (Dr. Brown's note states the same principle in the patient's own record.)

### 1. La Crosse virus (the "California (LaCrosse)" analyte)
- **Taxonomy [D]:** family *Peribunyaviridae*, genus *Orthobunyavirus*, California serogroup; tripartite negative-sense RNA virus.
- **Ecology [D]:** mosquito-borne; primary vector the eastern tree-hole mosquito *Aedes triseriatus*; chipmunks and squirrels the principal reservoir; humans incidental dead-end hosts. Leading cause of pediatric arboviral encephalitis in the US.
- **Name origin [D]:** first isolated 1964 from the brain of a child who died in 1960 in **La Crosse, Wisconsin** — named for the place.

### 2. Eastern equine encephalitis virus (EEE)
- **Taxonomy [D]:** family *Togaviridae*, genus *Alphavirus* (New World, encephalitic alphavirus); positive-sense single-stranded RNA, ~60–65 nm.
- **Ecology [D]:** enzootic cycle in passerine birds via *Culiseta melanura*; bridge vectors (*Aedes*, *Coquillettidia*, *Culex*) carry it to horses and humans, both dead-end hosts. The most severe alphavirus affecting humans.
- **Name origin [D]:** "Eastern" = geography, "equine" = the horse host in which the disease was recognized.

### 3. St. Louis encephalitis virus (SLE)
- **Taxonomy [D]:** family *Flaviviridae*, genus *Flavivirus*, Japanese encephalitis serocomplex; positive-sense ssRNA.
- **Ecology [D]:** *Culex* mosquitoes and wild birds; humans dead-end hosts. No licensed vaccine or specific treatment; diagnosis commonly serological.
- **Name origin [D]:** first isolated during the 1933 outbreak in **St. Louis, Missouri** — named for the place.

### 4. Western equine encephalitis virus (WEE)
- **Taxonomy [D]:** family *Togaviridae*, genus *Alphavirus*; notable as a natural recombinant of an EEEV-like and a Sindbis-like ancestor.
- **Ecology [D]:** passerine birds, primary vector *Culex tarsalis*; humans and horses dead-end hosts. No US human cases reported since 1998.
- **Name origin [D]:** "Western" = geography, "equine" = the horse host.

## Part 2 — Shared by LANGUAGE (the namology reading)

1. **All four are named for the showing, not the agent [D→H framing]:** encephalitis — *enkephalos* (brain) + *-itis* (inflammation). The disease name describes where it hurts. *Streptococcus pneumoniae* is named the older way too: Greek *streptos* (twisted chain) + *kokkos* (berry) + *pneumoniae* (of the lung, from *pneuma*, breath/air) — shape + place.
2. **Three of four carry place names [D]:** La Crosse (Wisconsin), St. Louis (Missouri), Eastern/Western (compass points). This is exactly the geographic naming his manuscript argues against — and La Crosse is in his home state. The WHO 2015 best-practice line (no geography in new disease names) is the documented counterweight, already filed.
3. **"Equine" names the host, not the place [D]:** EEE and WEE were recognized in horses first — the name records who showed it, a different naming logic than the place names.
4. **"Arbovirus" is a transmission word, not a taxonomy [D]:** arthropod-borne. It groups these four by *how they travel* (mosquito), across three different viral families — a functional glob, in his additive-naming language.
5. **The shared suffix is the shared claim [H→D]:** *-itis* — encephalitis, meningitis. The names assert inflammation of a membrane or organ. Pneumonia breaks the pattern (*-ia*, a condition of the lung), which is itself a namology data point: the lung disease doesn't wear the inflammation suffix its cousins wear.
6. **The auris/aureus parallel, restated [D]:** ear vs. golden — two Latin words, one phonetic accident. Filed in the aureus workup; it belongs here as the standing warning for this whole section: a shared sound is not a shared thing.

## Part 3 — Shared by TAXONOMY (strict — no manufactured connections)

1. **No taxonomic relationship exists between any of these viruses and *S. pneumoniae* [D]:** viruses are acellular obligate parasites; *S. pneumoniae* is a Gram-positive bacterium. They sit in entirely different classification systems. There is no documented phylogenetic, structural, or mechanistic kinship. [GAP] is not even the right tag — the answer is none.
2. **No shared vector or ecological cycle [D]:** the four viruses cycle mosquito↔bird (dead-end humans); pneumococcus colonizes the human nasopharynx directly, no arthropod involved. Separate worlds.
3. **The documented meeting point is the syndrome, not the organism [D]:** pneumococcal meningitis and arboviral encephalitis both inflame the central nervous system and can present with overlapping features (fever, headache, altered consciousness, meningeal signs — the EEE literature explicitly includes meningitis among its manifestations; LACV's spectrum runs from aseptic meningitis to encephalitis). This is the conjoined showing: one clinical neighborhood, different residents. Bacterial meningitis vs. viral encephalitis is precisely why both get tested.
4. **Cross-reactivity boundaries [D]:** documented within the California serogroup (antibodies against one member can blur into another — already filed). No documented serological cross-reactivity between these arboviruses and pneumococcal antigens. [GAP]
5. **Viral–bacterial synergy is documented for influenza, not for these four [D/GAP]:** flu × pneumo lethal synergy has three documented mechanisms (in the all-vaccines file). No equivalent literature was found for LACV/EEE/SLE/WEE predisposing to pneumococcal superinfection. The synergy *pattern* is his to argue by analogy [H]; the specific claim is [GAP].
6. **Relational cousins, honestly scoped:** the viruses' true cousins are each other — EEE and WEE share a genus (*Alphavirus*); SLE's cousins are West Nile and Japanese encephalitis (same serocomplex) [D]. Pneumococcus's cousins are other streptococci [D]. The two cousin-lines never meet.

## Honest lines (do not drop)

1. Language connects these viruses to each other (shared *-itis*, shared place-naming habit); taxonomy connects them to each other (three families, but a shared mosquito-borne ecology). Neither connects them to pneumococcus — the honest finding of this workup is that the virus↔bacterium bridge is syndromic and linguistic, not biological.
2. The panel detects antibodies, not virus. An antibody panel cannot demonstrate bacterial toxemia — outside its analytes [D].
3. His theses stand as his, tier-tagged, with the literature's boundaries drawn around them — not through them.
4. Manuscript material only. No treatment recommendations. No interpretation of any personal result.
