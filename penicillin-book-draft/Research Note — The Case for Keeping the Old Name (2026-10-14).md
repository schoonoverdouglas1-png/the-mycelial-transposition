# Research Note — The Case for Keeping the Old Name

**Filed:** 2026-10-14 · For local peer review alongside *The Mycelial Transposition* manuscript
**Source:** Reply in the *Journal of Clinical Microbiology* (doi:10.1128/jcm.01550-25) to a letter proposing the rename "candidiasis" → "candidozosis"/"candidozymiasis"; authors write from a working group of ISHAM, the European Confederation of Medical Mycology, and the Fungal Diagnostics Consortium
**Link:** https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/jcm.01550-25

## The piece (in brief)

- The authors advocate maintaining *Candida auris* and the disease term "candidiasis" until consensus is reached by an international working group — a formal, primary-source instance of the retention posture.
- Their objections to the *Candidozyma* rename: it rests on a single 2024 study (Liu et al., *Persoonia*) that examined only one *C. auris* strain, with insufficient sampling, no independent phylogenetic validation, and unclear ecological distinctions; there is "no regulating body for fungal taxonomy," so the change was the proposing authors' conclusion, never debated by an ICTF working group; *Candidozyma* is still unstable as species keep being added; and the taxonomic distances between *Metschnikowiaceae* genera are very small — they might equally be subgenera or sections.
- Their forward-looking objection: six clades of *C. auris* with distinct genetic diversity and antifungal susceptibility profiles may eventually be re-split into separate species, producing what they call unnecessary, interminable name changes that destabilize species classifications.
- Their user-side objection: the purpose of names is primarily unambiguous communication between users; more than 30 *Candida* species causing candidiasis face renaming, and it is impractical to rename diseases with every new designation; *C. auris* behaves clinically like other *Candida* (opportunistic, candidiasis spectrum, treated under the global candidiasis guideline), so the disease name should stand; premature renaming undermines surveillance, regulation, diagnosis, treatment, infection control, and research.
- Governance response: the ISHAM Working Group on the nomenclature of clinical fungi (clinicians, public-health professionals, clinical microbiologists, taxonomists) is developing practical, evidence-based criteria for implementing fungal name changes into clinical practice — built on the 12-society 2023 consensus framework (*J Clin Microbiol* 61:e0087323). Participation is open via ISHAM.

## The nomenclature hook

- This is the steelman the manuscript has been missing: the strongest formal case for NOT adopting the new name, argued by the institutional side (clinicians and mycologists) rather than individual authors. The adoption-spectrum chapter (intro, ch 6) gets its counterweight: the "acknowledged-but-retained" posture is now backed by a primary source with a full argument list, not just observed behavior.
- Direct lineage with the notes already filed: the *Nakaseomyces glabratus* retention note (2026-10-09), the CAP TODAY lab-chooses-the-name note (2026-10-10), and the adoption-spectrum insert (2026-10-11) all documented retention as practice; this reply gives retention its institutional rationale — stability-as-patient-safety.
- The "six clades may be re-split" argument is a new mechanism for the chapters: the fear that adopting a rename now invites a cascade of further renames later — rename cost compounds. Worth a line in ch 6's calibration setting.
- The governance detail moves the "who decides" question forward: a 12-society framework plus an open ISHAM WG building implementation criteria. Naming authority is being built in real time, by committee, with open enrollment — that is the manuscript's thesis about names doing policy work, stated by the people doing it.
- Note the tension with the thesis: the reply concedes the remote phylogenetic position of *C. auris* versus *C. albicans* (different families). The disagreement is not about taxonomy — it is about clinical implementation. That distinction sharpens the manuscript: the argument is over *when a true rename becomes a used name*, not over the tree.

## Honest caveats (do not drop these)

1. **A reply, not a study.** This is a short-form response to a letter — consensus and argument, not peer-reviewed experimental evidence. Cite it as the institutional position, not as data.
2. **Not new.** The reply is from early 2026 (~January). Its value is as the formal counter-argument, not as a development — file it as a primary source for the retention case, not as news.
3. **Does not settle the taxonomy.** The authors do not claim *Candidozyma* is phylogenetically wrong; they claim the evidence base is too thin and the clinical cost too high for adoption now. Do not cite it against the 2024 phylogenomic analysis itself.
4. **Keep separate from personal medical care.** Manuscript material only.
