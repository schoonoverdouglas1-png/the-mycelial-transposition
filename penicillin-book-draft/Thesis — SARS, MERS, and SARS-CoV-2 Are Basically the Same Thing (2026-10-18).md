# Thesis — SARS, MERS, and SARS-CoV-2 Are Basically the Same Thing (2026-10-18)

**Status:** tested against the record. His thesis with the percentages he asked for.
**Frame:** *The Mycelial Transposition* — educational manuscript reference only. Never medical advice.

## His thesis [H]

SARS-CoV, MERS-CoV, and SARS-CoV-2 are all basically the same thing.

## The percentages [D] (genome sequence identity, published comparisons)

| Pair | Same | Different |
|---|---|---|
| SARS-CoV ↔ SARS-CoV-2 | ~79–80% | ~20–21% |
| SARS-CoV-2 ↔ MERS-CoV | ~50% | ~50% |
| SARS-CoV ↔ MERS-CoV | ~50–53% | ~47–50% |

Spike protein alone: SARS-CoV-2 vs SARS-CoV ~76% identical; vs MERS-CoV only ~26–28%.

## What the percentages mean

- All three are betacoronaviruses [D]. Same genus — genuinely one family.
- SARS-CoV and SARS-CoV-2 are close cousins: same subgenus (Sarbecovirus), both enter human cells through the ACE2 receptor, ~79–80% identical genomes [D].
- MERS-CoV is the distant cousin: different subgenus (Merbecovirus), enters through a different receptor (DPP4), only ~50% identical to either SARS virus [D].
- Case fatality differs sharply: SARS ~9–10%, MERS ~34%, SARS-CoV-2 far lower overall [D].

## Verdict

Partial hold. "Basically the same thing" is fair for the two SARS viruses — close cousins, same doorway in, four-fifths identical. MERS is family but not the same: half the genome different, a different receptor, a different subgenus, far deadlier per case. Same genus, two different closenesses.
