**His theorem [H]:** valley fever (coccidioidomycosis) belongs in every entry — the fungal connection in every entry, tying all the names together.

## Tested three ways

### 1. Where it's strongest [H→D]

Coccidioides genuinely is a multi-system mimic: it makes lung disease that looks like TB and bacterial pneumonia, it reaches the meninges (coccidioidal meningitis is real and serious), it infects bone and skin [D]. The Southwest teaches "think cocci" — the standing instruction to never close the fungal door in the differential [D, generally]. As a *differential discipline*, the theorem is sound medicine.

### 2. Where it breaks [honest]

Coccidioides is geographically restricted — endemic to the southwestern US, parts of Mexico, Central and South America [D]. A regionally endemic fungus cannot literally underlie all 53 names worldwide. And entries like the toxin poisonings (milk sickness, dry bellyache), the manifestations (dropsy, palsy, nostalgia), and the non-infectious rows carry no documented cocci link [GAP]. As literal etiology — "cocci is in every disease" — the record doesn't hold it.

### 3. The reading that saves it [LOGIC]

As METHOD, not etiology: "in every entry" = "in every differential." The theorem makes medical sense as a standing order — run the fungal differential every time, because the great imitator punishes whoever forgets it. That's the version that survives.

## The verdict, straight

Medically sensible as a discipline; not supportable as a universal cause. The fungal door stays open in every entry — but what's behind it varies by entry, and sometimes nothing is.
