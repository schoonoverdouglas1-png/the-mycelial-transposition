# Three Theses — Non-Susceptibility, Tetracyclines vs MRSA, MRSA as Immune System (2026-10-19)

**Status:** three separate theses from one dictation — never collapsed. Each: his thought + my thought → the statement that fits.
**Frame:** *The Mycelial Transposition* — educational manuscript reference only. Never medical advice. General theses only — nothing about any person's medications.

---

## Thesis 1 [H]: Non-susceptibility shouldn't rule out the agent — you might be fixated on the wrong thing

**His thought:** just because something is technically not susceptible to that agent doesn't mean it shouldn't be used anyway — the fixation might be on the wrong thing.

**My thought [D]:** in-vitro susceptibility and clinical effect genuinely diverge both ways — resistance on paper doesn't always mean failure in the body (penetration, biofilm, intracellular sites), and susceptibility doesn't guarantee success. Plus the documented second life of these drugs: tetracyclines, doxycycline included, have anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects independent of killing bacteria — doxycycline is used at sub-antimicrobial doses for rosacea and periodontal disease precisely for the non-killing effects.

**Combined:** the susceptibility plate tests one thing (can the drug kill this bug in a dish). The drug in the body does more than one thing. "Fixated on the wrong thing" names the real gap between the plate and the patient.

---

## Thesis 2 [H]: Stopping tetracyclines because of MRSA

**His thought:** his example — tetracyclines get dropped when MRSA enters the picture.

**My thought [D]:** the record is mixed here, honestly: doxycycline retains activity against much community MRSA and is used for MRSA skin infections. The dropping he describes happens in some settings and not others.

**Combined:** his example stands as his observation of practice; the record's version is narrower — tetracyclines weren't universally abandoned against MRSA, but the pattern he names (the resistant label retiring the drug) is real wherever it occurs.

---

## Thesis 3 [H]: MRSA is part of the body's own immune system — not quite completely understood yet

**His thought:** MRSA is part of the body's own immune system, and this isn't fully understood yet.

**My thought [D]:** *S. aureus* — resistant strains included — colonizes the skin and nares of roughly a third of people without causing disease. It lives as part of the body's microbial self; the microbiome's role in training and tuning immunity is documented. "Part of the immune system" is his framing; the foothold is commensal staph as resident, not invader — present, tolerated, and part of the negotiated peace.

**Combined:** the bug that terrifies the ward is also the bug that lives quietly on a third of us. His "part of the body's own immune system" reads the colonizer as constituent rather than intruder — and his "not quite completely understood yet" is the honest state of the science on when the resident becomes the pathogen.
