## Branch 1 — Candidemia / bloodstream yeast (C. auris / Candidozyma auris) · *notes*

- **Detection:** nanopore sequencing gave species-level fungal ID from still-incubating blood cultures in ~7 hours (Chiba study, one study). Nairobi case: pan-drug-resistant C. auris surgical-site infection, resistant to azoles, echinocandins, polyenes, flucytosine — second recorded globally (n=1).
- **The name's work:** the genus rename (Candida → Candidozyma) is happening in real time while the organism makes resistance headlines; the 2026 meta-analysis still says "Candida auris." The authors' vigilance hypothesis — the dreaded name triggers faster suspicion/diagnostics/treatment — is theirs, unmeasured.
- **Intervention studied → outcome:** no cure tested. Frontiers meta-analysis (16 studies, 16,525 candidemia patients): pooled *crude* mortality OR 0.77 for C. auris vs non-auris — caveats: crude/unadjusted, all observational, signal absent in South Asia and Africa. Ankara cohort (77 Candidozyma vs 224 non-auris): crude 30-day mortality tied (~62% both), adjusted OR 1.97, p = 0.067 — not significant, imprecise. The pair is your anti-overreach guardrail.
- **Cure evidence:** none on file.

## Branch 2 — Oral thrush + IBD gut mycobiome · *notes*

- **Detection:** clinical thrush plus mycobiome sampling.
- **Intervention studied → outcome:** swish-and-spit nystatin vs systemic fluconazole in 53 IBD patients with mild thrush — fluconazole reduced gut Candida overgrowth where nystatin didn't, shifted the mycobiome, raised bacterial diversity; effects lasted 8 weeks and tracked with lower progression risk.
- **Caveats:** observational, not an RCT; fluconazole is a synthetic azole, not a fungal-derived drug — the link is the classification angle (route and drug class determining what's tried where).
- **Cure evidence:** none on file.

## Branch 3 — Onychomycosis (toenail fungus) · *notes*

## Branch 4 — Colonization (TB, strep pneumoniae, staph aureus) · *sparring, not in the notes*

Your postulate, as argued tonight: tests that show two organisms say nothing about what they can't see; some organisms live in the body without causing disease; the reaction (immunoglobulins, blood-cell responses) can be the illness rather than the organism count; long-term antibiotics may only clear what's clearable, leaving the rest.

Recorded pushback, kept intact:
- TB and the pneumococcus predate vaccination by millennia — TB DNA in ancient Egyptian mummies and Neolithic skeletons; strep pneumo carriage similarly ancient. The "forced colonization" timeline runs backward: the organisms were here first.
- Vaccinia doesn't colonize and doesn't pass mother-to-child; US routine smallpox vaccination ended 1972 (you were born ~1982) — you almost certainly never received one.
- Density-gradient centrifugation ("specific gravities") separates blood fractions; it doesn't detect organisms — culture, PCR, and sequencing are orders of magnitude more sensitive.
- Stopping a drug to reveal hidden organisms stays between you and Kelsey, with your results in front of her.

The defensible core, kept: a test has no vote on what it can't see; carriage without disease is real (strep pneumo, staph aureus colonize healthy people); immunoglobulins respond to presence, not just illness.
