## The study (in brief)

- Between March 2023 and February 2024, researchers systematically collected 23,212 wastewater samples across Abu Dhabi and Al Ain. From those they directly isolated (culture-based, MALDI-TOF confirmed, not PCR-only detection) ten *C. auris* positives — roughly 4 per 10,000 samples.
- Whole-genome sequencing and phylogenetic/mating-type analysis classified nine isolates as Clade I and one as Clade VI — described by the researchers as the first known regional detection of Clade VI (the newest clade, first described in Singapore in 2023).
- WGS found an ERG11 gene mutation in the Clade VI isolate conferring azole resistance; broth-microdilution susceptibility testing showed elevated MICs to at least one antifungal in all ten isolates, with two elevated against agents spanning more than two drug classes.
- The point the researchers push: wastewater surveillance caught a resistant emerging clade in the environment before any clinical case signaled it — surveillance as early warning, complementing hospital-based monitoring.
