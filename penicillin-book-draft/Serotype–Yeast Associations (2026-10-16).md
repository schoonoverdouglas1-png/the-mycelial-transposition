# Serotype–Yeast Associations (2026-10-16)

*Researched at Douglas's direction for The Mycelial Transposition. Association reference only — never medical advice, no treatment or diagnostic content.*

**His thesis, one line:** yeasts, fungi, and bacteria are byproducts of sick bugs and sick tissues leaching the same products, the bugs embedded with our tissues — "just like the immunoglobulin chunks are."

**Scope note:** this file reports what the literature documents on *S. pneumoniae* (any serotype) and yeasts. No per-serotype yeast associations exist anywhere in the literature. Nothing invented; gaps stated bluntly.

## The reverse angle — byproducts and "immunoglobulin chunks"

Douglas asked what exists on microbes leaching shared products in sick tissue, and on immunoglobulin deposits. What the literature has:

- **Metabolic cross-feeding in mixed biofilms is documented.** One species feeds on another's byproducts (syntrophy): e.g., *Fusobacterium*/*Prevotella* ammonia byproducts raise pH for *Porphyromonas*; *S. gordonii* secretes metabolic products *C. albicans* uses as a carbon source. The "same products leaching between bugs" has a real mechanism name: **syntrophy**.
- **Secretory IgA is embedded in biofilm matrices.** SIgA stabilizes bacterial biofilms through dual interactions with mucin and bacterial surface proteins; 24–74% of gut anaerobic bacteria are IgA-coated in vivo — coating does not remove them, it accompanies them.
- **IgG/IgA deposits are found in skin.** Deposits observed in epidermal cells and the stratum basale, occasionally in normal skin — immunoglobulin physically sitting in the tissue, as his "chunks" image suggests.

## What was NOT found

- **No per-serotype yeast associations exist.** Nothing in the literature ties any specific pneumococcal serotype (1, 3, 14, 19A, or any of the 23) to any yeast.
- **No *S. pneumoniae*–yeast interaction mechanism is studied.** No biofilm, metabolic, or signaling work pairs pneumococcus with a yeast — the cross-kingdom literature pairs yeasts with *oral* streptococci or *S. aureus*.
- **No respiratory-mycobiome carriage studies link yeasts to pneumococcal carriage.** The mycobiome–pneumococcus co-colonization literature does not exist at the level his thesis needs.
- **No "sick bugs leaching shared products" framework** as a general disease mechanism — syntrophy and cross-feeding are studied per-pair, per-biofilm, not as his universal cycle.

## For the book

- **Footholds (documented):** immunoglobulin physically embedded in biofilms and deposited in skin (his "chunks"); metabolic cross-feeding between cohabiting microbes (his "same products"); rare but real pneumo–yeast co-infections (Candida lung ball, Cryptococcus meningitis cases).
- **His to evidence:** per-serotype yeast associations (none exist — the map is his to build); yeasts/fungi/bacteria as *byproducts* of sick bugs as a general mechanism; the embedded-bugs-leaching-products cycle as a universal process.
- **One caution to keep:** the false-positive *Cryptococcus* PCR case — panels can report co-infection that confirmatory testing erases. His "one test, many names" chapter should carry this alongside the cross-reactivity note.
