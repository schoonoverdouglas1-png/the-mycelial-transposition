## His thesis [H]

Cholesterol is a byproduct of infection — of the liquids of the body, or the tissue of the body, or a combination of both.

## Honest lines (do not drop)

- **Footholds [H→D]:** infection genuinely alters lipid metabolism — the acute-phase response drops HDL and raises triglycerides during sepsis/inflammation [D]. And *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* literally uses host cholesterol as a carbon source during persistence in macrophages [D] — the TB pillar touches this thesis directly. Other intracellular pathogens (Chlamydia, Coxiella) also manipulate host lipid pathways [D].
- **Seam [D]:** cholesterol is also endogenously synthesized (primarily liver) and dietary — it is not solely infection-derived in the standard account. His "byproduct of infection" as a general claim overstates what the record holds; the infection–lipid interaction is real, the exclusivity is his thesis, his to evidence.
