## The three viruses [D]

| | SARS-CoV | Rotavirus | RSV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Family | Coronaviridae | Reoviridae (Sedoreoviridae) | Pneumoviridae |
| Genus | Betacoronavirus | Rotavirus | Orthopneumovirus |
| Genome | +ssRNA, ~29.7 kb | dsRNA, 11 segments, ~18.5 kb | −ssRNA, ~15.2 kb |
| Baltimore group | IV | III | V |
| Envelope | Yes | No (triple-layered capsid) | Yes |
| Virion | Spherical, spike corona, ~80–120 nm | Wheel-like, ~76 nm | Pleomorphic, ~150–250 nm |
| Disease | Severe respiratory | Gastroenteritis (infants) | Lower respiratory (infants) |

## Relationships

- **Not close relatives.** Three different families, three different genome strategies (+ssRNA / segmented dsRNA / −ssRNA). They sit on distant branches — united by being RNA viruses and major human pathogens, not by kinship.
- **Envelope split:** SARS-CoV and RSV are enveloped; rotavirus is naked but armored — a triple-layered capsid instead.
- **Genome size runs opposite to virion size:** SARS-CoV carries the largest genome (~29.7 kb, among the largest RNA genomes known) in the middle-sized particle; rotavirus packs a segmented genome into the smallest particle; RSV has the smallest genome in the largest particle.
- **Mucosal surfaces:** SARS-CoV and RSV attack the respiratory tract; rotavirus attacks the gut. All three are diseases of surfaces.

## Size order (smallest → largest)

Rotavirus (~76 nm) < SARS-CoV (~100 nm) < RSV (~150–250 nm, pleomorphic).
