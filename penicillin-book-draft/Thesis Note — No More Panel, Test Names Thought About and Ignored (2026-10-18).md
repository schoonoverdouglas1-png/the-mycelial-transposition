## His postulate [H]

Take the thought of "no more panel" — moving beyond thinking in panels-by-name — and parallel it to this fact: the things tested by name are connected to pneumonia, meningitis, encephalitis. The names of the tests should be **thought about and ignored both**, because tests by name don't always indicate the true origins of the things being tested.

## What "thought about and ignored both" means (his doctrine, stated plainly)

- **Thought about:** the name carries history — it tells you what the namer thought the test was for, which is itself information.
- **Ignored:** the name must not constrain what the result is allowed to mean. A result can point at origins the name never contemplated.

## Documented footholds [D] — where test names already mislead about origins

- A pneumococcal IgG panel measures **antibodies** — exposure/vaccination history — not active infection. The name says the organism; the test reports the immune system's memory of it.
- Urinary antigen tests detect polysaccharide that can persist **for weeks** after the infection is gone. The name says the organism; the positive can be an echo.
- Multiplex PCR panels detect **nucleic acid** — they do not distinguish colonization from infection, or live organisms from dead ones. The name says "meningitis/encephalitis panel"; the target may be a bystander.
- Nontreponemal tests (RPR) carry syphilis's name but detect anti-lipid antibodies, not the treponeme. Lyme antibody tests stay positive after treatment. The pattern is general: **the name on the order form is not the thing in the tube.**
