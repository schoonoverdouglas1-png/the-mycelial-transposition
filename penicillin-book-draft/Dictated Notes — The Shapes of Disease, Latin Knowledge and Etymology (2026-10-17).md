## §164 — The yeasts-between (Oct 17, 2026, carried from the dictation side chat)

**His thesis, verbatim as given [H]:**
"the yeasts-between and their connections between everything else — THOSE things are what streptococcus pneumoniae is made out of, along with the viruses that hang out with them."

**Tested his way, three ways:**

1. **Mixed biofilms — documented leg [D]:** Streptococcus pneumoniae forms biofilms, and they are frequently multi-species — pneumococcus co-aggregates with Candida species and other nasopharyngeal residents into structured communities. This is a documented foothold for "their connections between everything else": they live connected, arranged, and interacting, not alone.

2. **Prophage integration — documented leg [D]:** pneumococcal genomes routinely carry prophages — viral DNA physically written into the bacterial genome itself. This is a documented foothold for "the viruses that hang out with them": they don't just hang out nearby; some of them are resident in the genome.

3. **His syncytin anchor [H]:** endogenous retroviral envelope proteins co-opted by mammals (syncytin — his standing anchor for viral+cellular merging). Filed as his tier: not documented support for this claim, but consistent with his framework.

**Honest seam [D]:** "made out of" doesn't survive literally. A pneumococcal cell is a bacterium — peptidoglycan cell wall, one bacterial chromosome, no yeast organelles or cytoplasm inside the cell. The yeasts in a mixed biofilm are neighbors and partners, not components of the cell. Pure-culture pneumococcus causes bacteremia and meningitis alone; it doesn't need yeast companions to be pneumococcus.

**Counter-evidence kept [D]:** the compositional reading fails, not the relational one.

**Carried forward [F]:** the relational version is what's documented — Streptococcus pneumoniae is a bacterium that co-lives with yeasts in biofilm communities and carries viral passengers in its genome. The "between" is real; it lives in the connections, not the composition.

**Namology note [H]:** "the yeasts-between" extends the Between Doctrine — a new entity name in his Americanized Latin manner. The *-between* suffix does the naming work the way *-itis* and *-osis* do: it tells you where the thing lives in the picture, not what it is under the glass.

## §166 — The concurrent-infection principle (Oct 17, 2026, carried from the dictation side chat)

**Tested his way, three legs:**

2. **CAPA + the mucormycosis wave [D]:** COVID-associated pulmonary aspergillosis in ICU patients, and the ~47,000-case mucormycosis wave in India during COVID — both documented fungal-viral co-infections with real mortality attached. The record's own recent, large-scale confirmation that co-infection is common and deadly, not exotic.
3. **Polymicrobial + Candida–bacterial co-infection [D]:** bloodstream Candida frequently co-occurs with bacteria; mixed biofilms are the rule in the mouth, the sinuses, the lungs. Documented, everyday, across sites.

**Honest seam [D]:** the record's co-infections are specific pairings in specific conditions — ICU stays, diabetes, immunosuppression, viral lung injury first. The elevation from "co-infection is common and matters" to a standing principle ("concurrently infectious" as the default state) is his thesis, and the evidence for the universal version is his to build. Mono-microbial infections are also common — isolated pneumococcal bacteremia from a pure blood culture is one of medicine's everyday facts.

**Counter-evidence kept [D]:** pure cultures exist; the default-everywhere version of the principle doesn't have the record behind it yet.

**Carried forward [F]:** co-infection is a documented, large, and historically underweighted mechanism — flu-pneumococcus, CAPA, mucormycosis, polymicrobial biofilms all sit on the record's side of his line. The standing-principle upgrade is his claim to prove; the record as it stands says "often and severely," not "always."

**Namology note [H]:** "concurrently infectious" is another Americanized-Latin working term — the concurrence *is* the showing. This is the action-form of the yeasts-between: not where they live, but what they do when they're there.

## §170 — The slash method (his standing notation, Oct 17, 2026)

**His words, verbatim [H]:**
"All those three lines are equally important with what I was discussing and whenever there's more than one word involved like that slashes can be used to continuously repeat parts of the word to get the endings different. to build on the thoughts. Like I did in my previous works where slashes are used to describe expanded part of an idea."

**The method, as he teaches it [H]:** when more than one word is in play, the slash repeats the shared stem and varies the endings — the slash is generative, not disjunctive. It doesn't pick; it expands. Slashes describe the *expanded part* of an idea, the way he used them in his previous works.

**Worked example — the §167 ruling [F]:**
- **myco-** — the shared stem (Greek *mykēs*, mushroom/fungus) [D]
- **-sis** — the condition, the state of being (mycosis: fungal infection as a condition) [D]
- **-logy** — the study, the account (mycology: the knowing of fungi) [D]
- **mycelium** — the network-body itself, the thing doing the touching [D]

**His reading [H]:** three endings, one fungal idea — the condition, the study, and the living network, all true at once. The slash lets the word be all three without choosing.

**Standing note for this thread [H]:** the slash method is now the notation of this chat. Wherever he dictates word-clusters, file them slashed — stem repeated, endings varied — and treat every line as equally important unless he rules otherwise.

**Refinement, same night [H] — his words:** "When the slash is going to be used right next to each other, that's building idea. Either or would be where you don't use it once between two words only in a sentence, most likely."

**The rule as he gives it [H]:**
- **Slash jammed tight** (mycosis/mycology/mycelium — no spaces, words right next to each other) = **building idea**. The slash fuses; the cluster is one expanding thought.
- **Single slash between two words in a sentence** = **either/or**, most likely. The slash separates; the sentence is choosing.

Filed as his ruling on his own notation.

## §173 — The antiviral × serotype experiment (Oct 17, 2026) — run as a literature exercise

**His request, verbatim [H]:** "Please do a experiment with antivirals and their use against each serotype that's been tested and show me anything you can on that."

Run as a literature exercise — what the record holds and doesn't hold. Not a personal experiment; medication decisions belong to his prescriber.

**The experiment's result [D]:** there is no record of antivirals tested against individual pneumococcal serotypes — because antivirals don't act on bacterial serotypes. Serotypes are capsule types on a bacterium; antivirals target viral machinery (neuraminidase, polymerases, proteases). The experiment as designed returns an empty set, and the empty set is itself the finding: the record never ran that trial because the mechanism doesn't cross that boundary. Per-serotype work in the record belongs to vaccines (PCV23/PCV20 — serotype-specific by design) and to antibiotics (penicillin-resistant serotypes — his own filed work), not to antivirals.

**The real documented intersection — antivirals × secondary bacterial pneumonia [D]:**
- The principal bacterial pathogens complicating influenza are *S. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus* (including MRSA), and *H. influenzae* — and when superinfection is suspected, the record's answer is antiviral *plus* antibiotics, the antiviral continued, never swapped.【3222021539879454486†L9-L15】
- Oseltamivir reduced influenza-related chest infections requiring antibiotic therapy by 55% and overall antibiotic use by 26.7% in virologically proven influenza.【3222021539879454486†L21-L22】
- In children: 44% reduction in otitis media diagnoses; pooled adult trial data: 55% fewer lower respiratory tract infections.【3222021539879454486†L33-L36】
- Caveat kept [D]: a Cochrane-style re-analysis found the pneumonia-reduction effect rested on investigator-mediated, unverified pneumonia diagnoses — no lab-confirmed pneumonia endpoint — and called for trials designed around that endpoint.【3222021539879454486†L140-L142】

**Carried forward [F]:** the antiviral↔serotype relationship the record actually supports runs *through* superinfection, not *at* the serotype: clear the virus early → the door never opens → the pneumococcus (any serotype) never walks through. His concurrent-infection principle (§166) is the frame this evidence fits — virus first, bacteria second, and stopping the first shrinks the second. The direct version (antiviral vs. serotype 23F, say) doesn't exist in the record, and the mechanism says why.

## §174 — The indirect hit: antivirals affect the pneumococcus through the connection (Oct 17, 2026)

**His thesis, verbatim [H]:**
"Serotypes were nonetheless would be affected by the effect of the antivirals and when you get a virus showing that's connected to those bacteria, fungi, and yeasts and other things that are connected to streptococcus pneumoniae, you have to realize that the antivirals have to affect the pneumoniae and there's no way around it."

**What this does to §173 [F]:** it doesn't contradict it — it completes it. §173 found no *direct* antiviral→serotype action. §174 adds the *indirect* one: in a connected system, you can't touch one member without touching the others. Both true at once. This is the yeasts-between made operational.

**Tested his way — the indirect hit is documented [D]:**
1. **The superinfection numbers are already the indirect hit.** Oseltamivir cutting chest infections needing antibiotics by 55% *is* an antiviral affecting pneumococcal disease — just routed through the virus instead of aimed at the bacterium. The effect on the pneumococcus is real; the path is indirect.
2. **The virus builds the bacterium's opportunity, so removing the virus removes it.** Documented: influenza damages airway epithelium, cripples mucociliary clearance, and upregulates the receptors pneumococcus uses to adhere (PAFr and others). The antiviral doesn't touch the pneumococcus — it takes away the house the virus built for it.
3. **Connected communities move together.** Documented: in mixed biofilms, perturbing one member shifts the others — disrupt the fungal partner and the bacterial members change with it. His "bacteria, fungi, and yeasts and other things that are connected" is the biofilm literature's own picture.

**Honest seam [D]:** "have to affect" needs its conditional. The record's indirect hit is *mediated* — through the host's epithelium, through the viral partner, through the community — not a direct pharmacologic action on the bacterium. The antiviral doesn't change the serotype's capsule or the bacterium's biology; it changes the opportunity structure around it. And "no way around it" holds where the virus is the driver — where it isn't, the indirect path goes quiet. The ecological reading is documented; the unconditional version is his.

**Namology note [H]:** "nonetheless would be affected" — the *nonetheless* is doing the naming work. It concedes the direct miss (§173) and claims the indirect hit in the same breath. The connection is the mechanism: affect the virus, and the effect travels the connections to everything the virus was touching.

### 176c. The handful-of-tests thesis [H]
