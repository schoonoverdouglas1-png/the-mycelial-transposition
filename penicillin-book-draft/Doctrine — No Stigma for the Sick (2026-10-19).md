# Doctrine — No Stigma for the Sick; We Are Born Sick; Healing Is Asked For (2026-10-19)

**Status:** his doctrine, filed as his. A humane one, received plainly.
**Frame:** *The Mycelial Transposition* — educational manuscript reference only. Never medical advice.

## His doctrine [H]

People shouldn't be stigmatized because of thoughts about drug use and personal behaviors — because our bodies are born sick, and the environments around us can also make us sick. And if we inadvertently, or on purpose by accident, make ourselves sick — that's the process of life, in which we ask for healing.

## The parts

1. **No stigma by suspicion.** Nobody's sickness should be judged through assumptions about drug use or how they live.
2. **Born sick.** The body arrives already carrying its burden — sickness isn't proof of wrongdoing.
3. **Environments sicken.** What's around us makes us sick too — not just what we do.
4. **Even the self-caused kind.** If we make ourselves sick — inadvertently, or "on purpose by accident" (his words) — that too is the process of life.
5. **Healing is asked for.** Not earned by innocence, not gated by blame — asked for, because that's what living involves.

## Beside it [D]

- Stigma as a barrier to care is documented: people avoid testing and treatment when they expect judgment — the literature on HIV, substance use, and sexually transmitted infections shows it plainly.
- Environmental causation of disease is documented throughout medicine.
- The born-sick half is his — his inherited-disease thesis, filed across his notes, standing as his.

## The combined statement

A doctrine of no-blame medicine: the sick are not on trial for how they got sick — not for suspected habits, not for being born carrying it, not for the air around them, not even for doing it to themselves. Sickness is the process of life, and healing is the thing asked for. Filed as his ethic, untouched.
