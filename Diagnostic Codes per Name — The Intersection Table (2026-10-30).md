**Frame:** creative manuscript reference only. Never medical advice. No diagnosis, no application to any person. Sequencing rules (principal vs. secondary, Excludes1/Excludes2, code-first notes) belong to professional coders — this table lays names over the code list; it is not coding guidance.

**Simply put:** First, every disease name gets its code, one row per name. Then the names are set against each other in a grid — where two names share a code, or run parallel by germ, by body site, or by worsening, the cell says so; where they don't, the cell says "no shared code" and nothing is forced. One germ, one code, no matter how many capsule varieties it wears.

2. **PM × Enc — shared code, G04.2.** The one literal code both names own: bacterial meningoencephalitis, the meninges–brain bridge.
3. **MM × Bact and MM × Sep — organism-parallel, A39.2–A39.4.** Meningococcemia is the single organism on both sides at once — his CMS list's "one organism, both sides."
4. **PP × FP — site-parallel, J17.- family.** Same lung, different kingdom: J17.0 bacterial side, J17.2 mycoses side. The family is the intersection; the specific codes (J13 vs B44/B37) stay apart.
5. **Sep × FP — showing-parallel, different kingdom.** Bacterial sepsis (A41.9) and fungal sepsis (B37.7) are the same showing in two kingdoms — parallel, not shared. The classification keeps kingdoms apart even when the showing matches.
6. **Bact × Sep — the honest non-intersection.** R78.81 carries an Excludes1 from A41: bacteremia without sepsis criteria and sepsis are coded apart by rule, not joined. A forced intersection here would be a coding error, not a discovery.

## Part III — Reading the pattern

The intersections prove his Oct 30 one-code-per-organism theory in code form [LOGIC from Parts I–II]:

- **Names multiply, codes collapse.** Part I holds 58 rows of names; the codes they point to collapse to organism + site. J13 does not care which of the 23 pneumococcal serotypes showed up; A39.0 does not care which meningococcal serogroup. The serotype and serogroup detail lives in the lab report and the vaccine list, never in the code.
- **Three parallel kinds, and only three, appear in the matrix.** Organism-parallel (same germ, different showing: PP × PM via A40.3; MM × Bact via A39.2–A39.4). Site-parallel (same site, different germ or kingdom: PP × FP in the lung; PM × MM/FM in the meninges). Severity-parallel (same worsening ladder: every severe name meets R65.20/R65.21 + organ-failure codes). Nothing else in the matrix is a parallel — everything else is either a literal shared code (G04.2; G03.9 at unspecified level) or no intersection at all.
- **The classification keeps kingdoms apart.** Bacterial sepsis and fungal sepsis run parallel (A41.9 vs B37.7) but never share a code; bacterial meningitis and fungal meningitis share a site but never a code. The parallels are real, and the separations are real too — both halves belong in the table.
- **His split survives contact with the codes, with its seam intact [H + D].** Bacteremia + pneumonia sit on his bacterial side and their codes (R78.81, J13, A40/A41) are bacterial codes. Meningitis sits on his fungal side of the split while G00.1 is a bacterial code — the seam stated in the Shared Diagnostic Codes table, carried here unchanged. The fungal side has codes of its own (B37.5, B38.4, B45.1, B37.7, B44, B37.1, B59); toxemia, the other fungal-side term, has none [GAP].
