## The organizing principle: capsule chemistry, not spelling

Douglas's Oct 30, 2026 commission: now that he has the meningitis vaccinations, use capsular polysaccharides and cross-reference that information on *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, in an expanded table showing the meningococcal groups combined with the meningococcal information available.

The principle that organizes both systems is the same [D]: **the capsular polysaccharide defines the type**. Meningococcal serogroups are distinguished by capsule chemistry [D — his Architecture essay, Chapter 1.2: serogrouping "relies on the structural composition of their capsular polysaccharides"], and pneumococcal serotypes are distinguished the same way — 19A vs 19F differ in capsular polysaccharide linkage chemistry [D — Serotype-First table]. That is why the lettering in both systems tracks biochemistry rather than spelling [H→D — the Architecture essay's seam, applied here]: change the capsule chemistry and you change the type, and you change which vaccine covers it. The Oct 28 cross-reference set the two systems side by side but left the meningococcal side thin — capsule chemistry as "capsular polysaccharide" in general, and every meningococcal-side grouping GAP. This file expands that side: the actual chemistries, the vaccine types they force, and the pneumococcal meningitis ten beside them.

**Simply put.** Both germs wear a sugar coat, and the coat is what names them. Meningococcal coats come in six disease-associated styles, A, B, C, W, X, and Y [D — MicrobeOnline, Nov 1, 2026]. Four of those coats can be turned into conjugate vaccines — the coat sugar tied to a protein so the immune system remembers it. The fifth coat, B, looks so much like a sugar found on our own nerve cells that it can't be used as a vaccine at all — so the B vaccine uses bacterial proteins instead. The sixth coat, X, is flagged here only: the MicrobeOnline page names it as disease-associated but gives no capsule chemistry and no vaccine detail for it, so neither is claimed in this copy. Pneumococcal coats come in 23 styles on his panels, and ten of those styles show up as meningitis. Same idea on both sides — coat decides name, coat decides vaccine — but no coat on one side equals any coat on the other. Different germs, different coats, one shared logic.

### Neisseria pair — biochemical split, added from MicrobeOnline [D]
- *N. meningitidis*: typically acid from **glucose and maltose**; capsule central to invasive disease, resisting phagocytosis and complement-mediated killing; colonizes the nasopharynx without illness; invasive disease = meningitis, bloodstream infection, or both.
- *N. gonorrhoeae*: typically acid from **glucose alone, not maltose**; no conventional polysaccharide capsule. Included here only as the biochemical pair to *N. meningitidis* — it is not a meningococcal serogroup and is not rowed in Part I.
- Memory aid on the MicrobeOnline page: "M for meningococcus, M for maltose."

## Part III — Vaccine-mechanism explainer: three technologies, one capsule logic

Why the capsule decides the vaccine technology — and why B breaks the pattern. Every claim tier-labeled.

**1. Plain polysaccharide — PPSV23 [D].** Purified capsular polysaccharides, no protein attached. The immune response is **T-cell-independent**: B cells are activated directly by the long repeating sugar chains, without T-helper cells, producing mainly IgM and then short-lived plasma cells [D]. Two consequences [D]: **no immunologic memory** — antibody levels fade, and repeat doses add limited benefit; and **poor response in young children** — infants and children under two cannot mount a reliable T-cell-independent response, so PPSV23 is not effective in that age group. PPSV23 covers 23 serotypes — the broadest list — but with the weakest memory.

**2. Conjugate — PCV13/15/20/21 and MenACWY [D].** The same capsular polysaccharide, chemically linked (conjugated) to a carrier protein (typically CRM197, a non-toxic diphtheria-toxin variant, or a tetanus/diphtheria toxoid). Conjugation converts the antigen from T-cell-independent to **T-cell-dependent**: the B cell internalizes the complex, presents carrier-protein fragments to T-helper cells, and T-cell help drives class switching to high-affinity IgG, affinity maturation, and **memory B cells** [D]. That is why conjugates work in infants and give durable, boostable protection [D]. This is the technology behind four of the six meningococcal serogroups (A, C, W, Y); serogroup B uses protein, and serogroup X is flagged with no technology claimed and all the pneumococcal conjugates — the same solution on both sides, because the problem (a sugar coat the young immune system ignores) is the same.

**3. Protein — MenB [D].** No capsule at all. When the capsule itself is unusable as an antigen — serogroup B's α2→8 polysialic acid is chemically identical to human NCAM, so it is recognized as self and poorly immunogenic, and using it would raise autoimmune concern [D] — the vaccine switches targets entirely: recombinant surface proteins identified by reverse vaccinology (fHbp, NadA, NHBA, PorA) [D]. MenB is therefore not a "fifth conjugate" but a different technology forced by the capsule chemistry [LOGIC — steps: conjugate technology requires a usable capsular polysaccharide; B's is unusable as self; therefore the only route is non-capsular protein antigens].

**The pattern, stated once [LOGIC]:** capsule chemistry → vaccine technology, in both organisms. Usable capsule → conjugate (or plain polysaccharide, memory sacrificed for breadth). Unusable capsule → protein. Unknown capsule (X, on this source) → no technology claimed. The B exception is not a gap in the pattern; it is the pattern's proof — the chemistry forces the technology, and when the chemistry refuses, the technology changes rather than the chemistry being ignored.

## Part IV — Boundary, Flags, GAPs, Provenance

### Boundary — stated plainly

**NO meningococcal serogroup maps to any pneumococcal serotype.** Different organisms, different capsules, different chemistries. This cross-reference is structural — both systems classify by capsule, both show as meningitis, both let capsule chemistry predict vaccine coverage — never identity. A table pairing "B = 19A" or any like pairing would be inventing a mapping. The cross-reference stops at showing and capsule logic, and it stops there [D + LOGIC].

## Part V — Appendix: MicrobeOnline context NOT merged into the rows

*Added Nov 1, 2026, from the MicrobeOnline cross-check file. This appendix is context only; it does not add rows to Parts I–II.*

- **Moraxella placement [D]:** *Moraxella catarrhalis* is a **Gram-negative diplococcus**, not a coccobacillus, and can resemble *Neisseria* on Gram stain. Distinguishers on the page: oxidase positive, DNase positive, butyrate esterase positive; "hockey-puck" colonies; most clinical isolates beta-lactamase positive. Diseases on the page: otitis media, sinusitis in children; acute COPD exacerbations in adults. It is listed here because a reader could mistake a diplococcus for *Neisseria* — it is NOT a meningococcal serogroup and NOT a pneumococcal serotype, and it maps to neither.
- **Specimen-context rule [D, from the same page]:** same shape, different meaning by specimen — cerebrospinal-fluid Gram-negative diplococci urgently suggest meningococcal disease, while a respiratory specimen may be colonizing flora. A negative smear does not exclude infection. This supports, but does not change, the "showing" language in Parts I–II.
- **Boundary, restated for X:** no meningococcal serogroup — including X — maps to any pneumococcal serotype. X's addition changes the count (five → six disease-associated serogroups), not the boundary.
