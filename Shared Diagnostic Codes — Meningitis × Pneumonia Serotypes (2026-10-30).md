### The B95.3 correction (stated, not buried)

The brief proposed B95.3 as "the organism code used alongside both G00.1 and J13." Checked against the coding guidelines, that usage does **not** hold as routine coding [D]:
- B95.3 exists and is billable, never a principal diagnosis [D].
- But B95–B97 organism codes are for infections whose code does **not** itself name the organism: "Certain infections are classified in chapters other than Chapter 1 and no organism is identified as part of the infection code. In these instances, it is necessary to use an additional code from Chapter 1 to identify the organism" [D — ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines, Chapter 1].
- J13 "identifies both the infection and the causative organism in one code rather than two codes" [D — coding reference], so B95.3 is not added alongside J13.
- G00.1 likewise already names pneumococcus in its own title ("pneumococcal meningitis") [D]. The "use additional code to further identify organism (B95.0–B95.5)" note belongs to G00.2 (streptococcal meningitis, organism unspecified), not to G00.1 [D — coding reference].
- B95.3 belongs alongside infection codes that do **not** name the organism (for example, an unspecified bacterial pneumonia code, or a manifestation code carrying a "use additional code" note) [LOGIC from the guideline above].
- So: B95.3 stays in this table as a verified organism code, but its row is corrected — it is the organism bridge for unnamed-organism codes, not an add-on to G00.1 and J13. The brief's proposed usage is not followed, and the correction is filed here rather than silently adopted.

## Part IV — THE LOAD-BEARING HONEST POINT

**ICD-10-CM codes the DISEASE and the ORGANISM, never the serotype.** No code distinguishes serotype 14 from serotype 19A, or 23F from 8. G00.1 says "pneumococcal meningitis" — it does not say which capsule chemistry. J13 says "pneumonia due to *Streptococcus pneumoniae*" — it does not say which serotype. The serotype-level map in Parts I and II is his grouping laid over organism-level codes; the codes themselves do not carry serotype identity [D — the code titles themselves; LOGIC — the map is his overlay, the codes are the classification's].

That means: the intersection of six serotypes is a fact of **his grouping** [H→D — the groups are his, verified against the Corrected Copy], and the shared codes G00.1 + J13 are facts of **the classification** [D]. The two facts meet in this table but come from different places, and neither one proves the other. A coder reading G00.1 + J13 on a chart learns organism and showing; the serotype, if known, lives in the lab report, not in the code.

**The seam in his split, stated honestly, neither rewritten:** in his bacterial/fungal element split, bacteremia + pneumonia = the bacterial element and toxemia + meningitis = the fungal element [H]. But G00.1 is a **bacterial** meningitis code, sitting in the bacterial meningitis block G00.0–G00.9 [D]. So meningitis sits on his fungal side of the split while its pneumococcal code sits on the classification's bacterial side. Both framings are carried: his split is his doctrine about elements; the code block is the classification's division by organism kingdom. The tension is the seam, not an error to be smoothed over — and the fungal-side neighbors in his own CMS list (B37.5 candidal meningitis, B38.4 coccidioidal meningitis, B45.1 cerebral cryptococcosis [D]) show where fungal meningitis does have codes of its own. Toxemia, the other fungal-side term, has no code at all [GAP — see Part V].
